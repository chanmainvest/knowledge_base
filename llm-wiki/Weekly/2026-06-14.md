## The digest

Oil didn't just dominate this week's discourse — it whipsawed it. The week opened Sunday with Eurodollar University's [Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E), arguing the real squeeze lay ahead — global crude inventories at their lowest since 2003 — with [Jeff (Jeoff)](../People/jeff-jeoff.md) sketching how "that's how you get to $50 a barrel." Four days later the same channel ran [an alert that oil *and* interest rates were plunging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpZ_HYxaU_8), with [Jeff Snider](../People/jeff-snider.md) telling viewers to start from the Treasury curve and watch for WTI contango, while [Joseph Wang](../People/joseph-wang.md) closed the week noting spot Brent had "absolutely plummeted" ([Markets Weekly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80)). [Patrick Ceresna](../People/patrick-ceresna.md) supplied the peace-dividend read: equities "rallied on the deescalation with the S&P 500 climbing over 2% to 7420" ([Trade of The Week](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oqHNNzR9WNc)). The second macro driver was a genuinely hawkish June FOMC — Wang's [debrief](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Njd_JuRuEcI) marveled that the chair "didn't have to actually hike… he did tighten financial conditions and raise rates just by his communication" — and [何啟聰](../People/person-002.md) argued AI hardware inflation, with Apple raising prices to pass on chip and memory costs, is what has [backed the Fed into a corner](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4435475/AI%E7%A1%AC%E4%BB%B6%E6%BC%B2%E5%83%B9%E9%80%BC%E8%81%AF%E5%84%B2%E5%B1%80%E5%85%A5%E6%AD%BB%E8%A7%92) with inflation running far above the 2% target.

SpaceX was the week's spectacle, and the splits were sharp. The IPO popped roughly 19.2% on debut, drawing [克魯明 (Paul Krugman)](../People/paul-krugman.md)'s "human Ponzi scheme" broadside against Musk ventures while [Grok (xAI)](../People/grok-xai.md) framed it as a high-volatility growth bet trading near 100x sales, depending on Starlink and Starship execution ([徐家健's column](https://www.master-insight.com/article/48758)). On the trading side, Wang warned that "in the coming months, you'll have lockups unlock, and then you'll have the people sell" ([FOMC debrief](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Njd_JuRuEcI)), while Macro Dirt's [JD](../People/jd.md) could "see a scenario where SpaceX does the middle finger chart formation, goes parabolic" and "could double from here" ([Macro Dirt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-mvJ6poUk4)). Gold and silver (15 calls on GC=F) split most instructively *within* one shop: [Jeffrey Christian](../People/jeffrey-christian.md) of CPM Group sees volatile sideways consolidation above $4,000 into September, platinum stuck in a $1,600–2,000 downward bend, and a silver spike below $60 "very high" over the next two and a half months ([June 16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W1jdQOS8Lu8), [June 19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z1btQrMgRwo)) — yet his colleague [Jeffrey Kushin](../People/jeffrey-kushin.md) expects a $60–90 consolidation "and then higher prices in the final four months." [Brent Johnson](../People/brent-johnson.md) sits outside both camps, still expecting gold "to rally spectacularly after the Iran oil dislocation" toward a ~4,400 bounce ([MacroVoices #537](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)).

On equities and AI, the camps line up cleanly. [George Noble](../People/george-noble.md) on [George Gammon's](../People/george-gammon.md) show: "there is absolutely no way that I would go outright long the S&P 500 right now," and he dislikes homebuilders like LEN ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QxR6yvf8ueU)); Gammon's own hedge is a $10,000 long SPY position. [Jim Chanos](../People/jim-chanos.md) and [Val Zlatev](../People/val-zlatev.md) built the most substantive bear case: an accounting mismatch where chip suppliers recognize revenue immediately while hyperscalers capitalize costs for years, leaving Chanos "a bear" on Meta whose "AI strategy makes no sense," deeply skeptical of CoreWeave-style neo-clouds, and long Teradyne/Marvell calls — though notably even they demurred that "being outright short semiconductors right now might not be a risk worth taking" ([MacroMinds panel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NlIsoPhQePs)). [David Woo](../People/david-woo.md) is the week's most internally consistent bear-bull hybrid: long oil with a $150 target if the strait stays shut through July, bearish equities and gold, painfully short the NASDAQ, all wrapped in an AI-crackdown thesis about "token maxing" inflating profits ([Monetary Matters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=760Bpzc1cYM)). Against the AI bears, [Chris Semenuk](../People/chris-semenuk.md) argues the three-year US manufacturing recession is over, favoring "boring" short-cycle industrials ([Monetary Matters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aS6W2GJ2AzA)), and [Patrick Boyle](../People/patrick-boyle.md) adds a darker wrinkle: Anthropic's export-control self-immolation shows what happens "when your target market shrinks from the entire global economy to people who have passed a background check" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0RxMj0L0-fY)). Meanwhile [aminvest](../People/aminvest.md) is the cheerful-but-timing-wary voice, buying financials (long Morgan Stanley options, Goldman "too pricey") while warning the bull "may only have a few months to a year left" ([June 15](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-sheng-shi-161170184), [June 17](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-161328901)).

The China-credit thread ran bearish all week through Snider: a record collapse in household borrowing that's "much, much harder to just explain away" ([June 16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d7cNjW_mcEM)), then the Hang Seng "acting like a market that's tied to deteriorating credit" — "China's credit machine is lost" ([June 20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3j8NhL0FTo4)) — alongside continued private-credit redemptions with BlackRock cited ([June 17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WH-0Pl9ydM)). 胡孟青 added the policy angle: synchronized tightening across the US, Europe, Japan, Australia and Indonesia while China alone can cut, with a mass yen-carry unwind as the variable to watch ([column](https://www.master-insight.com/article/48870)).

The mind-changers deserve note because they moved fast. Well…" ([MacroVoices #537](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)) — and Johnson pulled off a triple flip: bearish-to-bullish on the dollar ("99% of all outstanding stablecoins in the world today are US dollar stable"), bearish-to-bullish on geopolitics ("National defense is… probably going to be the biggest investment opportunity any of us have ever seen"), and bullish-to-bearish on oil. In Hong Kong, [高天佑](../People/person-015.md) pivoted from June 16 bullishness on the "Hefei model" — ChangXin Memory clearing RMB 33 billion in Q1 profit as one of four HBM makers ([column](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/finnews/article/4431990/%E5%8C%97%E9%83%BD%E5%8F%AF%E5%80%9F%E9%91%91%E3%80%8C%E5%90%88%E8%82%A5%E6%A8%A1%E5%BC%8F%E3%80%8D)) — to June 18 bearishness as mainland e-commerce's 618 invasion squeezes local retailers, jobs, rents and property valuations ([column](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/finnews/article/4434076/618%E6%94%BB%E6%B8%AF+%E6%9C%AC%E5%9C%B0%E9%9B%B6%E5%94%AE%E9%9B%A3%E6%8D%B1)); Snider himself carried a bullish dollar view across the Sunday boundary only to declare by June 15 that "a rising dollar is not a victory signal.
