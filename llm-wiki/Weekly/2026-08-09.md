## The digest

Gold wasn't just the most-discussed asset this week — with 42 calls it out-drew the S&P 500's 26 by half again — and the discourse around it split cleanly into breakout euphoria and exhaustion suspicion. Chris Vermeulen managed to hold both positions at once, warning that the move looks like "short covering" and "FOMO buying" that "could easily sell" while simultaneously allowing that gold can rally to about $4,500 an ounce [VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jvVzlUayDlc). The bulls were more committed: Frank Giustra and Ian Harris declared the supercycle "not coming, it's here" [David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ua2V6X1ZqdE), one David Lin panel had the debasement trade "exploding" with a $9,000 gold target [David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JoEdBLw_JM), Jim Rickards built his $10K case on Iran-war uncertainty — the straits still closed, US midterms two months out — calling it "one more thing that favors gold" [The Julia La Roche Show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9uX8_jha68), and Mark Newton told Jimmy Connor he sees "a very strong 2027 for gold and silver" [Jimmy Connor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bkL0lcYOTMA). The macro backdrop feeding all this: headline CPI cooled to 3.4% with core at 2.5%, a 2021-era low, per AM Invest, who framed the real question as when hikes start and how big they'll be [AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-ji-xu-kan-166536089).

Rates and bonds were the week's largest view theme, and the Fed — now under Warsh — got it from every direction. Daniel Lacalle argued the Fed is simply wrong about rates [Real Vision](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U2WwLW-m-Qc); Christopher Whalen went further, saying the Fed "has lost control," that the dollar is "unusually strong" given the fiscal situation, and that inflation has always been America's mechanism for avoiding repaying its debt [VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k802efbalbg) — before returning later in the week to call private credit's first big unwind, with insurance next [The Julia La Roche Show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=400m6gYSRCE). Darius Dale's take was the Fed is trapped: deficits argue for tightening, but "on net they're essentially saying the Fed should remain on hold," long credit and stocks, not bonds [Forward Guidance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvMTOKRR0SE). On Warsh himself, Dave Collum cast him as Volcker 2.0 sent through the "valley of death" [VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ku8l-I6IU1A), while Mark Thornton called the appointment a "hit job" on gold and thinks Warsh "may prove less hawkish than expected" [VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=raWvZvwCtJs). Meanwhile Milton Berg, after "the worst panic in 4 years," was VERY bullish on stocks and fine with 10- and 30-year yields climbing toward 6-8% as normal fluctuation [The Monetary Matters Network](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Idj7jAOtE50), and the bond market itself got a strange 30-year auction [Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkyS0rLy1Uo) plus Patrick Boyle's warning that Japanese selling pushing yields higher would flow straight into American mortgage rates [Patrick Boyle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yh18YXKMk3g).

The sharpest divergences were in AI and the yen. On AI, Collum's layered-bubble thesis — "an AI bubble on top of a real estate bubble on top of a general equity bubble" that will "break down VERY quickly" [VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ku8l-I6IU1A) — and Norway's wealth fund preparing for an AI crash, on the logic that "passive confidence is not a risk management strategy" [Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=votJ0sjvFmI), ran straight into Raoul Pal and Blue Macellari's counter: Anthropic is "the largest, fastest scaling of revenues in any company in human history," with intelligence-output efficiency doubling every five months [Raoul Pal The Journey Man](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q2TAHZIBpdE). Thomas Hayes took the middle path — not wipeout but rotation, into Disney and even Diageo, because "people are drinking less, they're drinking better" [David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8kFLx1lEzU0); George Gammon, watching BlackRock and Nvidia together, just found it "dangerous" [George Gammon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gb5hBqdmnUI). The yen debate was nearly unanimous in its pessimism: after joint US-Japan intervention moved the pair from 161 to ~156 before drifting back above 158 [何啟聰](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4479275/%E7%95%99%E7%B5%A6%E4%B8%8B%E4%B8%80%E4%BB%A3+%E8%B2%B7%E7%BE%8E%E5%82%B5%E9%82%84%E6%98%AFSpaceX+%3F), Eurodollar University observed that short sellers "retreated, but that didn't change anything, which is why the yen went right back down," with a 160 breach forcing three obvious choices and a rushed BOJ hike risking JGB destabilization [Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QNDwry7_6C4); Boyle's verdict — intervention "buys you a few days, a week maybe" — and Jay Martin tied it to the S&P's worst day in two years off "one small tap in Tokyo" [The Jay Martin Show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_i-MswQoweE).

On stance changes, the evidence is subtler than outright flips but real: the veteran trader on David Lin's show said "Now I'm getting back into gold," an implicit admission he'd been out during the run [David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kmOsTT2grwk), and Figuring Out Money flagged he'll "probably get back into my crypto position" on strength [Figuring Out Money](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UFACPcXvCqc) — a notably brave re-entry given Michael Howell, the week's most ubiquitous voice across three appearances, put crypto "under a cloud" as liquidity turns and positioned structurally dollar-bull and defensively on financial assets [MacroVoices](https://www.macrovoices.com/1545-macrovoices-545-michael-howell-warsh-vs-the-markets), [The Julia La Roche Show](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FPB4Z1KhE3s), though even Howell split himself: structurally bullish gold, tactically near a correction inflection [Macro Voices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c06eQWQljXo). The metals camp's own internal fight — Rick Rule arguing copper has "WAY more potential than gold," that there isn't "enough euphoria in the gold stocks yet," and that near-term rising rates are "hard on the precious metals" [VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mfhDfeJuY6Y), against Ian Harris's smart-money-copper-and-Colombia thesis [VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ql9wm7AQs8) — rounded out a week where, honestly, almost nobody planted a flag: 281 views tagged and most hedged, the discourse less about calling the top than about positioning for whichever way the turn comes.
