## The digest

The Federal Reserve owned this week. Ten of the digest's 129 extracted calls landed on the 10-year yield (^TNX), second only to the S&P 500's eleven, and the interview circuit circled the July FOMC and Kevin Warsh's second meeting almost obsessively. The most concrete read came from Joseph Wang's [July 2026 FOMC debrief](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l5IGlZ8TXZk): the long bond's yield "explode[d] higher during the press conference" as the market grew concerned, even while the front end and belly rallied on the Fed's refusal to hike. Around that anchor sat a remarkably skeptical chorus — Komal Sri-Kumar on [Fed-triggered "carnage"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2jxIHKDzMw0), Eurodollar University on a [shocking Fed statement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lb7AFr5adWw), Kathryn Rooney Vera on the ["Mum's The Word" second Warsh meeting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fMNSCF1bW0w), Danielle DiMartino Booth ["Nobody's Happy, Cracks Are Showing"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GNIFA-Mm7fI), Jim Bianco [asking whether the Fed or the market solves inflation](https://www.macrovoices.com/1543-macrovoices-543-jim-bianco-who-solves-inflation-the-fed-or-the-market), Forward Guidance on [Warsh's long-end gamble](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PW2tgckfEtA), and Chris Whalen on [Warsh's credibility problem](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgRvK6Ug-SA) — with Dr Ng Ming Tak adding the political layer of the US president [loudly demanding rate cuts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=APg1De0Fe-s). When that many independent voices converge on "the Fed has a long-end problem," that's the week's story.

The second live wire was AI credit. Eurodollar University's [alert on AI credit spreads blowing out "just like 2008"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgZ2j6yI16A) came with evidence rather than vibes: a blue-chip data-center deal priced at a 7.53% yield — among the highest for that financing type — Amazon paper drawing noticeably unenthusiastic demand, and Google telling investors spending will "rise significantly again in 2027." Its companion piece argued [private credit defaults are exploding into public markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8XK1DP46RWk), AM Invest wrote up the [AI arms race](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/ai-ju-qi-jun-bei-165157899) and separately suggested it was [time to watch SQQQ again](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/shi-shi-hou-zai-165282941). The counterweight came at the week's open from the Monetary Matters Network, arguing the [next crisis is unlikely to start in private markets because fundamentals are solid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wM0_rexOj_w). Behind both sits the consumer: Eurodollar University heard the ["safest" companies warning the consumer is breaking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I475w6xb8LI) — another layoff wave coming, rates down "quite a bit," oil substantially lower — while Joanne Hsu discussed [worst-ever consumer sentiment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_FUXW-09Yg).

The sharpest split was deflation versus debasement. A [David Lin guest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_wm2gO04z_I) laid out the full deflation trade — gold to $3,000, oil to $40, the S&P down 10–20%, and Bitcoin where he'll "still make the call that I don't think Bitcoin's going to bottom till around $10,000." Lined up against him: Doug Casey, for whom the dollar is [being "inflated out of existence"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ca9zCylJcEA) and war in that region "a permanent feature," with an "excellent chance" the Fed is forced into yield curve control; Luke Gromen titling flatly that [YCC is the only way to stop a global bond crisis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lS67XZP81Tc); and Tarek Saab calling for ["gold's going to 10,000"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z__R2asKZVk) with silver at 200 — with John Butler's oil-bullish take in the same [David Lin slot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SjxmpuoQvVc) a week earlier in spirit. CPM Group split the difference twice in four days — [breakout or breakdown before August](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y44xymVXMOg), then an [August drop before the next rally](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EajzJMTVieM) — and Dr Ng cautioned retail that paper gold is a ["賭具" whose biggest risk is counterparty, not price](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5TlZuLlikYo). On equities, Real Vision's Caleb Franzen conversation argued the [bull market still has room to run](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hzD3kSL_haE), buying dips even in Bitcoin, while Figuring Out Money admitted [wanting the market to "bleed a little bit more"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6_4qbQS8XXM) before titling its August 1 video ["Nobody Knows Which Way To Feel"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ft2ftremg_4), with the monthly SPX implied move priced at 720 down / 774 up.

On mind-changers: honestly thin. Every call in this extraction arrived tagged neutral with no person-level stance history, so no verifiable reversals can be claimed. The closest is Peter Grandich [selling all his stocks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20QLicjAfbo) and rotating elsewhere — a de-risking pivot by any reading, but the digest offers no prior-week baseline to certify it as a flip. The rest was drift, not conversion. Meanwhile the periphery produced the rawest event: Dr Ng covering [Korea's one-day 11% crash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iqTyfm2BIzo) and leveraged ETFs becoming "automatic selling machines," echoed by 李聲揚's column on [Korean retail investors and a government caught flat-footed](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4469701/%E5%8D%97%E9%9F%93%E6%95%A3%E6%88%B6%E8%BC%B8%E6%89%93%E8%B4%8F%E8%A6%81+%E6%94%BF%E5%BA%9C%E9%80%B2%E9%80%80%E5%A4%B1%E6%93%9A), plus his two-parter on [the yen breaking a 40-year low](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q_CWAqllGas). The shape of the debate is telling: aside from the Monetary Matters bull case on private markets, almost nobody argued "all clear" — the argument is over sequencing, whether the bond market or AI credit breaks first, and whether the deflation trade or the debasement trade pays.
