## The digest

Gold owned the week. Out of 86 items and 46 voices, GC=F drew 46 calls — comfortably the most-discussed instrument, ahead of Bitcoin (28) and GoGold (26) — and the target ladder kept ratcheting higher. Nomi Prins made the case for $6,000 gold on a physical market "very close to a deficit," citing a Morgan Stanley estimate of a 600,000-ton copper deficit this year while admitting she "wouldn't necessarily buy copper" even expecting higher prices (). Don Durrett floated $8,000 gold and $200 silver, favoring producers over early-stage explorers at this phase of the bull market (), Ted Oakley called gold still cheap at $4,600 (), Darius Dale is watching for a return to the April highs near $4,800 (), and CPM Group pinned the metal in a $3,800–4,300 band (). The miners rode the same tide: GoGold management pitched the Los Ricos North permits as a "more than doubling" catalyst in a "tremendous bull market" (), and Agnico's $57 million investment in the RDS.V Quebec project gave the juniors their headline (). The other center of gravity was fiscal — Treasury's $1 trillion buyback/warchest — which Danny Moses framed as outright crisis ("$2 trillion deficit, debt over 40 trillion. We need to keep interest costs low and we'll do anything") (), Todd Horwitz gamed the cross-asset fallout (), and Dr Ng asked plainly whether the long-bond buyback is QE in disguise (). Rates-and-bonds was the biggest view theme at 51 entries.

The sharpest fault line ran between the "it's already breaking" camp and the "the market says otherwise" camp. Michael Oliver's trigger is a bond and financial-sector crisis that sends gold soaring (). Against that, Eurodollar University audited the Ray Dalio debt warning and found markets unbothered — TIPS and inflation swaps "not signaling an uncontrolled monetary response," term-premium estimates contained, and "if any of that changes, we will know about it" (). The Macro Dirt podcast built an entire episode on the calm: hiking angers Trump, cutting steepens the curve, but for now bond volatility sits "in the gutter" (), which Forward Guidance echoed alongside oil vol near its year-to-date lows (). Daniel Lacalle split the difference — Trump won't annihilate the system and a BRICS currency is "going to the past," though every fiat currency is "worsening relative to gold" (). Inside that, a genuine hawk-dove collision: Real Vision flagged three same-direction dissents for a rate hike at the last FOMC and a policymaker's "I stand ready to raise rates" as a possible fourth (), while Eurodollar University's consumer-side reading — employment down over 900,000, full-time jobs falling — concluded the long end faces "the exact opposite" of an inflation shock (). Collin Martin split the baby: the dire scenario is a risk, but "not in the here and now," with political pressure on the Fed very real ().

The AI-bubble argument ran on parallel tracks. Dr Ng mined the dot-com analogy — Cisco, the world's priciest company in 1999, fell 90% and only reclaimed that high last year; Amazon fell just as hard before 40x-ing — while conceding in the same video that big-tech earnings and cash flows remain strong (). 胡孟青 tallied the excess: HTX research says diffusion is early but capex and valuations are ahead, the five giants' 2026 AI capex is pegged near US$75.5 billion (up ~80% year-on-year), Goldman sees ~US$5.3 trillion of big-tech capex through 2030, and BofAML has called it a "super bubble" (). Warren Pies expects software to take the first pain in the IPO cycle and likes "energy as diversifier" (), Macro Dirt shrugged that Nvidia earnings would be a "non-event" (), and Forward Guidance doubted Anthropic's moat is durable (). Bitcoin split the same way: Jason Yanowitz rode the debasement trade toward $200,000, with Reuters tying the move to a softer dollar (), and Todd Horwitz saw another $10,000 before a pullback (), but Ben Cowen warned of "at least one more scare in Q4" and noted a true alt season would require a parabolic Bitcoin (). On tariffs, Matt Gertken argued the 50% move only unites Canadians behind Mark Carney, who won't touch critical minerals or fuel (), while The Plain Bagel documented the deal falling apart, with Canada delaying retaliation to September 8 amid sluggish GDP ().

Mind-changes were thin — worth saying outright. The clearest update came from CPM Group, whose standing view had gold range-bound through August and who now warn of "another wave of investor buying," their 51-year veteran calling current volumes the biggest he has ever seen (). Jeff Christian is a trim, not a flip: still bullish but taking profits off to reposition, on inflation staying above 3% and possibly pushing 4–5% (). Cowen spent part of his slot re-litigating the "four-year cycle is dead" argument the market already disproved (). AM Invest showed patience rather than reversal — Alibaba's earnings improved but sell pressure remains heavy, so buy back only on a resumed uptrend or a panic drop (), and by Thursday they noted positions nearing record highs and advised against attacking too early (). Beyond that, the record skews heavily neutral and hedged: the "Big Short" guest on David Lin's show offered "literally no idea" on both Bitcoin and gold (), and the Resource Talks crew repeated its mantra — "nobody out there has any special predicting abilities" — across multiple mining interviews ().

The shape of the debate, then, is a broad consensus that something in the fiscal-rates complex eventually breaks, set against positioning and volatility signals saying nothing is breaking yet — which is why gold targets ratchet while bond vol sits in the gutter. The skeptics' counterweight came largely from the Hong Kong side: in a recap on the $40-trillion debt question, Dr Ng argued the US won't collapse within a decade and the dollar won't fall from 99 toward 85 under Trump (); he read Hong Kong's rising deposits despite emigration as the city remaining a "savings pool" () and saw the renminbi's fastest appreciation leg done, settling into 6.6–6.98 (). 施永青 made the boldest structural call of the week — that the mainland should halt land sales entirely, since cheap auctions drag down home prices and household balance sheets alike (). A week that talked constantly about breakage while, in positioning terms, betting on none.
