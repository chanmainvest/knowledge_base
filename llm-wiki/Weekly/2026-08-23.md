## The digest

If you count calls, gold owned the week — 40 on GC=F, ahead of bitcoin (25) and silver (14) — but the narrative gravity was unmistakably fiscal. The US Treasury's trillion-dollar move dominated everything: David Lin ran two episodes on it, one warning ["We're in a financial crisis" as Treasury readies a $1 trillion warchest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EfKxbRuLZG0) — where the guest called it "seat belt time" and saw no way out for Japan — and another with [Todd Horwitz on the "$1 Trillion Rescue"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szfb2vqhLjo). Damped Spring [published a primer, TGA 101](https://dampedspring101.substack.com/p/tga-101), arguing the impact depends entirely on deployment — paying off bills is the smallest jolt, buying back 30-year bonds much larger — while Raoul Pal and Andreas Steno billed it as [a move that "could change everything"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i1CY09CypqU), and Hong Kong's Dr Ng Ming Tak devoted an episode to [whether the long-bond buyback is QE in disguise](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-PhcC7PbQvo). The Fed side of the story ran through [Real Vision's Jim Bianco and Andreas Steno](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lFpQuOEzGzc), who flagged three dissents in the same hawkish direction — the most at any meeting in this cycle — with a possible fourth, and Bianco arguing it's time to retire forward guidance entirely, noting Warsh still "owes us" a reaction function.

The gold camp agreed on direction and spent the week brawling over magnitude. [Michael Oliver's thesis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6TP13fhGqU) is that a bond crisis — potentially triggered by a non-regional-bank headline — sends gold soaring; [Nomi Prins pitched $6,000 gold and a silver buying opportunity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0), citing a Morgan Stanley estimate of a 600,000-ton copper deficit this year while conceding she "wouldn't necessarily buy copper" anyway; [Don Durrett went to $8,000 gold and $200 silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GpS3exTGU5o) on a "doom loop" of debt, sticky yields and Treasury intervention; [Jeff Christian floated $5,000 "or collapse first"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bgdCd8hJFw) with inflation persistently above 3%, maybe 4–5%; [Darius Dale watched for a return to April highs near 4,800](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UAGEIxhyL8c); and [Ted Oakley called gold still cheap at $4,600](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V5LzrcmhffA). [Jim Wiederhold tied it together](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9PbTMpA8KD4) — gold, silver, copper and oil all rallying at once, with $150–200 oil forecasts now circulating — and the mania reached small caps: GoGold drew [26 calls centered on the Los Ricos North permit and an 18-month timeline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJYFy1WEDSE), Brazilian Rare Earths faced [the funding question atop the highest-grade hard rock rare earth system on the public market](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iSYYQqlo7qI), and RDS.V drew scrutiny over [Agnico's $57 million ticket and a budget running through mid-2027](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bY40DOJPvxk).

The sharpest fault line was debt-crisis alarm versus market evidence. [Ray Dalio's newest warning that the US is approaching — or in — a debt crisis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oshjZjc5FJI) got an unusually honest fact-check from the same channel: TIPS and inflation swaps are "not signaling an uncontrolled monetary response," term premium estimates have stayed contained, and "if any of that changes, we will know about it." George Gammon ran the pure fear version — [the entire house of cards about to fall, Treasury terrified](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mXi5jB-kPJE) — while [Forward Guidance noted the opposite symptom](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEG3hZZX45M): bond vol is "in the gutter" and oil vol near its lows, the big risk factors mechanically removed. Bitcoin split cleanly: [Horwitz sees another $10,000 of rally before any pullback](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=szfb2vqhLjo), while [Ben Cowen expects "at least one more scare in Q4"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTn7-oSb_ic), noting bitcoin is still "basically 80K" and a real alt season would require a parabolic move first. On AI, the bubble camp had the heavier artillery — [胡孟青 citing BofA's "super bubble" label, Goldman's $5.3 trillion 2025–2030 capex estimate, and HTX's point that diffusion is early while valuation isn't](https://www.master-insight.com/article/49793), plus [skeptics on Forward Guidance doubting Anthropic's moat is durable](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aEG3hZZX45M) — against [Dr Ng's "real income and real bubble" framing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iqiTITzO8Ao), built on the reminder that Amazon fell 90% before going up 40x and Cisco needed until only last year to reclaim its 1999 high. Private credit stayed on the grill too, with [the Mark Walter affair potentially exposing billions in losses — a Guggenheim-linked billion-dollar loan trading around 73 cents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7K5dWPaUt4).

As for conversions: the week produced recalibration, not reversal — I found no clean about-face in the digest. The closest is [Jeff Christian](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bgdCd8hJFw), still bullish gold and silver but taking profits off the table to reposition, having watched investors trim for several weeks — hedging within the consensus rather than leaving it. Prins' copper stance has the same shape: higher prices forecast, no purchase. And Ben Cowen's aside that "the argument back then was that the four-year cycle was dead — but it wasn't" is a victory lap over other people's calls, not a change of his own.
