## The digest

The S&P 500 was the week's centre of gravity — sixteen separate calls on ^GSPC, with gold (10), oil (7) and the Hang Seng (6) behind it, and XLE, TSLA, NQ=F and ORCL at four apiece — and the argument was crash versus correction. On David Lin's show, Todd Horwitz stuck to his guns: "I'm still looking for an overall 40 to 60% haircut… there's more coming" ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3lcf3jksM-4)). Joseph Wang took the calmer side on his Markets Weekly: this "could just be a correction, maybe down to the 200-day moving average around 7,000," and he rejected bond-doom outright — "there's never going to be an implosion of the bond market. The bond market is controlled by the government" ([Joseph Wang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9MGlq_4xxjE)). Between them sat Figuring Out Money, which spent four uploads describing a market that is "very path dependent" — gamma-neutral, the S&P MACD "starting to curl over," the 10-year yield up "pretty tremendously," and an open refusal to turn bullish without price action confirming it ([Figuring Out Money](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOLz2iSE-iY), [Figuring Out Money](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qoim_txtgWM), [Figuring Out Money](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qr2t9t4uY9Y)). Underneath it all, Michael Howell told Real Vision the liquidity cycle "may be rolling over" with refinancing pressure building system-wide ([Real Vision](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HHeRDiWWprk)), and Eurodollar University ran a five-video drumbeat from a strange oil/yield-curve signal ([Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl_wTYKfiyg)) to "Asian Financial Crisis 2.0," where the rupee is "falling sharply" and Asian currencies sit at or near record lows ([Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4gNWY4bHYQ)).

Gold produced the widest spread of numbers in the corpus. On David Lin's channel alone it was near-daily CEO content: Mike Allen declared "Gold's Crash Is Over" with a new floor in ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AfX_ZBF53HQ)), while the guest of the "Brutal Shakeout" episode argued the washout sets up the next bullish pivot and that WRLG.V should climb "at the same sort of rate of change" as gold ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DB-0gMxwmzw)). CPM Group was the sober counterweight: silver had tested $55, and with roughly 19.3 million ounces of August COMEX open interest still outstanding, prices had support "during the next week or two" ([CPM Group](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u9jx89f0P3I)). The striking thing is that the bulls agree on direction and disagree wildly on magnitude — Joe Cavatoni framed a $5,000 breakout ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MIRTnky8Hkg)), Steve Hanke kept a $6,000 target though it is "going to take longer" ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X_D5weq9KrY)), Willem Middelkoop jumped to $14,000 gold and $500 silver on a "Big Reset" ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fqY4zMtBS74)), and Hanke was separately asked whether a $20,000 revaluation could erase US debt ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X_D5weq9KrY)). Dr Ng Ming Tak supplied the mismatch case: a Hong Kong gold jeweller's stock down 67% and over a hundred billion in market value gone, even as the PBoC bought gold for a twentieth straight month ([Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6iaBj14yT1Y)).

The AI trade split three ways. Steve Hou on Forward Guidance argued efficiency is repricing compute — GPU rental prices softening at the one-year mark, price down but quantity up enough that "everyone still… do very well" — and that he would be "very very shocked" if memory-makers' gross margins were still this elevated in two years, because "memory is ultimately a commodity" ([Forward Guidance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJE3YWA4LLk)). Russell Clark agreed memory is stretched — "DRAM, NAND… now priced like Nvidia chips" — but was "very skeptical" that Microsoft, Meta, Google and Amazon would all cut AI capex 50%, and relocated the real crisis to US Treasuries ([The Monetary Matters Network](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wS4MV0FbVm8)). He Q-C's HKEJ pieces put geopolitics first: Washington moving to bar US firms from Chinese large models, Google's stock falling on its warning even as 2027 capex stays high, and richly-valued Zhipu and MinMax facing real political risk ([HKEJ](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4460666/%E8%8F%AF%E5%BA%9C%E5%B0%87%E7%A6%81%E7%BE%8E%E4%BC%81%E7%94%A8%E4%B8%AD%E5%9C%8B%E5%A4%A7%E6%A8%A1%E5%9E%8B%3F), [HKEJ](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4464337/%E4%B8%AD%E7%BE%8E%E5%85%A8%E9%9D%A2AI%E7%88%AD%E9%9C%B8)). Technicians read the same tape oppositely: Figuring Out Money counted DRAM down 40% and EWY down 30%, "in that bear market territory" ([Figuring Out Money](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOLz2iSE-iY)), while AM Invest called a post-crash DRAM rebound potentially "quite sharp" and later flagged Micron as the strongest stock, having escaped its descending channel ([AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-164345791), [AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-164723944)). The adjacent scare was private credit: Monetary Matters insisted the next crisis "isn't in banks, it's in insurance & private credit" — commercial real estate "a disaster," FSK at a 50% discount ([The Monetary Matters Network](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5qkNpMxD9kw)) — and Eurodollar University declared the crisis had "just reached Amazon" ([Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSLkb3BB_0M)), with George Gammon adding a "$10 trillion derivatives timebomb" for good measure ([George Gammon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YbIKPH-rK8w)).

Hong Kong ran as its own cluster. Dr Ng was near-daily: the HSI gapping up 200 points then dumping 800 with 25,166 a "fake breakout" and A-shares diverging into "six Q3 storms" ([Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YRWS-k3V1Co)); roughly US$300 billion of Hong Kong share unlocks over the next twelve months, concentrated late July to mid-September, dragging prices 5–10% ([Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YRWS-k3V1Co)); and a US-China trade truce that holds, but with the market's real direction waiting on September ([Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AUvvBgTbZxY)). Note that several of his uploads were archive replays billed as 《淺見回顧》, including the HSBC piece doing the math on a Hang Seng Bank privatization — capital adequacy sliding from about 14.8% to 13.5% if HK$100 billion of cash leaves the door, dividend kept, buybacks trimmed ([Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ogzKQRUyfQ)). Oil threaded through the war angle: Goehring & Rozencwajg sized up commodities "in the wake of the U.S.-Iran War" ([Goehring & Rozencwajg](https://blog.gorozen.com/blog/commodity-outlook-in-the-wake-of-the-us-iran-war)), Dr Ng drew a line at $90 — a sudden break above being a negative signal ([Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YRWS-k3V1Co)) — an Idaho gold executive relayed chatter that crude is "headed to 120" ([Resource Talks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gXgvIhQTUI0)), and Eurodollar University noted oil "doesn't seem to want to get much cheaper anytime soon" ([Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4gNWY4bHYQ)), while Patrick Boyle's detour asked where Venezuela's oil money went ([Patrick Boyle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EzvdGKs05Rc)).

On actual conversions, the week is thin, and it is worth saying so plainly: 81 items and 199 predictions, yet almost nobody admitted being wrong. Horwitz explicitly refused — "I'm not changing my views" ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3lcf3jksM-4)) — and Hanke's is the only visible soft revision, a $6,000 gold target kept while its timetable quietly stretched ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X_D5weq9KrY)). AM Invest's drift from "the rebound could be sharp" to celebrating Micron's channel breakout is the closest thing to a mood shift toward the bulls ([AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-164723944)). Otherwise the discourse mostly escalated a standing bear case rather than flipping anyone — Luke Gromen's "As The Conflict Turns" on MacroVoices keeping that thread warm ([MacroVoices](https://www.macrovoices.com/1542-macrovoices-542-luke-gromen-as-the-conflict-turns)) — which tells you the week's real action was in conviction, not in minds being changed.
