## The digest

The week of June 21–27 had one center of gravity: whether the AI trade has finally cracked. The corpus says so on its face — AI/semiconductors drew 34 views, the week's largest theme, even as Bitcoin topped the ticker count with 18 calls ahead of gold's 12. The tape gave the bears their material. [高天佑](../People/person-015.md) tallied the damage midweek: US AI leaders down more than 10% in a single session, South Korea off 9.99%, Hong Kong at a one-year low ([AI敍事亟需「再驗證」](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/finnews/article/4438934/AI%E6%95%8D%E4%BA%8B%E4%BA%9F%E9%9C%80%E3%80%8C%E5%86%8D%E9%A9%97%E8%AD%89%E3%80%8D)). Around it: SpaceX sliding to $173 in its first days of listed trading ([SpaceX溢價僅馬斯克獨有](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4436065/SpaceX%E6%BA%A2%E5%83%B9%E5%83%85%E9%A6%AC%E6%96%AF%E5%85%8B%E7%8D%A8%E6%9C%89)), Bitcoin sitting more than 50% below last year's high and gold down from $5,000 ([Massive Liquidity Shock Coming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CDK7G4lTKII)), silver down 15% in two or three days ([The Dollar Reset Just Started](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RmNZLvOBu8M)). [Ed Zitron](../People/ed-zitron.md) supplied the bear-case centerpiece on [Jack Farley](../People/jack-farley.md)'s Monetary Matters Network — OpenAI's 2025: $34 billion of spending, losses up nearly eightfold — and called a pop in 2027 "the safe bet," giving Nvidia "two, three more earnings calls" to keep delivering guidance that thrills ([The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)).

Where commentators landed split cleanly. Zitron's frame is a financing one — what looks like "demand for AI" is really "demand for speculative debt" — while 高天佑 stopped short of calling the story dead, arguing it is neither falsified nor confirmed and, with MIT and PwC surveys showing most enterprise AI projects produce no measurable return, needs "re-verification" before its next leg ([AI敍事亟需「再驗證」](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/finnews/article/4438934/AI%E6%95%8D%E4%BA%8B%E4%BA%9F%E9%9C%80%E3%80%8C%E5%86%8D%E9%A9%97%E8%AD%89%E3%80%8D)). [Mike Alfred](../People/mike-alfred.md), on the other hand, told [Ash Bennington](../People/ash-bennington.md) the trade was never software but scarce land, power and physical infrastructure; he's holding his AI-infrastructure firm Iron, sees a multi-decade opportunity in the market's misunderstanding, and named a straight long Bitcoin as essentially the only position he publicly espouses ([The Hidden AI Trade](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-p3DhlY2TEk)). [aminvest](../People/aminvest.md) hedged from the bullish side — Micron as the single stock that dragged half the market higher, large-cap tech likely higher still, but the easiest money in US equities probably behind us ([美股市況短評 (20260625)](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-162048909)) — while Joseph Wang flagged a 10% limit-down stumble as "a big red flag" and open-source momentum as "a very big threat to the closed AI models" ([Markets Weekly June 27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UzRGWHUuJRI)), and [何啟聰](../People/person-002.md) warned the SK Hynix two-times leveraged ETF (07709), now bigger than the Tracker Fund, could turn a Hynix shock into a systemic event for Korea ([海力士槓桿ETF威脅韓股](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4440216/%E6%B5%B7%E5%8A%9B%E5%A3%AB%E6%A7%93%E6%A1%BFETF%E5%A8%81%E8%84%85%E9%9F%93%E8%82%A1)).

The head-to-heads were unusually sharp. On Bitcoin, Alfred's long stood against [George Noble](../People/george-noble.md)'s dismissal on Macro Dirt — "Bitcoin is the Facebook of speculative assets... just going to go into the sunset" — with another voice calling it "a story that is coming to an end" ([George Noble On Stocks, Gold, Oil, and AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LDrOV4i0EU4)). Gold split Noble (calling for $4,200, down with fiat) from [Jeffrey Christian](../People/jeffrey-christian.md), whose technical map ran the other way — a break of $4,100 "could point toward $3,800" — though Christian, ever two-handed, also called this a good time for long-term buyers to accumulate ([Gold And Silver Price WARNING](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O-RRUlGQb6o)). SpaceX produced the widest spread: [JD](../People/jd.md)'s claim that "if there was any stock that had a chance of going to a 10 trillion market cap, it would be SpaceX" versus Zitron's 2026 scenario if SpaceX keeps "trundling down," with Noble buying puts "big time" while conceding "it may go to 300 before it goes to 50" ([George Noble On Stocks, Gold, Oil, and AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LDrOV4i0EU4)). On the dollar, an unnamed bull pointed to the index "at 101 or going to 102," against Noble — "no opinion on the dollar... actually I think it goes lower, if you must know" — and JD, who called the greenback's run "a false breakout... I'm very bearish."

The kicker is how freshly those positions were taken. Noble flipped from bearish to bullish on the dollar between June 18 and the June 23 taping — fiat currencies "a waste of time," yet the dollar isn't going away, and "I'm putting it all in bonds" — while JD flipped the other way, from bullish on June 17 to bearish; the two then settled it on air with a bet on the 10-year, Noble "I'm higher," JD "definitely lower" ([George Noble On Stocks, Gold, Oil, and AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LDrOV4i0EU4)). Elsewhere the churn ran one way. 高天佑 was still cataloguing China's weak domestic demand on June 24 — May retail sales down 0.6% year-on-year, the first decline since end-2022 and the first outside the pandemic era, against exports up 13.8% ([《求是》響警號 內需新思維](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/finnews/article/4437891/%E3%80%8A%E6%B1%82%E6%98%AF%E3%80%8B%E9%9F%BF%E8%AD%A6%E8%99%9F+%E5%85%A7%E9%9C%80%E6%96%B0%E6%80%9D%E7%B6%AD)) — and was recorded bullish on China and Hong Kong equities; one AI rout later he was bearish ([AI敍事亟需「再驗證」](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/finnews/article/4438934/AI%E6%95%8D%E4%BA%8B%E4%BA%9F%E9%9C%80%E3%80%8C%E5%86%8D%E9%A9%97%E8%AD%89%E3%80%8D)). aminvest traced the mood best: watching a "key resistance" on June 22 ([美股市況短評 (20260622)](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-161763918)), halving equity exposure within a day and pricing bear odds at 20% ([倉位無大礙，但減倉保平安](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/cang-wei-wu-da-161877458)), then flipping bearish on rates and credit — "corporate debt default ratios are beginning to deteriorate" — by June 25 ([美股市況短評 (20260625)](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-162048909)), all while mocking anyone buying a break of $150 in SPCX as naive, given his math that Musk loses the top spot only at $17.61 ([美股市況短評 (20260624)](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-161958989)).

Beneath it all ran private credit, the week's connective tissue. [Jeff Snider](../People/jeff-snider.md) appeared three times: Swiss yields scraping zero as a "dire warning," the TIPS market saying "there is no inflation" ([Swiss Bond Market Just Gave A Dire Warning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2LzapdRuT4)); then, with Apollo Debt Solutions facing repurchase requests near 16.8% of shares, Blue Owl making new lows and MFIC near 85 cents on the dollar, "the bust is accelerating" ([Private Credit Default Rates Just Hit The Point Of No Return](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SibAV9HRe1I)); then doubling down on June 27 with Apollo's fund "cash flow negative" and Q3 redemption projections "even higher" ([Bitcoin And BlackRock Just Did Something VERY Strange](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=biyCX7UYAfQ)). Geopolitics hummed underneath — 雷鼎鳴 argued Iran holds the upper hand over Washington precisely because it can spike oil in US election years ([拆解伊強弱底牌](https://www.master-insight.com/article/48968)) — but stayed background noise. The honest coda came from [李聲揚](../People/person-009.md), who argued that even perfect foresight of macro events is almost zero help to investors — the market's reaction kills you anyway ([宏觀交易過五關 AI落場都輸錢](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4440220/%E5%AE%8F%E8%A7%80%E4%BA%A4%E6%98%93%E9%81%8E%E4%BA%94%E9%97%9C+AI%E8%90%BD%E5%A0%B4%E9%83%BD%E8%BC%B8%E9%8C%A2)). The week's unresolved question is whether Zitron's "speculative debt" and Snider's accelerating bust are two views of the same crack; nobody, least of all the 20%-bear bulls, claimed to know.
