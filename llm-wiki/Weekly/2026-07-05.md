## The digest

Gold and oil owned the conversation this week. [GC=F](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VwM4Z2yXxD0) drew seven calls and crude five, and the gold discourse ran on two parallel tracks: David Lin's channel was an interview assembly line — Dan Wilton framing gold as "50% crash or violent rally," Rick Rule on the one thing in credit that scares him, Matthew Piepenburg declaring the monetary reset underway — while the measured money, [CPM Group](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8b3o8gWKdeQ), spent the week explaining in two separate updates why investors are *selling* the metal and why prices remain under pressure. Oil got the same double treatment: [Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bwBj2SgdolY) ran the demand-shock alarm across multiple segments ("worst possible scenario," China's oil demand as the biggest macro signal, [Walmart cutting prices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wMG9-0SZIXU) because consumers are "out of money"), while [Adam Parker on MacroVoices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nIFH7B6OQzs) argued the moves are mechanical — spec positioning normalization and momentum alone could swing crude $6–20 a barrel, in either direction. [Joseph Wang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cpxOs7TnBZ0) split the difference: crude and refined products jumped, but overall markets were "pretty subdued."

The DRAM/memory trade was the week's second obsession and produced its sharpest disagreement. [何啟聰](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4449750/%E6%B5%B7%E5%8A%9B%E5%A3%AB%E6%A7%93%E6%A1%BFETF+%E6%B8%AF%E7%BE%8E%E9%A2%A8%E9%9A%AA%E7%9B%B8%E8%8B%A5) wrote twice in HKEJ — on Hynix leveraged ETFs carrying similar risk in Hong Kong and the US, and on how [crowded the memory trade](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4454123/%E5%85%A7%E5%AD%98%E6%93%81%E6%93%A0%E4%BA%A4%E6%98%93+%E6%A7%93%E6%A1%BF%E5%A5%97%E6%88%A5%E5%90%84%E6%9C%89%E7%9B%A4%E7%AE%97) has become — while [Dr Ng Ming Tak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r88X-YkTmcY) opened the week recounting SK Hynix's double-digit slide around July 2 and noting TSMC's market value had surpassed Samsung's, even as Taiwan's index has doubled from 20,000 to 46,000. On direction, the split was clean: [The Macro Dirt Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8IA0EW7aGYA) called MU "a dead cat bounce" and predicted SpaceX would be trading back at its IPO price within two weeks, whereas [AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-163435342) told readers DRAM "won't be a stock that falls and never rises" and that the Nasdaq has room to rise further. Dr Ng was also the volume leader in raw output, hammering Hong Kong themes all week: skepticism that the record [HK$42.2tn managed-assets headline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Exu_y3Vii1s) is anything but 風向, luxury-home records that are ["子虛烏有"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d4f7RQmX4ew) with no meaningful recovery visible and prices not crashing only because everyone is waiting, shop vacancies caused by [dying demand rather than rent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XFvEBVUS4_A), the [Bond Connect 300bn liquidity question](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvW1X2kOqHU), and the EU's 45.3% tariff on China. Local columnists joined the geopolitics beat — [雷鼎鳴](https://www.master-insight.com/article/49136) and [陸振球](https://www.master-insight.com/article/49168) both marking America's 250th with hegemony-decline essays — and [Patrick Boyle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cRiOkQ1ngAM) asked whether Russia is actually losing.

Beyond memory chips, the sharpest structural split was on the AI regime itself: [Raoul Pal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3n2UpyKETZw) declaring "the biggest investment cycle in history has already begun" against Parker's entire thesis of looking "beyond the AI bubble." The doomsday roster stayed busy — [Harry Dent](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3oRNtmTE0gA) on a 90% collapse, [George Gammon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=42cunoNk_HA) triggering a subprime crisis "bigger than 2008," Eurodollar University relaying [HSBC's warning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aOYRaNIz-NY) that spread widening can overwhelm years of extra yield — while [Figuring Out Money](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JvdwBimyqy4) cited JPM's SPX collar (upper bound near 8,000) and prior ratio extremes preceding 10% corrections as its bearish tell. Meanwhile [Juliette Declercq](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20SxYVwZDLE) and [Maleeha Bengali](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YsR_uqKU-5s) agreed on one thing only: the consensus on US inflation is wrong. Inside gold, Adrian Day closed the week as the [loudest bull](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=irw_Z6o1r44) — central banks shedding dollar exposure, a "meaningful revaluation" possible, gold stocks already 35% off January — set against CPM's selling-pressure read and [Lobo Tiggre's](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4rPvgifBkwg) hedge that his base case holds but January 2026 "was another one of those peaks."

On who actually changed their mind: the digest documents no clean about-face, and I won't manufacture one. The closest thing is Tiggre's softening — "stupid cheap" headline intact, but a new admission that January may have been a peak. Adrian Day turning aggressively bullish with gold stocks 35% below January reads as conviction into weakness, not a conversion, and CPM Group's July 7-to-July-10 shift from "why investors are selling" to "prices under pressure" is continuity. A caveat on the data itself: 107 predictions extracted this week were tagged almost uniformly neutral, which suggests genuine stance changes are being flattened by the labeling — a limitation worth fixing before next week's edition.
