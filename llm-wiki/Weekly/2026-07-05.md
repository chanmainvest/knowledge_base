The number-two battleground was memory, and it produced the week's most direct call-versus-call: The Macro Dirt Podcast called Micron's bounce "a dead cat bounce" ([Macro Dirt, Jul 9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8IA0EW7aGYA)) the same week AM Invest's desk notes insisted "DRAM 不會是一跌不起的股份" and that the Nasdaq could yet rise ([AM Invest, Jul 10](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-163435342)). 何啟聰's HKEJ reporting explains why both camps watch the same tape — the leveraged memory trade is crowded, with Hong Kong and US Hynix-leverage products carrying similar risk ([何啟聰, HKEJ](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4454123/%E5%85%A7%E5%AD%98%E6%93%81%E6%93%A0%E4%BA%A4%E6%98%93+%E6%A7%93%E6%A1%BF%E5%A5%97%E6%88%A5%E5%90%84%E6%9C%89%E7%9B%A4%E7%AE%97)). Parker's episode title, "Beyond the AI Bubble," was its own stance ([MacroVoices](https://www.macrovoices.com/1540-macrovoices-540-adam-parker-beyond-the-ai-bubble-diversifying-portfolios-in-an-earnings-driven-market)), and Bitcoin drew the bleakest thought experiment of the week: quantum "Q day" compressed from "decades away" to perhaps two-and-a-half to five years, IBM's ~2030 roadmap cited as the marker, with the twist that a reversed public key means total, irreversible loss ([David Lin, Jul 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NWOFw-CUrQA)).

The deeper argument was about the dollar, and the camps genuinely opposed.
