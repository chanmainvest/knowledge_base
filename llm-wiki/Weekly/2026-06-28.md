## The digest

With 77 items, 20 voices, 196 predictions and 246 logged market views, the loudest thread this week wasn't gold's 18 calls or oil's 13 — it was whether the AI trade is finally breaking. Meta's news was read in [David Lin's interview with David Woo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QjuThPhidXY) as "a bearish piece of news for all the chip sector… we bought too many chips, we overbuilt our AI infrastructure," [何啟聰](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4447682/Meta%E3%80%8C%E4%BB%BB%E6%80%A7%E3%80%8D%E9%9C%87%E5%8B%95%E5%B8%82%E5%A0%B4) framed Meta's "任性" as market-shaking, [Forward Guidance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18-zFIyoJAo) declared the AI trade cracking outright, and [AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-162495667) caught Nvidia printing lower highs and lower lows with Microsoft "仍處於長期弱勢之中". [Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ktLyXGRHNCk) went further, arguing private credit just burst a $25 trillion AI bubble. The bull rebuttal was just as emphatic: [Real Vision's guests](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3LVb6esEFOk) called it "nothing short of a spectacular earnings boom" and dismissed the war as "a distraction" from an earnings-fueled rally, while [The Monetary Matters Network](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fcuaGmxTFZk) aired the "Reverse Crash" case for the S&P 500 hitting 10,000 by 2027, with "hyperscalers not backing off… actually accelerating the bet". Historian [Liaquat Ahamed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=czdGQG9HvGM) offered the sober middle path: data centers near 2% of GDP and "a series of mini booms and busts… in the AI boom," on a railroad-crash parallel.

The sharpest formal disagreement in the digest is oil. The bull side — [EJ Antoni on David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0zIks7TeCWI) — argued "we're basically replacing a negative supply shock with a positive demand shock," with the MacroVoices camp adding "additional upward pressure on prices for a while until those reserves get refilled," the framing behind [Rory Johnston's "Hormuz Crisis, is it Really Over?"](https://www.macrovoices.com/1539-macrovoices-539-rory-johnston-hormuz-crisis-is-it-really-over) episode. The bear side, exemplified by [strategist Sam Burns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mVsHCgJcVc), countered that absent "a much bigger shock than what we've had," pump prices could "come back down and even reach pre-war levels and stay there for a very long time" — a view [Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21KrmQp4FWQ) shared in expecting the energy shock to fade and oil to normalize.

The same bull-bear fault line runs through equities. George Gammon put "a 67% probability that you have a bear market… at least 20% decline… in the S&P 500" on an MSTR/STRC divergence indicator, admitted "right now we're in a classic bear market… that's how we're trading" ([here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8QB9yHWVumQ)), then doubled down Saturday asking if we're on the brink of another financial crisis ([here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5GkdhYhW2-c)); [David Woo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QjuThPhidXY) warned of another 20% down once the AI story pops — with gold the beneficiary — and [Eric Basmajian](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YHWw-UDPo4w) cautioned that equities "do not lead the economy at business cycle peaks" but are coincident or lagging. Against that, [Joseph Wang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P9oxwMfNItU) allowed "we could actually see new all-time highs in the broader S&P 500 before anything bad happens" (while flagging Nasdaq's failure to make new highs), and the [analyst who called the Bitcoin top](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dK393rKN-a4) still expects "a decent rally at the end of the year" and new equity highs by end-2027. The rotation camp — [Sam Rahman on tech rotation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hlGXQC1css0), [李聲揚](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4447686/%E7%82%BA%E4%BD%95%E4%BB%8A%E5%B9%B4%E7%BE%8E%E3%80%8C%E5%83%B9%E5%80%BC%E8%82%A1%E3%80%8D%E9%81%A0%E5%8B%9D%E3%80%8C%E5%A2%9E%E9%95%B7%E8%82%A1%E3%80%8D) on value crushing growth — suggests the bulls may simply be changing seats rather than directions.

Gold's 18-call cluster was internally split too. [CPM Group](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-6Zt6_Rj4EQ) sees consolidation "over the next few two months, but then rise in the final part of this year," with repeated breaks below $4,100 and silver the most bullish metal; the [analyst who called the drop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nz7zxxJBNfo) says the crash isn't over; junior operators like [Mithril](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WIZH1VMJSYA) are still modeling $3,500–3,600 gold; and the [Equinox-linked interview with Ryan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI) conceded shares are down since the Orla deal, with management "price takers" and "share price appreciation agnostic to the metal price." Meanwhile the SpaceX debate bracketed everything: [克魯明](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdCPC3L8ta8) called Musk "真人版龐氏騙局," while [the IPO scholar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKT7oRk7OTI) noted that "if it wasn't for SpaceX, it's very likely that Anthropic and OpenAI… might be the first or second biggest IPO ever," and [The Macro Dirt Podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0JS8MCvpqoE) simply concluded "rates are far too high." Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor, the week's most prolific voice, ran his own parallel track on Hong Kong: 公私合營 and the capital-flight lockdown ([here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QDPaFmGE2ug)), the HSBC deposit trust crack ([here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UoPfdIK-NpE)), and the verdict that the HKEX reform "係唔可以救到個市" while Hang Seng fights to hold 22,500 ([here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZbDIpbS3gjo)) — with [施永青's](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EsLONcdNitc) call for property up 20% in a year and 85% by 2031 rebutted point by point, and [徐家健 with Grok](https://www.master-insight.com/article/48974) dissecting JD's price-war "迫爆" of Hong Kong retail.

On actual reversals, the week is thinner than the noise suggests — say so plainly. The only explicit pivot on record is conditional: [AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-162393012) stated it would turn medium-term bullish on US stocks if the Nasdaq breaks resistance in the first 90 minutes. The closest thing to a retraction came from the [Bitcoin-top caller](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dK393rKN-a4), who admitted all the conditions for Bitcoin to soar "ended up benefiting gold instead, and gold crushed Bitcoin" — a retracted bull case rather than a new call — and Dr Ng referenced his old "Bitcoin must come down" thesis on the [Polymarket video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FYpSkLdVYXY) without stating a fresh stance. Beyond those, the week was largely stance-holding at high volume, which is itself informative: nobody prominent flipped on AI, gold, or oil; they just dug in louder.
