## The digest

The loudest argument this week was about the AI trade, and for once the bears set the tempo. Meta's latest unilateral move — 何啟聰's word for it was 任性, "wilful" — shook markets and was promptly read as a bearish signal for the entire chip complex, "basically a sign that the day of reckoning" has arrived, in David Woo's telling ([Meta「任性」震動市場](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4447682/Meta%E3%80%8C%E4%BB%BB%E6%80%A7%E3%80%8D%E9%9C%87%E5%8B%95%E5%B8%82%E5%A0%B4), [David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QjuThPhidXY)). Forward Guidance declared "The AI Trade Is Finally Cracking" ([Weekly Roundup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=18-zFIyoJAo)), Eurodollar University went further and said private credit "just burst the $25 trillion AI bubble" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ktLyXGRHNCk)), and even 何啟聰 flagged memory-chip fat margins as a hidden vulnerability rather than a triumph ([HKEJ](https://www1.hkej.com/dailynews/investment/article/4442430/%E5%85%A7%E5%AD%98%E8%A8%98%E6%86%B6%E9%AB%94%E9%AB%98%E6%AF%9B%E5%88%A9%E5%8F%8D%E6%88%90%E9%9A%B1%E6%86%82)). The bull case didn't go quiet, though: Erik pushed "The Reverse Crash" — S&P 500 straight to 10,000 by 2027, hyperscalers "not backing off… actually accelerating the bet," even as he conceded semis are "crowding out every other sector" and worried aloud about Meta glasses flopping ([Monetary Matters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fcuaGmxTFZk)). Real Vision's panel split the difference — Ram still sees a bull market, Jeff insists earnings drive everything, and someone noted the extra $20–25 billion from Anthropic and Google renting Colossus capacity ([Real Vision](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3LVb6esEFOk)) — while Liaquat Ahamed supplied the historian's warning: data centers at roughly 2% of GDP, competitive herd behavior producing "poor returns for everyone," and "a series of mini booms and busts" in the AI boom ([Ahamed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=czdGQG9HvGM)).

Gold was the week's most-trafficked ticker — 18 calls on GC=F — and the metals crowd talked like a bottom was being mapped, not a top. Jeff, the analyst who called the drop, noted gold has broken below $4,100 repeatedly but expects consolidation "over the next few two months, then rise in the final part of this year," and said flatly he is "most bullish on gold and silver" ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nz7zxxJBNfo)). CPM Group countered with a rates framework — gold rises when real T-bill returns are zero, goes neutral at 2% — and flagged platinum at $1,211 risking a slide back into its $900–1,200 range, plus 78 million ounces of silver dumped by ETF investors ([CPM Group](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-6Zt6_Rj4EQ)). On the mining side, Ryan tied the metals complex to an inevitable "major debt crisis" reckoning while admitting Equinox Gold is down since the Orla deal and "we are price takers" ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)); Mithril was modeling $3,500–3,600 gold while its interviewer reminded everyone that "failure is the norm" in exploration ([Resource Talks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WIZH1VMJSYA)) — and Surge Copper's $4.6 billion BC capex question kept SURG.V, with ten calls, the busiest small-cap of the week ([Resource Talks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k)).

Hong Kong and China was the other dense cluster, and Dr Ng Ming Tak, Victor basically was the cluster: capital flight from Hong Kong getting harder ([淺見回顧](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IXQkE20FFa8)), Beijing re-raising 公私合營 with a 25-year retroactive look at outflows and his call that the HSI "will reflect these measures — already down ten" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QDPaFmGE2ug)), the HSBC "vanished" NT$200,000 deposit as evidence a big bank is "losing the people's trust" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UoPfdIK-NpE)), the HKMA's sudden disclosure of dollar assets in the Exchange Fund, and $430 billion of reserves against a trillion in planned issuance ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xNv8gt0ojIM)). He capped it with a defense of 22,500 on the HSI — the HKEX reforms are "tool-level" convenience, not a savior, but if US stocks hold, "at least in Q3" Hong Kong won't crack ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZbDIpbS3gjo)) — and a recap of the IMF's claim that the yuan's "rise" masks a 16% real devaluation ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0yAFQSCUJEU)). The sharpest local clash: 施永青's forecast of 20% property gains within a year and 85% by 2031, which Victor rebutted point by point ([施永青](https://www.master-insight.com/article/49024), [反駁影片](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EsLONcdNitc)), while 徐家健 used JD.com's Hong Kong "迫爆" opening — HK$35 billion-plus deployed, half-price promos — to argue this is structural creative destruction, not retail apocalypse ([Master Insight](https://www.master-insight.com/article/48974)).

The cleanest opposing lines formed around oil. The bulls had EJ Antoni arguing "we're basically replacing a negative supply shock with a positive demand shock," with chip and laptop price hikes already in the PPI ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0zIks7TeCWI)), and the MacroVoices camp expecting "additional upward pressure on prices for a while until those reserves get refilled" even as Rory Johnston asked whether the Hormuz crisis is "really over" ([MacroVoices #539](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V8hpqXTS6Q4)). The bears — Eurodollar University prominent — see the energy shock fading and pump prices back at pre-war levels "for a very long time" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21KrmQp4FWQ)). Equities drew a three-way split: George Gammon put 67% odds on a ≥20% S&P decline and a GFC-rival recession if it lands in 2026, yet refused a directional bet and traded the STRC/MSTR divergence instead ([Gammon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8QB9yHWVumQ)); Joseph Wang said we "could actually see new all-time highs" in the S&P before anything bad, while Nasdaq's failure to make new highs "could be the beginning of" something uglier ([Wang](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P9oxwMfNItU)); David Woo wants another 20% of downside priced and, on gold, blames higher real yields ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QjuThPhidXY)). On rates, Sam Burns sees no reason for the Fed to cut ([Burns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mVsHCgJcVc)) — the Macro Dirt crowd insists rates are "far too high" for an equity-funded market ([Macro Dirt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0JS8MCvpqoE)).

As for genuine conversions, the record is thin — nearly everything in this week's 256 views is tagged neutral, which is itself the story of a hedge-everything stretch. The closest thing to a pivot is Jeff, the gold-crash caller, now selling a two-month consolidation followed by a year-end rally ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nz7zxxJBNfo)), and AM Invest's explicitly conditional flip: no bottom call at pre-market strength, but a decisive NQ break of resistance turns them bullish mid-term, while failure risks an MNQ slide toward 28,300 ([AM Invest](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-162393012)). Even the analyst famous for calling the Bitcoin top spent his airtime looking for new equity highs by end-2027 ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dK393rKN-a4)) — less a conversion than the week's pattern: everyone holding triggers, nobody pulling them.
