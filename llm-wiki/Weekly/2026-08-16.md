## The digest

With 395 predictions and 397 market views logged from 47 voices, the week's center of gravity was unmistakable: rates and bonds (55 views), specifically the Treasury's bond buyback and whether it amounts to yield-curve control in all but name. Eurodollar University set the skeptical tone on August 19, calling the program "a pitiful $2 billion, two and a half billion when you include the tips" and wondering how the yield curve gets controlled "with just a couple billion extra buybacks" — a day after flagging the 30-year's surge, which MacroVoices' desk pegged near 5.30% before the reversal. By August 20 David Lin was hosting panels on why the Treasury had doubled the buybacks, and Joseph Wang's Sunday verdict was that the intervention "definitely buys time" but is "ultimately temporary," noting there's "no limit to this — he could just really buy it all if he really wanted to." Luke Gromen framed the trap most crisply: 10-year yields "have to go way higher," yet Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent (transcribed as "Besson") won't let them clear roughly 4.7–4.8% — so either the dollar weakens a lot or yields get forced down, and "all roads lead to gold." Chris Whalen asked the darker question — when is the next Treasury-market crisis, and how much paper must the Fed absorb — while michaelwgreen supplied the structural backstory across three posts: Japan's exit shrinks the automatic bid for Treasuries, the marginal buyer sets the price, and the bond sleeve of American retirement saving has become "a private-sector successor to QE, minus the discretion" ([Anchors Aweigh, My Boys](https://www.yesigiveafig.com/p/anchors-aweigh-my-boys)).

The currency side of the same trade drew heavy fire too — the yen racked up 13 calls, the dollar index 10. Weston Nakamura argued dollar-yen "would have already hit 200 if not for these kind of yen intervention caps," and that over the long term you'll "see dollar yen 200," adding that Japan's broken inflationary mindset is itself "unprecedented." Whalen agreed the yen "is going to continue to weaken," and characterized the dollar as sought after as "a means of exchange, not a store of value." [Darius Dale](../People/darius-dale.md) threaded the middle: the move "has neutralized the prior bull market" in the dollar, but "does that make me an outright bear? Probably not" ([MacroVoices #546](https://www.macrovoices.com/1546-macrovoices-546-darius-dale-darius-dale-for-potus-2028)), while [Peter Boockvar](../People/peter-boockvar.md) stayed with the dollar "ultimately" ending lower ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UrCuK2K8mAU)). George Gammon was the designated deflator of the genre, putting reserve-currency loss "at the very least 25 years and probably a lot longer." Gold, meanwhile, was the release valve everyone kept citing — Dale watching for "legitimate progress back to those April highs near 4,800," Gromen's "all roads," and a 14-call week on GC=F.

The ^GSPC split was the sharpest of the week, with 26 calls. The bull case had echelons: Erik of YWR told Maggie Lake that the $400 EPS he expected for 2028 "is happening in 2027," and "put a 25 times on it and that's S&P 10,000 — one way or the other, we're going to 10,000," because government, companies and banks are all spending ([For The Record](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wV4dksg_XjE)). [Chris Vermeulen](../People/chris-vermeulen.md) called the "next leg higher started" after support held ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=81UzjvXn2no)), the MacroVoices desk gave Nvidia solid odds of a beat-and-raise, and Dale argued "the bond market's already beat you to the task forces... stocks will beat you to the task force." Against that, Harry Dent offered the maximal bear case — "the biggest reversal in history... bigger than '29 to '32," a good time to be "as conservative as you can be," with tech to be bought again only "when they're down the most in history" ([Julia La Roche](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TInyOyKDOgI)). The credit complicates the equity story: [Robin Wigglesworth](../People/robin-wigglesworth.md) sees $1.5 trillion of hidden debt behind the AI boom, public BDCs going to "30 cents or 40 cents of net asset value" in a crisis, and "a bad default cycle" that's "started already" ([Monetary Matters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZXjQZWUHH0)); Patrick Boyle cited Fitch's record July private-credit default rate, one large fund with 7% of its portfolio distressed, and the pattern that manias fade "around the fifth year — we're currently in year three" ([Patrick Boyle](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wTiYaWFP59Q)); and Eurodollar University flagged Nvidia's own collateral clauses as the gray-swan X-factor ([Eurodollar University](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O450x8yyZ-k)). On the panel consensus, [Gareth Soloway](../People/gareth-soloway.md) put the pivot cleanly: 5% on the 10-year is the number — below it, the game of chicken continues ([David Lin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SWsGPoxqcR0)).

Beneath the macro noise, the speculative tail was busy. CoTec (CTH.V, 13 calls) got a full Rule Investment Media pitch — pellet-quality results "in the next few days," pig iron at "roughly 300 to 350" cost, and an admission no raise happens at the $2.5B pre-tax valuation, only "a significant discount" ([Rule Investment Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i25DJxYcDGw)). Silvercorp (SVM, 11 calls) called its China "safety shutdown... annoying" with no hard date for GC, while noting the register has flipped to roughly 60/40 institutional with quant funds circling ([Resource Talks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ialp-FjJiRI)); Justin Huhn had NXE with hyperscalers and BHP "sniffing around" ([VRIC Media](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eDPDUFOi6Uw)). The disagreements ledger also flagged long Treasuries as a trade — one camp noting TLT rose 40% in the 2008 crash and could "literally double" in a serious downturn, the other treating a break toward 80 as a sell trigger — and a purely conditional housing-index debate hinging on whether the long end gets taken down.

On mind-changing, the week was thin, and it's worth saying plainly: nearly every extracted view was tagged neutral, and nobody issued a full reversal. The closest thing was Dale's dollar recalibration, retiring his prior bull-market read without going bear. Vermeulen's arc ran within a single interview — waiting for the post-August 21 window before fully committing to the new leg higher. And AM Invest documented a genuine intraweek adjustment: trimming positions on August 18 ("減倉了，沒有大礙"), conceding the earlier conservative option strikes reflected a view that US stocks "有機會下跌一點," then by August 21 writing that a recovery "有機會再創新高" ([Aug 18](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/jian-cang-liao-167026322); [Aug 21](https://www.patreon.com/aminvest/posts/mei-gu-shi-kuang-167280931)). Otherwise this was a week of conviction expressed almost entirely in conditionals — if the buyback fails, if 5% breaks, if the 21st confirms.
