Only one speaker is even named. That gap between the tidy "3 neutral" label and the messy calls underneath it is itself the story here.

The one identifiable voice is [Jeffrey Christian](../People/jeffrey-christian.md), who on June 23, 2026 offered a range call rather than a directional one: "we think that the Ethereum will probably trade between say 10 and 12,000 over the rest of this year," with an upside case "as high as 17 or 20,000." His official stance is logged as unspecified, and that tracks — this is a walled-garden forecast, constructive on the level of the price but agnostic on the path. It's the kind of call that lets you be right in a chop market and wrong in a trend either way.

The unnamed speaker is where the actual drama lives. On June 27, 2026 they were bearish, describing the thesis for Bitcoin and Ethereum as, in their words, "touch and go" — fragility at the level of the core narrative, not just the price. Six weeks later, on August 6, that same voice had flipped bullish, and the reason they gave was specific: "the most interesting thing has been ETH Bitcoin. Everything else has just been [ __ ] around." In other words, the conversion wasn't a dollar-price epiphany but a relative-value one — ETH as the interesting leg of the ETH/BTC pair while the rest of the complex spun its wheels.

So the shape of the debate, such as it is: one analyst bracketing the price between $10,000 and $20,000 with no directional commitment, and one commentator moving from "the thesis itself is shaky" to "ETH/BTC is the only trade that matters" inside of six weeks. The bull case as expressed is a rotation case; the bear case was a solvency-of-narrative case that its own author abandoned. Whether that flip was conviction or just a better six weeks for the ratio is exactly what the record doesn't tell you — and with one named participant, this page should be read as a snapshot of a debate, not a verdict on it.
