## The debate

The record on Hong Kong equities in this corpus is lopsided enough that "the debate" is almost a misnomer: the aggregate tally reads neutral/watch, but that balance comes from a wall of no-opinion coverage. Among the voices who actually committed to a direction, there are zero bulls. What's on file is a macro bear and a local commentator who talked himself into one.

The macro case comes from [Jeff Snider](../People/jeff-snider.md), who on June 27, 2026 framed Hong Kong as a casualty of dollar-system stress: when the Eurodollar system isn't doing well, he argued, the world turns risk-averse just as the global economy is reversing — and China is "not doing well. In fact, it's accelerating to the downside." That's the entire bull/bear architecture in one sentence: Hong Kong equities as high-beta exposure to a Chinese economy that is deteriorating, not stabilizing.

The more interesting trajectory belongs to an unnamed Cantonese-language commentator who circles the index for two months before landing bearish. On July 23 he was running crash scenarios — the index shedding 5% in a single week, and in a transition shock lasting two to three weeks, drawdowns of as much as 50%. A month later, on August 23, he conceded the strongest bull point anyone in this file offers: valuations are cheap relative to global peers and US equities, a genuine positive. But he immediately smothered it with the sanctions risk hanging over Chinese enterprises. By August 28 the hedging was gone — after a fall of roughly 3,600 points from around 66,200 and an equally quick recovery, his verdict was that any such bounce "can only be treated as a rebound," not a turn in the trend.

So the shape of it: the bull case exists only as a caveat inside a bear argument, the timeline runs from scenario-mongering in July to outright bearishness by late August, and nobody on record flipped the other way. The neutral consensus is really an absence of conviction, not a counterargument.
