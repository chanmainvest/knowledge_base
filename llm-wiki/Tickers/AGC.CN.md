## The debate

As of the record here, AGC.CN doesn't really have a two-sided debate — it has a pitch. The only voice on file is Mohamed Cisse, the CEO of Avanti Gold, and every call attributed to him lands on the same date, August 12, 2026, and the same side: bullish. Meanwhile the tracked consensus sits at three neutral, zero bullish, zero bearish. That gap — management conviction on one side, institutional shrugging on the other — is the actual shape of the story.

The bull case, as Cisse tells it, is a low-mileage exploration story. He points to a 3.1-meter result that's been surfaced with only 20,000 meters of drilling behind it, much of it historical, the implied argument being that the deposit is under-drilled relative to its promise. From there he sketches a classic junior-miner ladder: expand the resource "very quickly because we see a lot of quick wins," grow the project to a "reasonable size," and only then start the preliminary economic assessment process. It's a deliberate sequencing — ounces first, economics later — and it signals management wants to fatten the deposit before spending money on a study that would anchor a valuation.

The bear side is notable for its absence. There are no recorded bear calls, no downgrades, no analyst reversals to chart — the timeline of opinion is a single date. The nearest thing to a counterweight is the neutral consensus itself: three watchers declining to commit in either direction, which for a story this promotional is its own quiet verdict. The honest read is that the bull case is entirely management-sourced; there's no independent validation in the record, and "quick wins" on resource growth and a future PEA are promises, not results. Until someone outside the company puts a flag down, the debate around AGC.CN is really Cisse's enthusiasm versus everyone else's patience — and the coverage is thin enough that even that framing rests on one day's worth of remarks.
