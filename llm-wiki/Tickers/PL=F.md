## The debate

Through the first half of 2026, tracked commentary on platinum leaned firmly bearish — zero bullish calls against three bearish and three neutral — and nearly all of it orbited a single number: $1,500. The recorded sequence opens with [Jeffrey Kushin](../People/jeffrey-kushin.md) on June 16 offering the mildest of the bear calls: he expected platinum to trade between $1,600 and $2,000 over the following couple of months, but with a downside bias. Days later, [Jeffrey Christian](../People/jeffrey-christian.md) was blunter, saying on June 19 that the working view was that both platinum and palladium would move to lower prices over the next few months. Notably, none of the early bears in this corpus is arguing from a fresh fundamental shock — the case is built on positioning and price levels.

That technical framing got explicit on June 23, when Christian identified $1,500 as "one place where technically oriented traders and investors are looking" — trader-speak for a level that, once broken, tends to invite more selling. By June 30, an unattributed voice in the corpus (the digest does not name the speaker) had platinum at $1,565 that morning in New York and was flagging live risk of a further fall and a break below $1,500. Two weeks later, on July 14, the same unattributed view was unchanged: the expectation was "still" downward movement in platinum and palladium. The word "still" is doing a lot of work there — it's the sound of a call that had been patient and saw no reason to retreat. This is a debate the bears were winning by repetition.

Against that backdrop, [Dave Collum](../People/dave-collum.md)'s August 13 entry lands like a counterpunch: he laid out why platinum "remains one of his preferred commodity trades." That is the entire bull docket in this digest — one asserted conviction, arriving only after weeks in which the bearish chorus had been pressing the $1,500 line, and with no recorded reasoning attached here. Whether that is contrarian timing or simply thin coverage of the bull side is impossible to say from this corpus; what is clear is that the bull case currently exists as an opinion without a documented argument, which makes it the weakest-recorded position even as it's the newest one.

As for minds changed: nobody here is documented flipping. The unattributed June 30 and July 14 calls are consistent with each other — at worst one speaker doubling down, at best two bears reinforcing the same view. The three neutral voices are the quiet majority, unwilling to commit in either direction. So the real fight is narrower than bulls-versus-bears: it's whether $1,500 is a floor the technicians will defend or a waypoint on the way lower — with Collum, as of mid-August, the lone voice betting the bears have already had their say.
