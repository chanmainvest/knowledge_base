## The debate

Through June and July of 2026, the platinum debate was barely a debate at all — it was a bear camp with a number attached to it. Jeffrey Kushin opened the sequence on June 16, expecting the price to trade between $1,600 and $2,000 over the following couple of months but with a downside bias, on the grounds of "greater" (his reasoning cuts off mid-sentence in the record) selling pressure [Jeffrey Kushin](../People/jeffrey-christian.md) — strike that: [Jeffrey Kushin](../People/jeffrey-kushin.md). Three days later, Jeffrey Christian said the prevailing view was that both platinum and palladium could move to lower prices over the next few months, and by June 23 he had sharpened it into a level: $1,500 was where "technically oriented" traders and investors were looking [Jeffrey Christian](../People/jeffrey-christian.md). An unattributed commentator in the corpus put the price near $1,565 in New York on June 30 and flagged serious risk of a break below that $1,500 mark — the same voice was still calling for downward movement in platinum and palladium on July 14, a "still" that tells you this was continuity, not a fresh thesis.

The bear case as recorded is notably technical rather than fundamental. Nobody in the digest argues from auto demand, mine supply, or ETF flows; the argument is that $1,500 is the level everyone is watching, and Christian's framing implicitly concedes it could be self-fulfilling. The falsifiable claim on the table was simple: does it break, or doesn't it.

Against that stands one man. Dave Collum, as of August 13, was bullish, naming platinum one of his preferred commodity trades [Dave Collum](../People/dave-collum.md). That makes him the lone bull in a tally of zero bullish, three bearish and three neutral — and, frustratingly, the digest records the stance without the argument behind it. The bull case here is a position, not a case.

On mind-changing: nobody in this record actually flips. Christian is consistent across both his June entries, Kushin's range call already embedded the downside skew, and Collum appears bullish at his only recorded data point — a contrarian marker rather than a documented reversal. The quiet tension in the timeline is that the bears' $1,500 test was live at roughly $1,565 as of June 30, and Collum's bull call arrived six weeks later. Whether he was catching a turn or fighting a breakdown, the digest doesn't say. Coverage after mid-August is thin, and the page is honest about that.
