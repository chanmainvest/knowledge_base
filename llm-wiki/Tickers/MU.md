## The debate

The Micron debate as logged here is carried by a remarkably small cast: of the nine voices on file, seven sit neutral, leaving a one-man bear case and a loosely bullish technical camp to argue it out. Nobody in the record disputes that demand is real — the fight is entirely over when the memory cycle turns, and whether the chart says it already has.

The bear case belongs to David Woo, who staked out his down call on June 15, 2026 with an argument that concedes everything the bulls say about the present and then pivots: all the chips Micron is making right now are sold out into 2028 for forward sales — and yet he flags down anyway, because the trouble starts once capacity comes online [David Woo](../People/david-woo.md). It's the classic memory-semiconductor syllogism: today's sold-out shortage is precisely what funds tomorrow's glut. Notably, even the bear's setup reads like a bull fact — sell-through visibility three years out is not a distressed company.

The bullish side is split between a thematic and a technical. aminvest, on June 25, framed MU as a stock that arguably lifts half the market — less a stock call than a systemic-weight claim, the idea that Micron has become load-bearing for the broader tape. The chart work comes from an unattributed technician in our log: on July 24 they had MU among "the strongest stocks" breaking out of its descending channel, and by August 11 they were watching an inside candle with drying volume and a "potential move to one thousand" — a call that hedges itself in the same breath, pairing a big upside marker with an explicit note that momentum is compressing.

The most honest timeline of conviction actually belongs to that unnamed chart-watcher, whose whipsaw tracks the price rather than leading it. On July 19 the tone was pure alarm — year-to-date gains had collapsed from over 300% to 190% ("Oh, no") — then five days later MU had "escaped the falling channel," and by August 11 the same voice was projecting a run at $1,000. That's sentiment flipping twice in under a month, which tells you how much of the bull case here is price-derived rather than thesis-derived. Coverage overall is thin, and the record doesn't yet show anyone changing their fundamental mind — Woo's June bear call stands unchallenged in the log, and the bulls haven't answered his capacity argument so much as ignored it.
