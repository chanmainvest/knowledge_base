## The debate

For an asset carrying 26 neutral ratings and zero calls on either side of the fence, GoGold's "debate" is almost a misnomer — the entire recorded record in this corpus sits on a single day, August 25, 2026, and every voice in it belongs to the company. That session produced both of the tagged-bullish quotes in the file, which tells you something about the shape of the argument: the bull case is currently being made by the only people with a microphone. An unnamed management speaker (logged simply as "?") and CEO Brad Langille carried the whole show, and the analyst community's 26 abstentions are, for now, the only counterweight.

The bull case, as management states it, is a cash-flow arithmetic pitch. At current prices, the unnamed speaker claimed the operation is "generating 80 to 90 million US after costs," producing "around 400 ounces of gold" every 10 days alongside a second figure the transcript truncates mid-number. The sharper version of the argument came right after: the company sits at "a close to 290 million" market value while generating "after tax about 60 million every 12 months," before even counting "just the interest on our bank balance." Run that math and you get the entire bull thesis in one line — roughly $60 million of after-tax earnings against a ~$290 million valuation. Management didn't need to say "undervalued"; the numbers were doing that work for them.

The closest thing to a bear note in the corpus also came from inside the building. Asked where costs could realistically come in above feasibility assumptions — the mill or the underground — the same speaker conceded it is "obviously in the underground," while pegging all-in costs at "around 20 25 26 $27, pretty similar" across scenarios. That's a modest but real admission: the cost-overrun risk in GoGold's story lives underground, not in the plant. No outside skeptic has put a bearish argument on the record here, so the neutral wall remains an absence of conviction rather than a rebuttal.

Langille, meanwhile, framed the story as purely event-driven around Los Ricos North — flagging as catalysts "a permit for Los Rico North or even the application" itself, and arguing "a lot of the catalysts are going to come out of" that project because it means "more than doubling of, you know, what we're doing." His claim that "everybody recognized there going to be a lot of value created when you get this thing up and running" is doing a lot of load-bearing — and notably, when he got to the timeline, the transcript trails off mid-sentence. That's fitting. Nobody has changed their mind on this asset, because there's no prior record to change from; the entire call history is one afternoon. The 26 neutrals aren't disputing the cash-flow math — they're declining to underwrite a permitting timeline management itself won't pin down. The debate resolves, one way or the other, whenever Los Ricos North permit news actually lands.
