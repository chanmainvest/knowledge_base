## The debate

The honest answer about "the debate" around Brazilian Rare Earths is that there barely is one yet. The tracker shows fifteen analysts on the file and every single one sitting at neutral/watch — zero bullish calls, zero bearish calls. What fills the record instead is one side of the argument: management's own August 24, 2026 conversation, in which the company laid out its development story and its demand thesis essentially unchallenged.

That bull case, as the CEO tells it, has three legs. On demand, he is categorical: "the Chinese will buy every last drop we produce" — a striking claim in a sector where Chinese buying power is usually framed as the competitive threat rather than the customer of last resort. On scale, the CEO points beyond the core asset, predicting "a number of satellite deposits" feeding in, adding "our production is going to be much bigger" and that the company will "produce for much longer." On execution, management laid out a sequence in the same conversation: an environmental impact assessment to be filed "early next year" that "will get the ball rolling," a prefeasibility study hoped for "late next year, early the year after," and then a jump "straight into full feasibility and final investment decision which we hope to have out by say 2029."

The bears, for their part, are silent in this record — but the absence of bullish conviction from fifteen watchers is itself a data point. The most obvious tension is time: even on management's own optimistic sequencing, a final investment decision sits years out, meaning a long stretch between the story and anything bankable. The other flashpoint is the processing question. Management argues the deposit sidesteps the industry's ugliest problem — "you're not leeching or processing... you're not ending up with radioactive tailings or anything like that" — which, if it holds up in the prefeasibility work, would be genuinely distinctive. As of now, though, it is a claim, not a study.

Nobody in this record changed their mind; there was no prior record to change from. The near-term tells management itself flagged — the EIA filing and the prefeasibility study — are the milestones most likely to force the neutral wall of fifteen to finally pick a side.
