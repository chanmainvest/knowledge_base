## The debate

For a pair usually framed as a bull-versus-bear call, USD/CNY in this corpus is strikingly argument-free on direction: nine tracked voices, zero bullish, zero bearish, all neutral. That doesn't mean there's nothing to argue about — it means the argument has moved underneath the headline rate, to questions of regime, policy intent, and what "devaluation" even means when the fixing pins the external price.

The most active contributor in the digest — unnamed in our records, and quoted in Cantonese transcripts that are partly garbled — actually sketched a full arc over roughly a month. On 23 July they floated a scenario, clearly hypothetical rather than a forecast, in which the renminbi halves against other currencies as part of a "full internal circulation" posture. By 28 July the tone had turned conditional-stability: if US–China negotiations kept progressing well, the rate would hold near its level rather than strengthen much further — though the same day they flagged a risk that large capital could rotate out of renminbi assets, with the transcript suggesting toward US assets. Then on 26 August came the closest thing to a directional view in the whole file: there is still room for "mild appreciation," but "the fastest phase of appreciation is already behind us," and the pair will now search for a balance between 6.6 and 6.98. That is a decelerating-strength call, not a bear call — and the shift from a 50%-depreciation tail scenario to a range-trade view inside five weeks is the single most notable change of mind in the digest.

Michael Howell, the liquidity-focused strategist, offers the structural counterframe rather than a level target. His 13 August read is that Beijing is running what he calls a "bifurcated exchange rate regime" — "maintaining a stable yuan US dollar externally while trying to devalue the yuan internally," an exercise in internal devaluation behind an externally stable facade [Michael Howell](../People/michael-howell.md).

That is where the real debate sits, and why the consensus tag is "watch." The unnamed speaker's range of 6.6–6.98 and Howell's bifurcated-regime thesis can both be true at once: the nominal external rate drifts mildly stronger inside a band, while the real, internal value of the currency erodes through policy. The digest is thin — one named voice, one unidentified one, no bulls or bears to referee — but the shape is coherent: nobody is betting on a break of the external rate, and the disagreement is over whether stability at the fixing masks the devaluation that matters.
