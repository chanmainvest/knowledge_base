## The debate

For an asset carrying eight neutral calls and zero on either side of the ledger, Amazon's page in late August 2026 still has a discernible argument — it just lives almost entirely in one number. On August 27, [高盛 (由作者引用)](../People/person-016.md) tagged a bullish stance on the AI capex supercycle: Amazon and four fellow megacaps are on track to spend roughly $75.5 billion on AI capital expenditure in 2026, up about 80% year-over-year, inside a Goldman forecast of some $5.3 trillion in cumulative big-tech capex between 2025 and 2030. Note where the stance is attached — to the capex supercycle, not to the equity. It is the closest thing to a thesis on this page, and it is a thesis about spending, not about Amazon's margins, AWS growth, or anything company-specific.

The rest of the timeline belongs to an unnamed voice, and it whipsaws. On August 8 the framing was bullish-technical: "maybe we can break out and get some sort of continuation and try to target up to that big round number up there around 300." By August 11, Amazon was "holding strong" and pushing toward its upper weekly implied move. Two days later the same feed turned skittish — "I wouldn't be shocked to see it come down into that area." That is less a change of mind than a tape-reader hedging in real time: the $300 target was never retracted, but neither was the downside scenario, and both were left hanging a week apart.

The most revealing entry is the historical analogy from August 24, delivered in Cantonese: back in the dot-com era, Amazon — "not yet started" — fell roughly 90%, yet went on to ride the internet to a price some 40 times above its pre-crash high. The "similarly" at the start of that quote suggests it was drafted to defend some other drawdown-suffering name by pointing at Amazon's history, and its function in the debate is clear either way. It is the long-horizon bull's comfort blanket: the claim that the eventual platform winner can absorb a brutal drawdown and still compound into multiples of its bubble peak. That it sits comfortably beside the anonymous August 13 downside musing is exactly the tension worth tracking — nobody here is bearish, but nobody has committed to the ticker either.

That is the honest shape of it. Goldman supplies the macro arithmetic, the unnamed trader supplies the price levels and the $300 mark, and the dot-com analogy supplies the moral. What is missing is anyone willing to flip the coin — no one on this page has taken $5.3 trillion of planned spending and built a bear case out of it, and no one has translated the capex boom into an earnings claim on Amazon itself. The 0/8/0 neutral tally isn't cowardice; as of late August 2026, it fairly describes a page where the thesis and the stock have not yet been introduced to each other.
