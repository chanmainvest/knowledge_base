## The debate

Call this page what it is: not a debate so much as a management monologue with the crowd watching quietly. The corpus carries twelve neutral stances on RDS.V and not a single bullish or bearish one, and every quote on record dates to the same session on August 26, 2026 — meaning there's no timeline of calls to reconstruct and nobody on record who changed their mind, because there's no prior record to change from. One of the two voices, moreover, isn't attributed in the transcript at all, which tells you how thin the independent coverage of this name is.

The case management lays out is a growth-and-momentum story. Matt (Radisson CEO) frames the resource work as deliberately incremental — "we'll endeavor to do resource updates on a step-by-step basis," with one already completed — while promising "a lot of results in the pipeline" and heavy drilling-related news flow ahead ([Matt (Radisson CEO)](../People/matt-radisson-ceo.md)). He leans on drill success as proof of execution, "we're hitting 80% of the time," and defends the current financing in ownership terms: "we're selling 15% of the business here," but not 100%, because this is "a growing resource." The program, the same session notes, is scheduled and budgeted all the way through the middle of 2027, with room to run longer.

The upside math comes from the second, unattributed speaker, and it's where the real argument lives. Against a market cap he puts at roughly C$600 million — "a lot of wood still to chop" in achieving full value — he suggests applying comparable-type valuations to a potential 3–4 million ounce O'Brien yields figures the current cap doesn't remotely reflect (the sentence trails off mid-transcript, but the direction is obvious). The strategic prescription is equally blunt: "just go for it with big stepouts" to find out whether the project is "1 2 3 4 or more million ounces." Note how much weight that size range carries — the valuation case is built on the upper end of a spread the company itself hasn't pinned down.

What's conspicuously absent is anyone arguing the other side. The closest thing to a counterweight is internal to the bull case itself: the 80% hit rate and the multi-million-ounce framing are management's own numbers, the resource is still being established through stepouts rather than established fact, and the 15% raise is dilution that management has to justify. A genuine debate would require someone independent stress-testing whether the stepouts actually deliver the ounces the valuation math assumes. As of the August 2026 record, no one in this corpus has — hence a neutral/watch consensus that reads less like conviction and more like a wait for the resource updates to settle the question.
