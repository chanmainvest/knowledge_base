What exists is one monologue — and it is worth parsing, because it is more directional than the "neutral" label suggests.

The speaker's argument has two stages. First, a near-term capitulation to the bond market: the Fed is "going to be forced probably to tight[en] monetary policy modestly and that's only to bend the knee of the bond market." Second — and this is the operative call for ZQ=F — the destination: "ultimately where this is all headed is a policy rate that is significantly lower than the lowest estimates on the OAS curve," with monetary policy "going to get very dovish in the next 18 months. Very dovish, substantially more dovish than what is currently" priced. (The transcript is rough — "doubbish" is clearly "dovish," and the curve reference is garbled — but the thrust is unambiguous.) Translated into a bill future that trades at 100 minus the three-month yield, that is a path call: modest tightening pressure at the very front, then a sustained rally in the contract as the front end collapses toward a terminal rate below even the lowest current curve estimate. The doubled emphasis — "Very dovish" — reads like conviction, not hedging.

The tension worth flagging is between the content and the filing. Three quotes that culminate in a sharply dovish 18-month destination got tagged neutral, presumably because the speaker concedes near-term tightening first; that looks like a filing artifact more than genuine ambivalence. What the record genuinely lacks is an opposing voice — nobody argues sticky inflation or a hawkish Fed keeps front-end yields elevated, and nobody revises a prior view because there are no prior views to revise. Until a second speaker enters the ledger, the "debate" on ZQ=F is one unattributed voice from August 20, 2026, leaning dovish beneath a neutral sticker.
