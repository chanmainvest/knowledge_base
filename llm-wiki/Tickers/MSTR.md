## The debate

The formal read on MSTR in this corpus is all caution and no conviction: eight neutral "watch" stances, zero bullish ratings, zero bearish ones. But the quotes tell a sharper story than the labels do, because the most active voice in the record — unnamed in our source files — isn't neutral at all in tone. On June 23, 2026, this speaker laid out the structural bear case: that Saylor is "sitting on… 800,000 some odd Bitcoin that he can't sell," and is therefore "selling what he can, which are his own shares." The same speaker went further the same day, predicting that "eventually the whole company collapses" and adding the line that defines this camp's framing: "regardless of what you think about Bitcoin, strategy's a disaster." That last clause matters — it's an attempt to sever the company's fate from Bitcoin's, arguing the wrapper is broken even if the asset is fine.

A week and a half later, that view got operationalized. On July 2 the same voice put on a relative-value call: "I'm betting that… if Stretch goes down" — the STRK preferred — "the common shares will go down by a greater percentage." In other words, the common is the most fragile slice of the capital stack and should underperform even Strategy's own preferreds in a drawdown. By August 6 the critique had a technical overlay: "From a hundred to about ninety, it's been this very, very tight ten-dollar range… It is bearish context," though the speaker immediately hedged — "sometimes you get…" — a caveat our transcript cuts off mid-word, so the resolution scenario (breakout or breakdown) is left unstated.

The closest thing to a bull-side or even agnostic-big-picture argument in the file comes from Andrew Kang, who on August 20 zoomed out rather than defended the thesis: "Micro Strategy, their TAM was Bitcoin. At their peak, they reached around a hundred billion dollars in market cap" [Andrew Kang](../People/andrew-kang.md). That's not a buy call — it's a ceiling framing, treating MSTR as a leveraged vehicle whose addressable market is the asset itself, capped by Bitcoin's own size. Notably, in a record this thin, that framing passes for the other side of the debate.

As for mind-changes: there are none on this record. The unnamed critic holds a consistent line from late June through early August, and Kang's observation sits outside the ratings entirely. The honest read is that the "debate" hasn't fully formed — one coherent bear case built across three dated calls, one neutral valuation remark, and a consensus layer too cautious to put a label on any of it. Coverage is thin, and the gap between the 8-neutral tally and the "strategy's a disaster" quote is the most telling detail in the file.
