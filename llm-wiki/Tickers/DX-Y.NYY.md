## The debate

The dollar debate in this record is unusually lopsided — and unusually short. There is essentially one voice in the corpus, and that voice sits on both sides of the argument at different points in 2026. Back on June 26, the case was the classic cyclical one: "Dollar's going up. Bond markets, yields are rising" — the familiar argument that higher yields pull capital toward dollar assets and lift the currency with them ([June 26, 2026 call](dx-y-nyy-calls)). Less than two months later, on August 21, the same speaker had flipped to the structural bear case: "I think there's a major diversification going on in terms of uh trade and capital flows, which over time is going to be dollar negative" ([August 21, 2026 call](dx-y-nyy-calls)).

That reversal is the most interesting thing about the debate as recorded here. It traces a shift in framework rather than just a change of mind — from a rates-differential story, where yields call the tune, to a flows story, where the slow reallocation of trade settlement and reserve capital away from the dollar grinds down the currency regardless of where yields sit. The August framing is notably hedged on timing ("over time"), which softens it into a medium-term drift call rather than a prediction of imminent downside. The June call, by contrast, was immediate and directional.

The honest caveat: this "debate" is thin. The consensus tag reads bearish, but the tally is one bear and one neutral, with no bull currently on record — the June bull case has no surviving defender in the corpus. The speakers behind each call aren't identified in the digest, so it's impossible to say whether the June-to-August flip represents one analyst genuinely changing their mind or two different voices. What the record does show plainly is the shape of the argument: bears anchor on structural diversification of trade and capital flows; bulls, at least as of mid-2026, anchored on rising yields. The bear side currently holds the field largely by default.
