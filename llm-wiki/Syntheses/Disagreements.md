# Synthesis: Disagreements

Topics where **different people** currently hold opposite stances (each person's latest directional call on the topic). Sorted by how many voices are in the fight.

The deepest fault line in this corpus runs through AI and semiconductors, and it is not really an argument about the technology — it is an argument about what is financing it. The bulls are reading price and momentum: Jeff Snider notes that "US stocks are surging, tech is soaring, semiconductors are vertical" ([link](../People/jeff-snider.md)), Val Zlatev says the names in question are "4 to 5x as we speak. They'll go more" ([link](../People/val-zlatev.md)), Mike Alfred sees the market still underpricing a 5-to-15-year opportunity ([link](../People/mike-alfred.md)), and Raoul Pal finds comfort in the build-out's "inability for a lot of this infrastructure to go bust" ([link](../People/raoul-pal.md)). The bears think that is circular reasoning dressed as analysis. Ed Zitron points to Broadcom and OpenAI's pledge of 10 gigawatts of data centers and concludes the bulls are mistaking "a demand for speculative debt" for demand ([link](../People/ed-zitron.md)); Jim Chanos frames it as a market with "opportunities on both the long and the short side," with his short book aimed at "inherently unprofitable business models" attached to the theme ([link](../People/jim-chanos.md)); and aminvest goes furthest of all, arguing that many AI-related and unrelated companies alike will simply fail ([link](../People/aminvest.md)).

Bitcoin is the cleanest binary in the corpus. Mike Alfred calls a straight long the only position he has publicly espoused ([link](../People/mike-alfred.md)); Raoul Pal sketches a "super abundance" world in which Bitcoin becomes genuinely important ([link](../People/raoul-pal.md)); Brent Johnson predicts stablecoins are "a revolution that many just don't yet understand" ([link](../People/brent-johnson.md)); and Erik Voorhees champions DeFi lending markets as "really cool" ([link](../People/erik-voorhees.md)). Against them, George Noble delivers the corpus's most quotable put-down — "Bitcoin is the Facebook of speculative assets" headed "into the sunset" ([link](../People/george-noble.md)) — while Chanos hunts the unprofitable models grafted onto crypto rails ([link](../People/jim-chanos.md)).

The broad-market and rates debate maps onto a cycle-timing argument. Chris Semenuk says "we're entering the early stages of the cycle" and credits tariffs with driving "incremental capital investment back to the US" ([link](../People/chris-semenuk.md)); David Woo notes he made money this year being long oil ([link](../People/david-woo.md)). On the other side sits a credit-and-economy camp: Jeff Snider's blunt "It is the economy, stupid. It is the single biggest lesson from Starmer's fall" ([link](../People/jeff-snider.md)), his insistence that the TIPS market is "perfectly clear" about something equities ignore ([link](../People/jeff-snider.md)), aminvest tracking deteriorating corporate default ratios ([link](../People/aminvest.md)), and 何啟聰 warning that a leveraged wobble — Hynix shares plunging on tax-talk and dragging the KOSPI 200 down a tenth — could leave the Fed forced into bolder hikes next year ([link](../People/person-002.md)). Noble again supplies the sharpest edges: he doesn't just expect the market to "fall out of bed," he "actually want[s] it to," and dislikes homebuilders if rates rise ([link](../People/george-noble.md)).

Just as telling is where the corpus doesn't fight. Crude is close to a one-way call: Noble ([link](../People/george-noble.md)), JD citing a stochastics reading of nine and RSI 29 ([link](../People/jd.md)), Tony "a buyer down here" ([link](../People/tony.md)), Patrick Ceresna looking for a settle "back above 80... maybe even as high as 90" ([link](../People/patrick-ceresna.md)), Brent Johnson expecting gap fills "at least up to 85" ([link](../People/brent-johnson.md)), and Erik Townsend eyeing a chart gap near "84 and a half" ([link](../People/erik-townsend.md)). Gold is near-consensus too — Snider's "If gold is money, it should act like money. And now it finally can" ([link](../People/jeff-snider.md)), Townsend's bounce toward 4,400 ([link](../People/erik-townsend.md)), Michael Howell's simple "gold is probably going up" ([link](../People/michael-howell.md)) — with Jeffrey Christian the notable dissenter: a break below 4,100 targets "around $3,800" ([link](../People/jeffrey-christian.md)), and he sees downside specifically in platinum and palladium ([link](../People/jeffrey-christian.md)).

The remaining genuine geographic divide is China, where Jeff's bull-steepening of ultra-low Chinese rates ([link](../People/jeff.md)) and Patrick Boyle's open-source case built on Jipu and DeepSeek ([link](../People/patrick-boyle.md)) collide with 高天佑's tape-watching — AI leaders down more than 10%, Seoul down 9.99%, Hong Kong at a one-year low ([link](../People/person-015.md)). And the whole debate has a neat microcosm in SpaceX: JD's scenario of a $10 trillion market cap ([link](../People/jd.md)) against Noble's shrug that "you may be able to short SpaceX at 250. I have no idea" ([link](../People/george-noble.md)) and Zitron noting it has been "trundling" ([link](../People/ed-zitron.md)). The tell about what this corpus is really arguing over, though, may be Snider himself — simultaneously the man calling semis "vertical" and the man shouting "It is the economy, stupid" — which suggests the true cleavage here is less bull-versus-bear than leveraged-narrative-versus-hard-asset.
