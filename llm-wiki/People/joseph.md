Joseph Wang is the markets commentator known across macro-finance circles as "Fed Guy," a moniker earned the hard way: he spent years as a senior trader on the Markets Desk — the Open Market Trading Desk at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York — where he helped execute the Fed's Treasury and quantitative easing operations. That insider pedigree is the spine of his public work. He wrote *Central Banking 101* in 2021, runs the Fed Guy blog and newsletter on Fed policy, reserves, liquidity, and money markets, and now splits his time between appearing as a podcast guest and hosting his own show. When Wang talks about plumbing — reserves, repo, the desk's mechanics — he's speaking with unusual authority; when he ranges into trade and geopolitics, he's a macro tourist like the rest of us, and the record below shows both modes.

The captured record here is thin and narrow — essentially one week in June 2026 — so it is a snapshot rather than a trajectory, and no honest claim about how his views have "moved over time" can be made from it. What the snapshot shows is a decidedly bearish tilt: eleven bearish stances against three bullish and four neutral across the tracked items. His strongest call is blunt. On his June 2026 FOMC Debrief he said the pieces "all point to me to a sustained and meaningful decline in risk assets" ([June 2026 FOMC Debrief](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Njd_JuRuEcI)) — no hedging there. Around it, on Markets Weekly dated June 20, 2026, he flagged the collapse in crude — "if you look at spot oil prices, they've absolutely plummeted... you can see spot Brent all the way down" — and read it as "a large, large disinflationary wave" ([Markets Weekly June 20, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80)). For a rates-desk veteran, leading with the disinflation channel rather than the geopolitical one is telling.

The same episode sketches his geopolitical frame, and it is more mixed than the risk-asset call. He argued the post-Iran-war "ceasefire that is probably going to hold" and speculated the conflict might mark "the twilight of the influence" of the region's hardliners — a constructive read ([Markets Weekly June 20, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80)). On trade he is gloomier and more animated: he sees "the Chinese moving up the value chain and threatening a wider range of industries in the West," notes that Beijing manages the currency so that it is "structurally undervalued," and observes that Germany's export decline has been a big drag on its growth — with "Euroland" now weighing its own tariff wall in response ([Markets Weekly June 20, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80)). The one place he stays deliberately neutral is rates itself: despite the disinflation impulse, he simply observes that markets are "still pricing in an ongoing mini rate hiking cycle" ([Markets Weekly June 20, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80)).

So the portrait that survives this record is of a former desk trader turned public commentator whose June 2026 posture was bearish on risk assets, oil, and the Western trade position, grudgingly impressed by China's industrial ascent, and noncommittal on the rate path. Whether that is a durable worldview or one week's reading of the tape, the corpus as it stands cannot say — more appearances, especially from earlier periods, would be needed before calling him a perma-bear.
