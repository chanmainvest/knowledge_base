Joseph Wang — known across the macro world as "Fed Guy" — is one of the few commentators on the Federal Reserve beat who has actually operated the machinery he now dissects. A former senior trader on the Markets Desk at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, he helped execute Treasury and QE operations before distilling that experience into *Central Banking 101* (2021) and the Fed Guy blog and newsletter, where he writes on Fed policy, reserves, liquidity, and money markets. He appears regularly as a guest on macro-finance podcasts and hosts his own recurring commentary, including a "Markets Weekly" format.

The record in this corpus is small but strikingly lopsided: of eighteen coded stances, eleven are bearish, three bullish, and four neutral — and they all cluster inside a single week in June 2026. That week deserves to be taken at face value, because it catches him at his most emphatic. On the [June 2026 FOMC Debrief](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Njd_JuRuEcI) he argued the evidence points to "a sustained and meaningful decline in risk assets." Three days later, on [Markets Weekly June 20, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80), he supplied the mechanism: spot Brent had "absolutely plummeted" after the Iran ceasefire, and he read that collapse as a "large disinflationary wave" washing over markets — bearish oil, and by extension bearish the risk complex that had been riding it.

What stands out is that his bearishness runs deeper than an oil trade. On the same June 20 episode he framed the tariff-fragmentation story end to end: Euroland weighing its own tariff wall, German export decline already weighing on growth, and China "moving up the value chain and threatening a wider range of industries in the West" while managing a currency that, by some estimates, is kept structurally undervalued ([Markets Weekly June 20, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80)). Notably, he coded the Chinese industrial ascent as bullish in its own right — the threat to the West is the flip side of the same observation, and he didn't blur the two.

The nuance matters, though, because he was not uniformly gloomy. He judged the Iran ceasefire "probably going to hold," and on rates he was neutral, flagging exactly the kind of market-versus-narrative gap his desk background primes him to spot: markets were "still pricing in an ongoing mini rate hiking cycle" even as oil slumped ([Markets Weekly June 20, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fI3fVnAqi80)). The honest caveat is that the coverage here amounts to one interview appearance and one solo Markets Weekly, both in mid-June 2026 — a snapshot, not an arc. It shows a clearly bearish posture with a coherent structural argument behind it; whether that posture is durable or week-specific is something this record cannot yet answer.
