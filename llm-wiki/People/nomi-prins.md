# Nomi Prins

Nomi Prins is the rare commentator whose authority comes from having been inside the machine she now critiques — a former Wall Street managing director who reinvented herself as one of the sharpest chroniclers of central bank power, best known publicly for books like *Collusion* and *All the Presidents' Bankers*. In this corpus, though, her record is unusually concentrated: every entry traces to a single interview dated August 23, 2026, titled "Gold To $6,000, Silver Buying Opportunity, Copper To $7" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)). That means what follows is a snapshot, not a track record — and it should be read as one.

The August interview lays out a structural gold thesis rather than a simple price punt. Her core argument is that central banks around the world "are turning to gold to diversify not just against the dollar" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)), and that the buying has room to run — she estimates the typical bank still has "about 5% more gold to buy," effectively doubling its allocation to return to historic reserve levels ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)). Beneath that sits a supply argument: the market remains "very close to a deficit for gold based on current demand," before any demand growth at all ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)), plus a claimed "distortion, a dislocation" between paper and physical pricing in gold and silver ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)). On levels, she sees roughly $6,000 "around the turn of the year" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)) and "probably 8,000 next year," with fresh forecasts promised in January ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)). Her logic for ignoring pullbacks is that central bank buyers simply don't stop — "It didn't stop buying at 5,500. It's not stopping at 4,500. It didn't stop last year at 3,500" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)). The title's "Copper To $7" earns a mention, but the transcript excerpt offers no real support for it beyond the headline ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)) — a thin leg of the thesis.

On rates, she called for the Fed to "hold rates in September" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)), and framed some fiscal or monetary maneuver to strip debt off the system as "the first of many such moves which will grow in magnitude" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)) — the intervention theme that has defined her career-long argument that official liquidity, not fundamentals, sets the tape. Whether her September hold call proved right isn't captured here; the corpus doesn't say.

As for movement over time: there is none visible in this digest. One date, one source, and every stance coded neutral — eleven of eleven ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Hnok1i10K0)). That's worth flagging plainly. The neutral coding arguably undersells the material, since $6,000-then-$8,000 gold is a directional call by any plain reading, but the transcript excerpts themselves are explanatory in tone — she's describing a mechanism, not ringing a bell. A proper portrait of how her views have evolved will need earlier and later appearances; this page currently rests on one long August afternoon.
