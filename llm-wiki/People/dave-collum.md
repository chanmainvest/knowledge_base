Dave Collum is an organic chemist by trade and a macro pessimist by avocation — a longtime Cornell University professor who built a second career as a finance commentator, best known for his lengthy and irreverent annual "Year in Review" essays, originally published on Peak Prosperity and widely passed around sites like Zero Hedge. His framing is skeptical and Austrian-economics-influenced: bubbles, debt, central bank policy, gold, and the fragility of fiat currency are his recurring beats, and he has been a regular guest on finance podcasts such as MacroVoices to make that case.

The clearest documented snapshot of his current thinking comes from an August 13, 2026 interview, and it is a stacked-bubble argument: "we have an AI bubble on top of a real estate bubble on top of a general equity bubble on top of... private credit," in his telling ([Dave Collum: The AI Bubble Will Break Down VERY Quickly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ku8l-I6IU1A)). Within that stack, he singles out AI as the likely first domino — "the AI bubble could be the one that you see first" — and argues the boom could unravel quickly, pointing to AI companies raising hundreds of billions of dollars amid pervasive optimism ([Dave Collum: The AI Bubble Will Break Down VERY Quickly](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ku8l-I6IU1A)). That is his strongest recent call: not a general crash forecast, but a specific claim about which layer of the froth gives way first.

That's the characteristic Collum move — maximalist-sounding theater on metals, with the actual stated forecast more measured.
