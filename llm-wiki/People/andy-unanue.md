# Andy Unanue

**Role:** Founder and Managing Partner

Andy Unanue sits at an unusual intersection for an investor: he came up inside a family food business — he served as chief operating officer of Goya Foods before leaving to become a founder and managing partner on the private equity side, a transition that frames almost everything he says about the sector. That operator's résumé is precisely why his commentary carries weight in conversations about the so-called family business opportunity, a theme he addressed head-on in an appearance billed around the "$1.4 Trillion Family Business Opportunity." He speaks less like a trader calling direction and more like someone who has run the manufacturing lines himself.

The substance of what he argues is craft-level and structural rather than directional. Discussing where value and defensibility actually live in consumer staples, he drew a sharp line between the two halves of the aisle: "on the food side, there is more science and more manufacturing internal know-how versus on the beverage side, which seems to be more replicable" ([Ex-Goya COO on the $1.4 Trillion Family Business Opportunit…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=suivZa95fkM)). It's a demarcation with real investment implications — the implication being that food businesses carry harder-to-copy operational moats, while beverage brands are easier to knock off. His other recurring theme is generational: "that authentic, right, food or beverage is extremely important to your generation and and all future generations" ([Ex-Goya COO on the $1.4 Trillion Family Business Opportunit…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=suivZa95fkM)) — authenticity, in his telling, is not a marketing flourish but the durable asset that keeps consumers loyal across cohorts.
