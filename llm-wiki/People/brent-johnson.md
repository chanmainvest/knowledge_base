Brent Johnson is an American investor and macro commentator who spent roughly a decade as a financial advisor at Credit Suisse before founding Santiago Capital, a boutique firm organized around capital preservation and gold ownership. He made his name with the Dollar Milkshake Theory — the argument that global dollar shortages would drive the greenback *up* while other currencies and markets broke around it — and he has pounded that table through a relentless run of podcast appearances across the 2010s and 2020s. One honest caveat up front: the corpus behind this page is essentially two passes over a single conversation, MacroVoices #537, "There's No Turning Back," captured on June 18 and 19, 2026. So this is a portrait of Johnson at one moment, not a career-long track record ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)).

That moment is still revealing, because June 2026 is exactly when his thesis is at its most contrarian. "If the dollar is ever going to die, it's going to die of strength and it's not going to die because it got inflated away and went to zero," he said on June 19 — a direct rebuke to the debasement trade that dominates gold-friendly circles ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). He extended the same logic to stablecoins, calling them "a revolution that many just don't yet understand" and pointing out that 99% of all outstanding stablecoins in the world today are US dollar stablecoins — in his telling, fresh plumbing for dollar hegemony rather than a threat to it ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). He also reminded listeners that "part of the milkshake initially was saying interest rates would rise and treasury bonds would fall," and warned that historically, "75% of the time, whenever we've gotten outside of that band, within a very short period of time, sovereigns around the world went into distress" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)).

His strongest tactical calls in this window are specific. On the Iran ceasefire he was blunt: "a very very tenuous deal at best. And as I said at the very beginning, I don't expect it to last," adding it was "very unlikely" that the United States, Iran, or Israel lives up to its side, and "I don't think it's over" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). Notably, this lifelong gold advocate was the one flagging gold's fragility — "from the moment the war broke out in Iran to the moment that it looked like we were getting this deal signed last week, gold fell 20%" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). On oil, he openly walked back his own doomsday view of "$150 to $200 by year end," reframing the setup as a gap-fill to at least $85 while still saying "I wouldn't be surprised if we're back over 100 before the year is out" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). Rounding out the board: national defense as "probably going to be the biggest investment opportunity any of us have ever seen," and a potential "food cascade later this year or next year" via knock-on effects on fertilizers, chemicals, and disrupted crop inputs ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)).

The shape of the man's debate style is best read from his own tally — 18 bullish, 16 bearish, 34 neutral across tracked statements — which is the profile of a scenario-builder, not a permabull or permabear, despite the gold-firm letterhead. His big-picture framing is stark but characteristically double-edged: he'd sooner bet on "the fall of the republic and the rise of the empire than the fall of the empire and some new power takeover" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). He also flagged a structural shift worth tracking — commodity prices have begun to diverge based on where they're traded or where they're used ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). A final note for anyone reading the stance-change logs: the apparent flips between June 18 and 19 are artifacts of the same episode captured twice, not a genuine reversal — the only real movement on record here is the oil walk-back, and it happened inside the interview itself.
