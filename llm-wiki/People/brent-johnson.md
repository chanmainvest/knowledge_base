Brent Johnson is an American investor and macro commentator who spent roughly a decade as a financial advisor at Credit Suisse before founding Santiago Capital, a boutique firm built around capital preservation and gold ownership. He made his name in macro circles as the author of the Dollar Milkshake Theory — the contrarian bet that in a world drowning in debt and rival currencies, capital keeps flooding *into* the dollar rather than out of it — popularized through frequent podcast appearances across the 2010s and 2020s. He is also a longtime, outspoken gold advocate, which makes his mid-2026 posture genuinely interesting reading.

The milkshake machinery was on full display on MacroVoices episode #537, pointedly titled "There's No Turning Back" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.macrovoices.com/1535-macrovoices-537-brent-johnson-there-s-no-turning-back)). Johnson reprised the original call — that "part of the milkshake initially was saying interest rates would rise and treasury bonds would fall" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)) — and pushed the thesis further, arguing that "if the dollar is ever going to die, it's going to die of strength," not by being inflated to zero ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). His newest wrinkle is stablecoins, which he calls "a revolution that many just don't yet understand" and a force that will further solidify dollar dominance ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)), noting that 99% of all outstanding stablecoins today are US-dollar stablecoins ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). He keeps a darker statistic stapled to the bull case: 75% of the time the dollar has exited a certain historical band, sovereigns worldwide tipped into distress shortly after ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)).

On geopolitics he was distinctly gloomier than on the dollar. He dismissed the freshly signed Iran deal as "a very very tenuous deal at best... I don't expect it to last," predicted neither the US, Iran nor Israel would honor its side, and expects "more wars, more conflict, more non-ooperation" ahead ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). He framed America's path not as hegemonic collapse but as "the fall of the republic and the rise of the empire," saying he has a hard time seeing the US surrender primacy regardless of who sits in the White House ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.macrovoices.com/1535-macrovoices-537-brent-johnson-there-s-no-turning-back)) — and called national defense "probably going to be the biggest investment opportunity any of us have ever seen" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). He also flagged a potential "food cascade" late this year or next, with the larger trade in disrupted fertilizers and crop inputs ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)).

The notable tension: the man who built his brand on gold spent much of the episode talking it down tactically, pointing out gold fell 20% from the outbreak of the Iran war to the appearance of a deal, and scoffing at the $6,000–$8,000 gold and $150–$200 oil choruses ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)) — including walking back his own prior call for $150–$200 oil by year-end, conceding the market misread storage as scarce when "the opposite is true" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There’s No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). A caveat on the record itself: nearly everything documented here flows from that single June 2026 interview, so the apparent day-over-day "flips" in the data reflect how the transcript was cut, not real reversals — and his stance mix runs heavily neutral (36 of 70 points), a fitting profile for a dollar bull confident in the destination but hedged on the timing.
