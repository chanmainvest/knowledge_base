Steve Van Metre is a U.S.-based investment adviser and bond-market commentator whose YouTube channel is devoted to the Treasury market, the dollar system, and Federal Reserve policy. He sits squarely in the "eurodollar" school popularized by Jeff Snider — the view that global dollar liquidity, not Fed policy alone, drives markets — and he is a recurring guest on Eurodollar University and on podcasts hosted by Snider and Emil Kalinowski, where he works through yield curves, debt issuance, and money markets. He is best known for a long-running bullish Treasury thesis, the widely followed "TLT trade" (long call options on the iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF), premised on falling long-term yields as recession and dollar-shortage dynamics unfold.

The record in this corpus captures him mid-thesis, across two Eurodollar University appearances a week apart in June 2026. Yet his headline price call is deflationary, not inflationary — oil at $50 a barrel, but "not because there's just a sudden flood of supply in the market. It's the demand destruction, the coming rec[ession]" — paired with a warning that "inflation is going to just come right down isn't so likely at least in the short-term" ([Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E)). The shape of the call is a near-term squeeze giving way to a demand-driven collapse, which is the recession thesis his bond trade depends on.

A week later he sharpened the macro side. Falling yields plus impotent central banks is exactly the environment the TLT trade is built for.
