Steve Van Metre is a U.S.-based investment adviser and bond-market commentator whose YouTube channel picks apart the Treasury market, the dollar system, and Federal Reserve policy. He sits firmly in the "eurodollar" school built around Jeff Snider — the argument that global dollar liquidity conditions, not the Fed's policy lever alone, are what actually move markets — and he is a recurring guest on Eurodollar University and on podcasts hosted by Snider and Emil Kalinowski, with yield curves, Treasury issuance, money markets, and the macro cycle as his standing beats.

His signature position is a long-running bullish Treasury thesis, expressed most concretely in the widely followed "TLT trade": long call options on the iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF, premised on long-term yields falling hard as recession and dollar-shortage dynamics play out. That trade is his worldview in a single position — a bond rally he expects not because policy succeeds, but because the system is breaking.

The interview record here is compressed into one month — three Eurodollar University appearances on June 14, 21, and 27, 2026 — so there is no documented drift in his views to chart over time; what it shows instead is a coherent, and heavily bearish, through-line: nine bearish and ten neutral marks against just two bullish. On June 14, in [Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E), he ran a deliberately two-sided oil view: a supply shortfall he called "the big shoe to drop over the next couple of months" — the same episode carrying both of his bullish tags, on oil tightness and a China/Hong Kong equities mark — alongside a warning that India could be "without sufficient diesel for several weeks if not months further on." But the bearish half of the same conversation won: he doubted inflation would "just come right down" in the short term, and put the destination for crude at $50 a barrel — not from a supply flood, but "demand destruction, the coming rec[ession]."

A week later, on [Swiss Bond Market Just Gave A Dire Warning to the World](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2LzapdRuT4), he pushed back directly on surface-level calm: "the shock is growing, it's real," against people who look around, see "spending's up, market's up," and conclude it can't be happening. Asked whether rates keep dropping and go negative, his answer was unqualified — "Absolutely. Will central bankers panic? Absolutely. Is it going to work? I don't think it's going to." Note how neatly that fits the TLT trade: falling yields as confirmation of a liquidity shock, not a policy victory.

By June 27, on [Bitcoin And BlackRock Just Did Something VERY Strange](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=biyCX7UYAfQ), he was mapping the mechanics of the bust: in a recession "they need dollars... they'll take them from anywhere they can get them"; when credit dries up "there's no money for payroll"; private credit has a "déjà vu" feel, with Q3 redemption projections "even higher than they are now" at the BlackRock/Blackstone/Blue Owl/Apollo end of the complex. Bitcoin's slide he reads as a liquidity tell — "nobody's going to gate you if you want to sell it," even if a MicroStrategy-scale seller would plunge the price, because no one closes the door. One honest caveat: a portrait built on a single month of tape can't establish trajectory. Within that month, though, man and trade align completely — the economy is what he's bearish on; the long bond is how he expresses it.
