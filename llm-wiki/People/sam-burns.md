# Sam Burns

**Role:** Chief Market Strategist

Sam Burns is a Chief Market Strategist whose on-the-record footprint in this corpus comes almost entirely from a single appearance: the June 30, 2026 interview titled [What Would End Bull Market Overnight? Strategist Reveals Tr…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mVsHCgJcVc). That format matters for reading him — he was invited to map out kill-scenarios for a bull market, and what he delivered was less a set of directional calls than a transmission-mechanism lecture. All eight logged stances are neutral, which is less a sign of fence-sitting than of a strategist who prefers explaining *how* money moves to predicting *where* it goes.

His most distinctive argument concerns the AI trade's insulation from rates. He identified tech and its adjacent sectors — explicitly including industrials "somehow tied to AI" — as "the big driver of equities" in this cycle ([What Would End Bull Market Overnight? Strategist Reveals Tr…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mVsHCgJcVc)). Then he went a step further and detached that trade from the Fed: "Rates going up 50 basis points is just not going to change much for, you know, AI related spending," because most of that spending "is equity financed one way or another" ([What Would End Bull Market Overnight? Strategist Reveals Tr…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mVsHCgJcVc)). That's the sharpest analytical claim in the record — it argues the usual bear-case lever for breaking an AI-led bull market (higher rates) doesn't actually bite where the money is being spent.

The same rate-sensitivity framework runs through his commodity views, just inverted. Gold, he noted, is sensitive to rates and real rates in particular — "if those are going to go stay higher, you know, where they are go up, then gold typically would not benefit from that" ([What Would End Bull Market Overnight? Strategist Reveals Tr…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mVsHCgJcVc)). On oil he was simply skeptical of a spike: "It's going to take a lot for oil to go back to the 110 where it was, you know, a few months ago" ([What Would End Bull Market Overnight? Strategist Reveals Tr…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mVsHCgJcVc)). The through-line is coherent — equity-funded AI capex shrugs off rates, rate-sensitive gold suffers if rates hold, and oil needs an exogenous shock — but it's a map of dependencies, not a portfolio recommendation.

As for how his views have moved over time: the record simply can't say. Every logged item carries the same date and the same source, so there is no before-and-after to chart, no stance that flipped or firmed. The coverage here is thin — one interview, no dated calls, no bullish or bearish positions on file at all. What the corpus does establish is a strategist who thinks in terms of financing structure and rate transmission rather than index targets; what it doesn't establish is whether he acts on that framework with conviction. Anyone building out this page will need more dated appearances before any evolution — or any verdict — can honestly be written.
