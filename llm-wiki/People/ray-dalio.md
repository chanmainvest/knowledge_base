# Ray Dalio

**Role:** Investor, author, and founder of Bridgewater Associates; served as co-chief investment officer until stepping back in 2022

Ray Dalio is the founder of Bridgewater Associates, the macro-trading firm he built into one of the largest hedge funds in the world, and he ran its investment engine as co-chief investment officer until stepping back from that role in 2022. He is as much an author and systematizer as a money manager — the "Principles" brand and his long-debt-cycle framework are the lens through which he reads everything, and the lens through which this wiki tracks him.

The single dated entry in the corpus captures that framework in its late-stage form. As of August 24, 2026, his stance on rates, bonds and the dollar is logged as neutral, but the substance of the argument is anything but calm: the claim is that "the government knows UST demand is becoming a problem" and is "quietly trying to control the bond market with buybacks" — Treasury-market management dressed up as routine operations ([Ray Dalio Just Issued His Most Terrifying Debt Warning Yet.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oshjZjc5FJI)). There is a telling gap between the packaging and the substance here. The video's title promises his "most terrifying debt warning yet," yet the recorded position is neutral — which reads less like a settled view than a man describing a slow fiscal squeeze he thinks is structural, without betting on a catalyst or a date. That hedged alarm is characteristic Dalio: the debt-cycle thesis has been his drumbeat for years, and the 2026 entry suggests he sees the mechanism (weak Treasury demand, backstop buying) arriving on schedule, but stops short of a directional call.

It should be said plainly: this corpus holds only one dated entry for him, so there is no documented evolution to narrate — no earlier bull or bear calls on record here to chart a turn against. What the snapshot does show is the shape of his late-career public persona: less a trader making timing calls than a historian of monetary regimes warning that the buyers of last resort are becoming the buyers of first resort.
