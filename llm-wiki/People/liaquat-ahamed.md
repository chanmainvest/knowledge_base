Born in Kenya to a South Asian family and educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, and Columbia University, he began at the World Bank before spending roughly two decades managing fixed-income portfolios in New York and London, including as a partner at Fischer Francis Trees & Watts. That practitioner's résumé gives *Lords of Finance: The Bankers Who Broke the World* (2009) — his account of the interwar central bankers whose monetary policy missteps helped cause the Great Depression — its particular bite; it won the 2010 Pulitzer Prize for History.
