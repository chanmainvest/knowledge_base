# JD

JD is a macro trader and recurring guest on The Macro Dirt Podcast whose 2026 record is built around one aggressive twin trade — long bonds across the curve, short the dollar — wrapped around the most emphatic SpaceX call in this corpus. On the June 17 episode, [Nobody Owns Bonds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-mvJ6poUk4), he laid it out plainly: "I like bonds. I like the front end of the yield curve. I like the back end… I like the dollar short," with the dollar thesis resting on the expectation that Warsh at the Fed would want to cut rates. The same day he called SpaceX the one stock in the S&P's top ten that "could 5x from here," floating a "middle finger chart formation" in which SpaceX goes parabolic "and everything else becomes a funding trade."

Six days later, on June 23's conversation with [George Noble On Stocks, Gold, Oil, and AI @nobull-markettal…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LDrOV4i0EU4), the preference had hardened into a bet — this is the flip the record logs on rates, bonds and the dollar. He was now explicitly looking for the stock–bond correlation to turn negative, and he named the catalyst: "a sharp decline in stocks like maybe even a crash." The dollar's move he dismissed as "a false breakout… I'm very bearish," and his positioning line was the bluntest of the run — "I'm putting it all in bonds" — though he also allowed he was "keeping Brazil," an odd hold for a man calling a crash. On the index he was unambiguous: with the S&P at an all-time high and "now 22% semiconductors," he was "very bearish on the index." Only SpaceX escaped the gloom, and he doubled down within a week: "if there was any stock that had a chance of going to a 10 trillion market cap, it would be SpaceX. And I still believe that."

By [Bond Market Calm](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o2bF4sPqBdQ) on August 27, the crash posture had visibly cooled. The macro read turned descriptive — "the data sucks. We're in a slowdown. Inflation is coming down. There's no reason to hike rates" — and his advice to would-be bond shorts was mockery: "You're going to pick a fight with the US Treasury and the Fed. You think you're Soros. You think you're going to break the Bank of England." On the dollar specifically he conceded it "looks like it's setting up for a bounce here… I am flat here" — a striking retreat from "very bearish" ten weeks earlier, whatever the tagged stance still says. He also admitted he had "a tough time being short" AI and semiconductors "because the fundamentals are really, really good," and offered nothing firmer on bitcoin than that it "probably goes to 100 in the short term," direction unstated.

The through-line is SpaceX, not the macro. The bond-and-dollar trade went from all-in to flat inside a single summer; the SpaceX conviction never wavered, scaling from a 5x scenario to a $10 trillion thought experiment without a hint of hedging. Read together, the record shows a trader quick to size up a macro story and quicker still to stand down when it stops cooperating — discipline or a short conviction half-life, depending on how much weight you give the June crash talk.
