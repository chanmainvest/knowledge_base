Jeff (listed in some records as "Jeoff") is a macro commentator whose footprint in this corpus rests on a single appearance — a June 14, 2026 guest spot on Eurodollar University, a show closely associated with the bearish, Eurodollar-system school of macro analysis. The profile is thin by design: one interview, one day, one tightly argued thesis. But within that narrow window he stakes out an unambiguous position, with six of his eight tagged statements bearish and none bullish, spanning oil prices, broad macro conditions, and China ([Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E)).

His strongest and most specific call is a downside target on crude. With the Iran risk premium fading, he argues the market's "fundamental value is what we saw in December, not what we had, you know, in the middle of April" — and that the arithmetic of collapsing demand leads ultimately to $50-a-barrel oil ([Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E)). The evidence he leans on hardest is Chinese demand destruction: physical crude imports to China were "down by 3% in March, 20% in April, and 29% in May," a deterioration steep enough that he treats it as the defining data point of the cycle ([Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E)). Layered on top is a macro argument that cuts against consensus: energy shocks "are not inflationary. They end up destroying demand, especially for an economy that's weak to begin with... They don't end in inflation" — a direct rebuttal to the supply-shock-driven inflation narrative ([Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E)).

What keeps him from being a pure perma-bear on tape is tactical flexibility. He expects front-end oil prices "to come down and then bounce," and tells viewers the first tell will be watching "WTI futures to see how the curve reshapes in this next stage" rather than the spot price itself ([Now That Iran Is Done, The Real Oil Crisis Begins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTh7cPI8z3E)). That is a trader's concession inside a structural bear case — direction down, path choppy. Because the entire record here dates from a single June 2026 interview, there is no basis to trace how his views have evolved; whether the $50 call and the energy-shocks-aren't-inflationary thesis hardened or softened afterward is simply not documented in this corpus.
