# Darius Dale (guest)

**solo author/host (1x)**

**Role:** Founder and CEO of 42 Macro

Darius Dale is the founder and CEO of 42 Macro, and on Forward Guidance he plays the role of the framework-driven macro generalist — someone more comfortable ranking assets against each other than planting a flag on any single one. His episode "The Growth Strategy Trapping The Fed," from August 12, 2026, is the clearest snapshot of that style in this corpus, and it's also one of the appearances logged as a solo hosting/authoring turn rather than a straight interview ([The Growth Strategy Trapping The Fed | Darius Dale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvMTOKRR0SE)).

His headline call on that episode was deliberately cross-asset: "it's a great short to medium-term outlook for stocks, gold, bitcoin, and commodities, not so much for the dollar, uh, and bonds" ([The Growth Strategy Trapping The Fed | Darius Dale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvMTOKRR0SE)). That's a risk-on tilt with two explicit exclusions — a weak dollar and a bond market he isn't interested in owning. But the more interesting part is the tension he builds into the same appearance: alongside the constructive tape call, he flagged "bubble-like conditions in the equity market" and "issues in the bond market associated with all that extra nominal GDP growth" ([The Growth Strategy Trapping The Fed | Darius Dale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvMTOKRR0SE)). Read together, the stance is: ride the liquidity-driven upside near term, but treat it as a bubble you're trading, not a foundation you trust — which is why the coding here lands him neutral across equities, gold, bitcoin, the dollar, and S&P 500 exposure rather than cleanly bullish or bearish.

As for how his views have moved over time: this record can't say. The corpus holds a single dated appearance, all of it from August 2026, with no earlier calls to compare against. That's thin coverage for a strategist whose whole brand is regime-switching, so any claim that Dale "turned" on any asset would outrun the data. What the record does show, on one day at least, is a coherent house view: everything rallies except the dollar and bonds, and the everything-rally itself is the problem the Fed eventually has to deal with.
