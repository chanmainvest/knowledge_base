# Erik Townsend

**host of Macro Voices**

_No finance-industry bio established (LLM bio unavailable or not a finance professional); the page below is DB-derived only._

Erik Townsend is the host of MacroVoices, the weekly macro podcast he built after a career as a software entrepreneur, and the format matters for reading his record: he books big-name guests for long interviews, then uses the companion "Trade of the Week" segment to put his own positioning on tape. The stance log in this corpus skews constructive — 21 bullish calls against 13 bearish and 3 neutral — but that headline tilt undersells how deliberately two-sided his market view is. A caveat is warranted up front: everything documented here comes from a single week of broadcasting in mid-June 2026, centered on episode #537 with Brent Johnson ("There's No Turning Back") and its Trade of the Week companion ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). It is a narrow slice for a portrait of views "over time," but it caught Townsend mid-dislocation, opining on nearly everything that moved.

His signature call that week was oil, and it ran in two directions at once. He was blunt about the flush already underway: "someday we're going to get a real peace deal and when we do, oil is going to sell off and sell off hard. Well, that's exactly what's happened" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). But his forward view was firmly bullish on the supply hole the Iran conflict left behind — "you can't drain 20% of the world's oil reserves and suddenly think that we're going back down to a sustained under 70 level on oil" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). On WTI he flagged a "great big glaring gap in the chart" up to roughly $84.50 and said he expected "prices settle back above 80 at some point here and maybe even as high as 90," while conceding he "wouldn't be surprised if we go lower still as the market continues to celebrate what it thinks is a certain and clean resolution to the Iran deal" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). In the Trade of the Week segment he went further: "I think we're going to go fill those gaps at least up to 85. I wouldn't be surprised if we're back over 100 before the year is out" ([Trade of The Week - MacroVoices #537](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oqHNNzR9WNc)).

The stance tracker logs oil flipping from bullish to bearish between June 18 and June 19, but that reads less like a change of heart than an artifact of the same episode appearing under multiple upload dates — with bearish quotes about the realized selloff and bullish quotes about the rebound sitting side by side. What's genuinely consistent is his skepticism of the market's euphoria: he noted "the market seems to be celebrating its perception of the Hormuz resolution being unconditionally good for financial markets. I'm a little bit skeptical" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)), and warned that "physical shortages, and the realization that this military conflict might not really be over after all, could all lead to higher prices" ([Trade of The Week - MacroVoices #537](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oqHNNzR9WNc)). The debate he's picking, in short, is with anyone pricing a clean peace.

The rest of the book followed the same war-premium logic. He was bearish the dollar — "The Iran conflict was a source of a lot of that US dollar strength, so a downside retracement makes perfect sense here" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)) — with "a lot more downside as we retrace a lot of the war premium" and the Dixie "back down to 99 and a half... testing what was previously resistance as support" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). Gold he called "set to rally spectacularly after the Iran oil dislocation and associated knock-on inflation effects are really and truly over," though he saw the current bounce capped around the 4,400 level with "a declining 50-day moving average just above" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). Uranium rounded it out: "I remain uber duper bullish long long-term on uranium," with late August to early September as when the market might "catch a bid again" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). The through-line is a macro commentator who fades the consensus celebration, respects the supply shock underneath it, and staggers his bullishness in time rather than holding it all at once.
