Erik Townsend is the host of MacroVoices, the widely followed macro interview show — and a former software entrepreneur turned macro investor who, unusually for a host, puts his own book on the air in the show's Trade of the Week segment. The record gathered here is concentrated in one violent stretch of markets, June 18–25, 2026, straddling the Iran/Hormuz dislocation and episodes #537 with Brent Johnson and #538 with Lyn Alden. The stance tally runs 21 bullish to 13 bearish with 5 neutrals, but that understates how two-sided his tape actually is: this is a host thinking out loud through a dislocation in real time.

The strongest call in the file is oil. On June 18, on [MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ), he claimed the downside correctly — he'd argued that "someday we're going to get a real peace deal. And when we do, oil is going to sell off and sell off hard," and "that's exactly what's happened." Then he pivoted immediately to supply destruction: "you can't drain 20% of the world's oil reserves and suddenly think that we're going back down to a sustained under 70 level on oil" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)). Technically he flagged "a great big glaring gap in the chart" up around $84.50 — a solid $10 above the market — and said he expects prices to "settle back above 80 at some point here and maybe even as high as 90." In that week's [Trade of The Week - MacroVoices #537](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oqHNNzR9WNc) he extended it: fill the gaps at least to 85, and "I wouldn't be surprised if we're back over 100 before the year is out."

The rest of the June book is consistent with that inflation-dislocation frame. Gold, he insisted, is still "set to rally spectacularly after the Iran oil dislocation and associated knock-on inflation effects are really and truly over" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N7mX2NJ3nxQ)), even while noting tactically that the bounce back toward 4,400 runs into a declining 50-day moving average ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). He was bearish the dollar on the same logic — the Iran conflict "was a source of a lot of that US dollar strength, so a downside retracement makes perfect sense" ([Trade of The Week - MacroVoices #537](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oqHNNzR9WNc)) — with the Dixie back at 99.5 "testing what was previously resistance as support" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)). He remains "uber duper bullish long, long term" on uranium despite recent upside retracement after weakness ([Trade of The Week - MacroVoices #537](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oqHNNzR9WNc)). And he was the geopolitics skeptic in the room: the market was "celebrating its perception of the Hormuz resolution being unconditionally good," and if the Iran deal really is as strong as the president hopes, there's "a lot more downside as we retrace a lot of the war premium" ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)).

Anyone looking for a long arc of view evolution should know this digest is essentially one week of tape. What it does show is a whipsaw the tracker itself logs: oil marked bullish June 18, bearish by June 19 after the test of the 200-day moving average at 73.50 on the August contract ([MacroVoices #537 Brent Johnson: There's No Turning Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7IDIRbZa2o)) — even as that same transcript repeats the bullish gap-fill call, and the topic file ends tagged "now bullish." Read it as a trader running both sides of a dislocation rather than a clean reversal. By June 25, on [MacroVoices #538 Lyn Alden: Is The War Really Over and What…](https://www.macrovoices.com/1537-macrovoices-538-lyn-alden-is-the-war-really-over-and-what-s-next-for-markets), he had turned more descriptive than directional, observing that the dollar index "broke out to the upside" just as the stock market "started to roll over slightly lower" on news the conflict was winding down. The through-line isn't a direction; it's a persistent distrust of markets that price in clean resolutions.
