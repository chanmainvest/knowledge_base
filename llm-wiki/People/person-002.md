劉強東 (Liu Qiangdong, widely known as Richard Liu) is the founder and chairman of JD.com and the archetype of the self-built Chinese e-commerce empire. Born to a poor rural family in Suqian, Jiangsu, he graduated from Renmin University in 1996, burned through a failed restaurant venture, and in 1998 opened a small electronics stall in Beijing's Zhongguancun technology district that grew into Jingdong. The 2003 SARS epidemic pushed him to pivot the business online, and his answer to a market awash in counterfeits was brute-force vertical integration — self-built warehousing and a self-run delivery network to guarantee authentic goods and fast service. That bet scaled into a NASDAQ listing in May 2014, one of the largest Chinese tech IPOs of its time, a secondary listing in Hong Kong in 2020, and an ecosystem spanning JD Logistics, JD Health, and JD Technology (formerly JD Digits). Liu stepped down as CEO in April 2022 but retained the chairmanship and controlling influence over the company. He remains one of China's wealthiest individuals, is married to Zhang Zetian, and is known both for philanthropy in his hometown and for a paternalistic management style toward JD's army of couriers. The record is not unblemished: a 2018 sexual-assault allegation in Minnesota dented his reputation, though it resulted in no charges and ended in a settled civil suit.
