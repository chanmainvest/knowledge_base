Tony Greer and Jared Dillian are a pairing of two trading-desk veterans turned macro commentators — Greer of the TG Macro morning note, with a background as a gold trader at Goldman Sachs, and Dillian of The Daily Dirtnap, formerly head of ETF trading at Lehman Brothers. In this corpus, though, their joint footprint rests on a single appearance: the June 17, 2026 discussion "[Nobody Owns Bonds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-mvJ6poUk4)," which sweeps across rates, currencies, energy, metals and even private-space assets in one sitting.

The through-line of that conversation is a coherent disinflationary, liquidity-friendly macro view. The headline call is that "bonds may be the world's most underowned asset" ([Nobody Owns Bonds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-mvJ6poUk4)), paired explicitly with "dollar weakness" and a bearish read on energy amid "collapsing oil prices" ([Nobody Owns Bonds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-mvJ6poUk4)). That long-bonds, short-dollar, weak-oil configuration is classic disinflation trading — and it makes their hesitation on gold the notable exception. Rather than riding the metals rally, they sit neutral, weighing "what gold's recent correction signals for the precious metals sector" ([Nobody Owns Bonds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-mvJ6poUk4)). Meanwhile the speculative appetite they decline to spend on gold shows up elsewhere: they ask "whether SpaceX is becoming the ultimate momentum trade as capital rotates across global markets" ([Nobody Owns Bonds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-mvJ6poUk4)), a tell that they see froth rotating into private-side stories even as they position for lower rates.
