# Surge Copper CEO

**solo author/host (1x)**

**Role:** President and Chief Executive Officer

The man behind Surge Copper (SURG.V) — president and chief executive Leif Nilsson — appears in this corpus exactly once, as the solo guest on a June 29, 2026 episode of Resource Talks whose title frames the whole debate for him: [$4.6B Copper Project in BC, But Can the Capex Be Funded? | …](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k). That framing matters. This is a CEO whose central problem isn't geology or grade but capital formation at scale, and nearly everything he says in the interview is arranged to answer it.

His argument runs on disciplined sizing and stress-tested economics. He caps the ambition up front, calling 120,000 tons per day of throughput "probably the maximum size" for the project, and dismisses incremental add-ons as bolt-on extras that "aren't going to be transformational from an NPV or IRR perspective" ([…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k)). The resilience claim is his strongest number: even the "negative 60% case" evaluated "at base case copper prices" still clears "like an 18% IRR" ([…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k)). Underneath it sits a straightforward capital-market truth he states plainly — anyone writing billion-dollar cheques would "rather recoup that capital in a couple of years" ([…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k)) — which is the lens through which the whole capex question should be read.

On the corporate endgame he is looser, sketching three paths rather than one. He declares some lingering chapter closed — "I believe that's done. I believe they're out now" — and suggests recent share buyers "are going to be pretty pleased" with their entry price ([…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k)). From there: ARM could increase its ownership and "contribute significant capital" within the current structure, or a major could become "a buyer of Surge one day" and run its own JV process ([…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k)). On copper itself he lets the bull case breathe without shouting it, arguing equilibrium prices may be "a lot higher than where we are," while situating the project in the broader "polarization" theme convulsing the global economy — and he points to peer Solaris (SLS.TO), whose share price has roughly "doubled in the last year," as proof the market rewards this sector ([…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvML35iIl2k)).

The neutrality is a register, not an absence of conviction; his conviction lives in the asset, expressed through funding math rather than price targets.
