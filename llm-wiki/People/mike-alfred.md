Mike Alfred is an American fintech entrepreneur who has spent his career turning financial data into businesses, and who has since recast himself as a professional Bitcoin investor and mining-sector commentator. With his brother Ryan he co-founded BrightScope, the retirement-plan ratings company acquired in 2016, and later co-founded Digital Assets Data, a crypto market-intelligence firm whose platform was bought by Coinbase — after which Alfred took a market intelligence role inside the exchange. Today he is best known in markets circles as a relentless presence on X covering Bitcoin and the publicly traded miners, and as a board member of Bitcoin miner Iris Energy (IREN), the company at the heart of his public investing identity. He is a self-described long-term Bitcoin holder, and nearly everything in this record comes from a single June 2026 appearance on Real Vision, an episode pointedly titled "The Hidden AI Trade: Land, Power, & Compute" ([The Hidden AI Trade: Land, Power, & Compute | With Mike Alf…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-p3DhlY2TEk)).

The thesis he laid out there is coherent and blunt. AI has been the only game in town — "the only thing that that has benefited over the last 3 years in mass is AI," with the S&P 500 carried by its largest, most-exposed companies — and the binding constraint on the trade is not chips but electricity: whether you "can spin up heavy-duty infrastructure… that you can keep online and you can run it at low price" ([The Hidden AI Trade: Land, Power, & Compute | With Mike Alf…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-p3DhlY2TEk)). That is the "hidden" leg of his argument: land, power and compute, with Bitcoin miners that already control cheap power positioned to pivot into AI workloads. His conviction is explicitly long-horizon — "over 5, 10, 15 years, I think there's still quite a bit of opportunity here because the market largely still doesn't understand these economic mo[ats]" — and he thinks it would be "a mistake in my opinion" to get too cautious about the theme "unless you really have to manage for like very short-term drawdowns and short-term performance" ([The Hidden AI Trade: Land, Power, & Compute | With Mike Alf…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-p3DhlY2TEk)).

On his own positioning, Alfred is unusually tidy. Bitcoin is "really the only position that I've sort of publicly espoused over that period," he says, describing it as a straight long holding ([The Hidden AI Trade: Land, Power, & Compute | With Mike Alf…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-p3DhlY2TEk)). On IREN the record is formally tagged "neutral," but that label does a lot of diplomatic work: as a director he says "I'm on the board, so I can't… give you advice to buy it," while volunteering that he's "never sold a share of Iron, even though it's gone from 28 to 1 to 75 to 28 to to 60," that "it's unlikely I'd be selling anything," and that his personal bet is "if Iron could pull this off at scale, it'll be very hard to compete with them" — the transcript's "Iron" being IREN itself ([The Hidden AI Trade: Land, Power, & Compute | With Mike Alf…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-p3DhlY2TEk)). The neutrality, in other words, is a governance constraint, not a hesitation.
