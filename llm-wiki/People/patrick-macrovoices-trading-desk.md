# Patrick (MacroVoices Trading Desk)

**Role:** Co-host of the MacroVoices podcast; derivatives/market strategist; presenter of the weekly MacroVoices Trading Desk segment

Patrick — the derivatives and market strategist who co-hosts the MacroVoices podcast (widely known as Patrick Ceresna) and fronts its weekly Trading Desk segment — is the chart-side counterweight to the show's macro interview format. Where guests make sweeping directional arguments, his job on the Trading Desk is to convert the week's price action into levels, scenarios and trade structures, and the recorded record reflects exactly that: eleven positions tagged neutral, none outright bullish or bearish, all logged from the August 20, 2026 session alongside guest Darius Dale ([MacroVoices #546 Darius Dale: Darius Dale for POTUS 2028](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRHWmEc1QAE)).

His most concrete call from that session was on the S&P 500, and it was a defense of the trend: "far from seeing significant technical damage," with prior highs acting as support and retracement lines, and even another 50 points of downside still consistent with a generally bullish trend. He reserved "serious" trouble for a selloff reaching the 7,400–7,500 area ([MacroVoices #546 Darius Dale: Darius Dale for POTUS 2028](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRHWmEc1QAE)). The same conditional style marked his Nvidia preview ahead of earnings: on balance of probabilities a beat with solid guidance, but the real signal would be how traders respond to the print, since the semiconductor laggards now "hinge on what Nvidia has to say" ([MacroVoices #546 Darius Dale: Darius Dale for POTUS 2028](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRHWmEc1QAE)).

On rates and currencies he was marginally more willing to declare a regime change. He noted bonds had reversed off 30-year Treasury yields near 5.30% and framed what follows as the open question ([MacroVoices #546 Darius Dale: Darius Dale for POTUS 2028](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRHWmEc1QAE)). On the dollar he went further — the prior bull market "has neutralized" — but pointedly refused the bearish extension: "does that make me an outright bear on the dollar? Probably not." He left the dollar index as a range question, asking whether it returns to the 96–100 band that prevailed through 2025 and the first half of 2026, and flagged a resumption of dollar weakness with yen strength as "the thing to watch" ([MacroVoices #546 Darius Dale: Darius Dale for POTUS 2028](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRHWmEc1QAE)).

He also builds trades, not just commentary: that week's trade of the week took Darius Dale's thesis that fiscal dominance and financial repression have arrived, and positioned around scarce assets as the beneficiaries ([MacroVoices #546 Darius Dale: Darius Dale for POTUS 2028](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRHWmEc1QAE)) — a fair picture of his role as the person who stress-tests guest theses into actionable structures.

One caveat belongs on the record: every dated item in the corpus comes from that single August 20, 2026 episode, so there is no timeline here from which to trace a shift in views. The all-neutral tagging is a function of his method — levels and conditionals rather than verdicts — not evasion; he demonstrates the distinction himself by declaring the dollar bull neutralized while stopping short of bearish. Until more Trading Desk sessions are logged, this is a portrait of technique, not of evolution.
