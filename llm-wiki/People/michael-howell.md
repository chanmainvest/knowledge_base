# Michael Howell

**interview guest (1x)**

**Role:** Managing Director

Michael Howell is a liquidity-first macro man — Managing Director of CrossBorder Capital and the analyst behind the "Capital Wars" framing of global finance — and his single appearance in this corpus, MacroVoices #545, "Warsh vs. The Markets" (August 13, 2026), shows the method in full. He reduces the tape to simple anatomy: "there are two moving parts, Eric. One of those is the currency of denomination" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)). Almost everything else in the hour — gold, oil, the yuan, crypto — is downstream of that frame, which is both his appeal and the reason his record reads as framework analysis rather than a wall of directional arrows.

His most committed call in the record is gold, and the mechanism is China: "the story of why gold is going up and going down," he argued, is "all about Chinese devaluation" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)). Beijing, in his telling, can run a regime of "maintaining a stable yuan US dollar externally while trying to devalue the yuan internally" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FsPoAmywH3U)), and that internal devaluation leaks into hard assets, because "as liquidity increases, it fuels precious metals" — an obvious monetary inflation hedge, in his phrase ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)). When it was put to him that "the correction is over" and "the new move higher is on," his answer was "100%" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FsPoAmywH3U)). He then set the level: assume gold is "underpinned at $4,000 an ounce, maybe it's higher, who knows" — and "gold is probably going up, at least on my estimation" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)).

The $4,000 anchor feeds straight into his oil number. Take the long-run average gold/oil ratio — historically "around about 20 times," maybe a stretched 30 — divide it into the gold price, and "we still get an oil price of $135 a barrel so substantially above where we are" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)). He flags the method's softness himself — "that may be way way off the scale... We're just triangulating here" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)) — but the episode treats the conclusion as landed: a "very persuasive argument in favor of higher oil prices and also in favor of higher bond yields," agreed "on both scores" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)). The lone outright bearish tag in his record is the dollar: a scenario he walked through "would be really bad for the dollar in my estimation and it would cause the gold market to shoot up" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn3Mkp6FCss)) — though the digest doesn't preserve what trigger he had in mind.

On crypto he stayed neutral but not kind: when the S&P dropped 25%, "Bitcoin dropped 75%," even if "some of that drop in crypto is already in the bag" ([MacroVoices #545 Michael Howell: Warsh vs. The Markets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FsPoAmywH3U)). The ledger — three bullish, one bearish, sixteen of twenty neutral — is an honest portrait: Howell is a triangulator, not a flag-planter, and even his biggest numbers arrive wrapped in hedges. With just one dated appearance there is no timeline of his views drifting across months to track; the movement the corpus shows happens inside the interview itself, from "maybe it's 30 times" hedging to an unqualified "100%" on gold's next leg higher.
