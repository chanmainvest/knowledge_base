# Milton Berg

**Role:** Market strategist and technical analyst

Milton Berg is a market strategist and technical analyst, and in this corpus his record is essentially a single snapshot: one appearance on August 12, 2026, framed around "The Worst Panic in 4 Years Just Happened" ([The Worst Panic in 4 Years Just Happened. Here's Why Milton…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Idj7jAOtE50)). Everything dated here comes from that one piece, which is worth saying up front — there's no multi-year trail of calls to reconstruct a migration in his views. What the snapshot shows instead is a technician working through an unusually split picture of the market, and saying so out loud.

His one outright bullish stance in the record is on the S&P 500. In that August discussion he laid out a median projection of 8,565.42 within a year of "the signal" — another 10.42% above where the index stood at the time — while anchoring the call to historical analogs in which the median return after this signal was 37% within a year ([The Worst Panic in 4 Years Just Happened. Here's Why Milton…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Idj7jAOtE50)). Notably, he framed the panic itself as something that "didn't really affect the broad market at the S&P," positioning the event as a technical signal rather than a fundamental rupture ([The Worst Panic in 4 Years Just Happened. Here's Why Milton…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Idj7jAOtE50)).

The bullishness comes wrapped in a lot of caution, and the surrounding calls are the more interesting part of the file. He flagged signs of a top in the semiconductor index back in June, which "ultimately proved to be a top in the semiconductor index and the Nasdaq 100 and the Nasdaq" ([The Worst Panic in 4 Years Just Happened. Here's Why Milton…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Idj7jAOtE50)). He described private credit as "really collapsing in a way," observed the 30-year bond trading at its highest levels in what he took to be decades, and argued the Fed chair would be "forced to raise rates" whether he wants to or not ([The Worst Panic in 4 Years Just Happened. Here's Why Milton…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Idj7jAOtE50)).

Read together, the picture is a barbell: double-digit upside on the broad index, but tops called in its tech leadership, a credit niche breaking, yields at generational highs, and a rate hike he considers inevitable. The obvious way to square that — a market broadening out beyond semis even as its former leaders roll over — is consistent with his numbers, but he doesn't spell the mechanism out in this material, so it stays an inference. The stance mix underscores the same caution: one bullish call, zero bearish calls, eight neutral observations. With the entire record resting on a single August 2026 document, any claim about how Berg's views have shifted over time simply isn't supportable here — this wiki can say what he argued and when, but not yet how he's moved.
