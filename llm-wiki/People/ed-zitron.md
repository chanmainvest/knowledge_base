Ed Zitron is an American writer who has become one of the loudest and most quotable critics of the modern technology industry. He cut his teeth as a technology and video-game journalist before crossing over into public relations, where he founded the firm EZ-PR, and he now runs the newsletter "Where's Your Ed At" — the venue where he coined the term "Rot Economy" for a style of management obsessed with growth and metrics at the direct expense of products and users. His 2024 essay "The Man Who Killed Google Search," which laid Google's search decline at the feet of managerial dysfunction rather than technological necessity, was widely circulated, and since 2024 he has hosted the podcast "Better Offline." In market terms, he is a one-note bear in the best sense of the phrase: his entire analytical project is the argument that generative AI is a capital-incinerating bubble, and he repeats it with unusual specificity.

His core case, laid out in his June 21, 2026 appearance on The Monetary Matters Network, is that "the sheer size of the losses by the large language model (LLM) companies are unsustainable" and that the operational costs of training and running these models dwarf anything the technology actually earns ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)). His framing is a daisy chain of red ink: "Every AI company's unprofitable. The largest customers of AI compute are unprofitable. Their customers also unprofitable" ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)). After "trillion plus dollars" spent, he argues, "the fact we're still debating the ROI kind of says everything" ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)).

His hardest call in the corpus is a dated one: asked whether the AI bubble pops in 2027 or 2028, he answered "I think 2027's the safe bet" ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)). He backs the timing with a supply-demand argument — the compute demand "does not exist," the heavy spenders are unprofitable AI companies, and the industry would need "two or three more OpenAI and Anthropic sized customers that do not exist" plus "hundreds of billions of dollars" of AI compute revenue to justify the buildout ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)). He is openly skeptical of the Broadcom–OpenAI announcement, made the previous October, of 10 gigawatts of AI data centers ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)), and he reads the coming financing wave — roughly "$150 billion within the next 6 to 12 months," much of it via IPOs — not as AI demand but as "a demand for speculative debt" ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)). Even Nvidia, whose revenue he ties to this spending, gets only a countdown: "two, three more earnings calls" if guidance stops making markets "rock hard" ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)). The moral edge of his argument is aimed at who pays: he says the industry is trying to "dump these companies on retail investors who are going to be the victims of this theological hype cycle" ([Ed Zitron: The AI Bubble is Bleeding Cash, Here Are The Rec…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFd8X7TI7Sc)).
