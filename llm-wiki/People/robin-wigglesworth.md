# Robin Wigglesworth

**solo author/host (1x)**

**Role:** Financial journalist; global finance correspondent at the Financial Times

Robin Wigglesworth is a financial journalist and the Financial Times' global finance correspondent — the author of *Trillions*, which chronicled the rise of index investing — and he hosts his own show on the Monetary Matters Network. In the corpus he appears as a solo host, and his role shapes everything else about his profile: he is not a stock-picker putting ratings on names, but a finance reporter using historical analogy to frame where the system's stress is building. That shows up in his stance mix, which is 0 bullish, 0 bearish, 6 neutral — though "neutral" undersells how uneasy some of his actual commentary is.

His single captured appearance, the August 17, 2026 episode on the $1.5 trillion of hidden debt fueling the AI boom, lays out his framework. The core argument: "whenever a transformative new technology comes that typically manifests itself in both the stock and the bond market and sometimes the most dangerous" ([The $1.5 Trillion of Hidden Debt Fueling the AI Boom | Robi…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZXjQZWUHH0)). In other words, the AI story isn't just an equity story — the credit market is where the hidden leverage lives. He scales the moment historically, noting that AI data centers represent "a few trillion dollars" today but arguing the comparison only becomes meaningful when scaled to the size of the US economy against past technological buildouts ([The $1.5 Trillion of Hidden Debt Fueling the AI Boom | Robi…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZXjQZWUHH0)).

His sharpest language is reserved for credit. On private credit, he steps back from the noise to deliver a structural warning: "I think it's going to be a bad default cycle. It's started already" ([The $1.5 Trillion of Hidden Debt Fueling the AI Boom | Robi…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZXjQZWUHH0)). And on the debt propping up AI-adjacent companies, he argues investors "have penciled in… I suspect unrealistic recovery rates when a lot of these companies are not going to have any recoveries whatsoever" ([The $1.5 Trillion of Hidden Debt Fueling the AI Boom | Robi…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kZXjQZWUHH0)). Calling that "neutral" is a classification artifact — a bad-default-cycle call with recoveries going to zero is about as cautious as journalism-as-analysis gets without issuing a sell rating.

One honest caveat: the corpus holds only this one episode, so there is no visible evolution of his views over time — no earlier stance to have moved from. What the single data point does establish is his throughline: when a speculative boom arrives, watch the bond market, not just the ticker.
