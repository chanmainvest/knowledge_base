Ryan King is the Chief Financial Officer of Equinox Gold Corp., the mid-tier gold producer founded with the backing of mining entrepreneur Ross Beaty, with producing assets concentrated in the Americas. He has held the CFO seat since the company's early years, which means the balance sheet story is largely his own: Equinox grew by acquisition and heavy capital raising, then worked its net debt down from roughly $1.6–1.7 billion to around $400 million prior to its most recent deal ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)). That deleveraging arc — grow first, repair later — is the spine of how he sells the company.

The growth math he laid out in a July 3, 2026 interview is aggressive by any mid-tier standard. Equinox's guidance heading into fiscal 2026 was 700,000 ounces of gold, and between the two companies — post-deal — the midpoint of combined pro forma guidance annualizes to 1.1 million ounces a year ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)). He then claims a 70% annual production growth rate over the next five years once new assets come online ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)) — a compounding figure worth treating as management's own framing rather than a banked outcome, but it tells you the acquisition machine hasn't downshifted.

The rest of his pitch is jurisdictional and valuation-driven. He positions the portfolio so that 70–75% of NAV comes from Canada and the United States — "safe jurisdictions, tier one jurisdictions" with deep institutional investor bases ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)) — and argues that execution, track record, and where you operate "can get premium valuations" relative to gold-mining peers ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)). It's the classic re-rating argument: the assets and the delivery record justify a multiple the market hasn't yet granted.

On the metal itself, King is conspicuously a non-caller — and that restraint is the most telling thing in his record. He narrates gold's run to $5,600 an ounce and its correction back toward $4,000, and when pressed on downside, concedes "could it go lower than this? Yes" ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)). Rather than stake his own target, he reaches for others': Pierre Lassonde's claim that M2 money supply alone implies $17,000-plus gold, and a forecaster whose model anticipated exactly this kind of pullback ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)), plus the 2011-era analog of gold hitting $1,900, correcting to $1,100, then rising again ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)). His operative philosophy is that miners "are price takers in this environment" and must build the business to work across price regimes, with a sustained bull market as pure upside for shareholders ([How High Can Gold Go? Why A 'Major Debt Crisis' Reckoning I…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NBToObvXTmI)).
