## What the corpus says

The crypto and stablecoins coverage in this corpus is, in practice, a Bitcoin file. Of the fourteen tickered calls, twelve sit on BTC-USD, with a single ETH-USD mention and one hybrid call on neocloud operators and Bitcoin miners pivoting into data-center development — a telling overlap with the AI-infrastructure debate that runs through the broader collection. The stablecoin angle, despite anchoring the theme's name, gets exactly one substantive voice: [Brent Johnson](../People/brent-johnson.md), who argued on June 19, 2026 that "stable coins are going to be a revolution that many just don't yet understand." That's thin, but it's the only place in the corpus where the "stablecoin" half of the theme is doing any work.

The bullish case is concentrated in a tight mid-June cluster of crypto natives. [Erik Voorhees](../People/erik-voorhees.md) made the strongest epistemic claim on June 18 — Bitcoin "is the best form of money that humans have ever created," and if that's true, adoption follows — while conceding that institutions "should and will" come in, "but they haven't yet." [Raoul Pal](../People/raoul-pal.md), same day, supplied the mechanism Voorhees was gesturing at: adoption by the financial system is "the thing that was required" for crypto to become a base layer of money. [Mike Alfred](../People/mike-alfred.md) rounded out the camp on June 21, framing a straight long Bitcoin position as "really the only position that I've sort of publicly espoused" over the period. It's a coherent bull bloc, but note how much of it is conviction and narrative rather than price levels or catalysts.

The skeptical side is noisier and more idiosyncratic. [George Noble](../People/george-noble.md) delivered the most colorful dismissal on June 23 — "Bitcoin is the Facebook of speculative assets... I think it's just going to go into the sunset, I just think it's irrelevant" — while [Jeffrey Christian](../People/jeffrey-christian.md) the same day aimed at the informational layer, warning that "AI is generating a tremendous amount of misinformation, bad information, inaccurate information." [Jim Chanos](../People/jim-chanos.md) sits adjacent here: his June 20 stance is technically bearish equities, targeting "inherently unprofitable business models" attached to the AI ecosystem, which bleeds into the miners-turned-neocloud story the corpus tags elsewhere. Even the host on the June 23 program leaned bearish, musing that the tape "looks like... a story that is like coming to an end."

The more interesting shift is chronological. After the June conviction cluster, the taped calls from late July and August turn distinctly sour: July 30 brings both "I fully expect it's continue to go lower" and an explicit bottom call — "I don't think Bitcoin's going to bottom till around $10,000." By August 11–13 the framing hardens into a macro story, with crypto described as "under a cloud" because it is "very liquidity sensitive," gold outperforming, and one caller projecting a breakdown below $40,000 if support fails. There's even a miner-capitulation read — that at some point "hash rate drops low enough where you can actually mine again profitably" — the classic washout marker. Only the August 7 calls push back, hedging into "middle ground" territory: not "an amazing entry in either direction." One honest caveat: the digest leaves most of these July–August calls unattributed, so the apparent bearish turn may partly reflect a change in who's on tape, not just a change of heart. On the evidence here, though, the corpus moves from true believers in June to liquidity-driven bears — with a $10,000 floor and a sub-$40,000 breakdown scenario — by August, and never really circles back.
