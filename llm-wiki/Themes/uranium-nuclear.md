## What the corpus says

The uranium thread in this corpus is small but surprisingly coherent, and it follows a clear arc across the summer of 2026. In mid-June, the tickered calls were all about timing, and none of them were enthusiastic yet. Patrick Ceresna admitted he was still "trying to discover where these uranium stocks are going to start showing bottoming formation" — code for: the bottom hasn't confirmed itself ([Patrick Ceresna](../People/patrick-ceresna.md), 2026-06-18). Erik Townsend, on the same date, put a clock on it, telling listeners to "look for late August, early September as maybe when this market starts to catch a bid again" ([Erik Townsend](../People/erik-townsend.md), 2026-06-18). That's a June conversation full of patience, not conviction.

By late August, the tone has visibly warmed, and the timing starts to rhyme. Justin Huhn argued the market was "just about to enter what is typically a strong seasonal period," with no reason to expect this year to be an exception ([Justin Huhn](../People/justin-huhn.md), 2026-08-22) — which lands almost exactly in Townsend's late-August window. Daryl Thomas confessed on the same episode to "quietly getting a little more bullish on uranium" (Daryl Thomas is the host in question, though his name carries no linked page in this digest). More substantively, an unnamed voice in that August 22 discussion laid out the structural case: utilities "forced into the spot market," trying to "piece together their uranium needs" through smaller-volume carry trades. That's the tightness argument — buyers with no choice about participating.

The sharpest clash in the corpus isn't about the metal at all — it's about the equities wrapped around the nuclear narrative. Chris Semenuk dismissed the SMR trade outright, telling listeners "you don't need to go out and buy Oklo or Nano or New Scale. All these are kind of revenue-less, profit-less businesses" ([Chris Semenuk](../People/chris-semenuk.md), 2026-06-18). It's the only outright bearish tickered call in the theme, and it draws a clean line the rest of the corpus respects: enthusiasm for nuclear power is not the same thing as a good reason to own pre-revenue nuclear equities. Nobody in the digest argues the other side.

There's also a stranger, quieter subplot: uranium as a regulatory liability rather than an asset. In the August 24 Resource Talks conversation, the Brazilian Rare Earths CEO explained the company deliberately kept uranium — along with niobium, scandium and tantalum — out of its formal resource estimate, because Brazilian legislation requires uranium to be sold back to the government at cost-plus terms, and "until there's actual definition" on how that works, they'd rather just leave it out. His framing was blunt: "we're not producing uranium... we're really just taking radioactivity out of the ground" ([Brazilian Rare Earths CEO](../People/brazilian-rare-earths-ceo-unnamed-in-transcript.md), 2026-08-24).

The honest caveat: the tickered record here is thin — four calls, three clustered on a single June episode — and several of the August views come from unnamed or unlinked speakers. But the shape is legible. June was about waiting for a bottom; August was about seasonality, forced utility buying, and a host sheepishly admitting he's turning bullish. The metal got the benefit of the doubt. The SMR names did not.
