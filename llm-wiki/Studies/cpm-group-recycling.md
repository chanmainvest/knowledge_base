# Study: How CPM Group recycles its own material

A deep read of **46 transcripts** (of 443 items, 2015-08-13 → 2026-08-04) mapping the recurring topics, what gets reused, when new material enters, and when the author says something has changed. Titles are classified across the full archive; the recycling evidence comes from the deep-read sample.

## The recycling machine

CPM Group's archive — 443 items spanning August 13, 2015 to August 4, 2026 — is a case study in a single-topic publishing engine. "Gold & Silver Price Outlook" appears in 339 titles, more than the next four categories combined, with "Debunking Myths and Misinformation" (113) and "Investment Strategies and Hedging" (69) as the main supporting frames, then supply/demand fundamentals (64), recession risk (50), geopolitics (49) and Fed policy (45). Since those tags sum to well over 443 items, the topics are less separate beats than rotating lenses clamped onto one standing question: where gold and silver are headed, and why the crowd misreads them. Notably, the deep-read evidence clusters almost entirely in the price-commentary and Fed-policy threads; the myths, hedging, supply/demand and ETF-flow categories carry no such detail, which itself tells you where the channel's real, observable output lives.

The cadence is event-anchored with a few fixed calendar slots. The annual review/outlook is one — the December 18, 2020 "Gold: 2020 Review and 2021 Outlook" logged the year's damage (gold up about 24%, silver about 46% through December 17) and set the standing hedge that Q1 optimism "may dissipate" before a mid-to-late-year recovery. Tactical pieces key off the Fed and options calendar, like the July 23, 2021 "Critical Short-Term Pivot" built around the July 27–28 FOMC and the August COMEX roll, which largely re-described the existing state — silver sideways since the August 2020 selloff. Scenario packages appear around binary events, as with the September 18, 2020 special report mapping four election scenarios for precious metals. And then there is pure restatement: the March 7, 2022 "Betting Against the Fed and Russia's Invasion" entry logs literally nothing new, and the July 14, 2020 trade-recommendation piece reiterates the multi-year call for record annual average gold prices — possibly years away — with nothing added to the thesis. That call is the spine of the channel, restated across the decade and never reversed in this record.

What gets reused is the thesis and the frame; what gets refreshed is the market-state description. The August 13, 2015 oil-market piece shows the pattern early: the reusable part is a correlational argument (the gold-oil-dollar relationship has "flipped" as the US moved toward energy self-sufficiency, the gold/oil ratio having spiked to roughly 25 before easing back toward 20); the disposable part is the numbers. Seven years later the same price-commentary template runs on war and inflation inputs — April 12, 2022 attributes a ~$25 gold move to Donbass escalation overnight and the CPI print intraday — and by September 23, 2022 the template carries that morning's Bank of England 50bp hike. The misinformation frame is similarly load-bearing: the December 7, 2021 piece wears it in the title while quietly updating the substance underneath, acknowledging the Fed's signal to exit bond purchases sooner and possibly raise rates sooner than expected.

The genuine changes of view are signaled in small edits, not manifestos. The most telling is an admission of failed expectation: on July 29, 2022, CPM notes the week's 75bp hike "did not have the effect" that a lot of people, including CPM Group, expected — the hikes-pressure-commodities transmission had broken — in the same breath as the observation that CPI and PCE had turned from rising inflation to decelerating momentum. From there the regime-change language accumulates: "peaking inflation" and "peaking interest rates" enter by September 23, 2022, and by November 11, 2022 the channel is citing the CME FedWatch path — hikes to roughly 4.75% by March, plateau through 2023, cuts from November — with fed funds at 3.75–4.0%. Not every shift is a conversion: on March 14, 2023, post-SVB, the channel explicitly marks the distance between the market's easier-policy flip and CPM's own call for 25bp hikes in March and May — a dissent, not a change. And the May 16, 2023 silver outlook shows the subtlest refresh type, re-framing inflation as affecting the availability of metal, not merely its price. Read the titles and nothing at CPM Group ever changes; read the deltas and you can watch the house view migrate from "inflation forces the Fed's hand" (March 7, 2022) to "tightening is losing traction" (July 29, 2022) to "peaking everything" (November 2022) — with the long gold thesis never once disturbed.

## Gold & Silver Price Outlook

**339 item(s) in the archive.**

The corpus runs on two clocks. There is the annual yearbook cycle — the [CPM Gold Yearbook 2019](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QgF10YLHwM0) presented March 26, 2019, the [Silver Yearbook launch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-jGHFUKTio) that April, the [2020 Gold Update](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OWNX0_Q6H80) on March 24, 2020 — and then, from April 2020 onward, a near-weekly video commentary whose default product is repetition. The deep-read sample is thin relative to 339 items, but the pattern inside it is unambiguous: all but one entry is flagged recurring, and four are outright reruns — July 14, 2020, where "nothing" is new to the thesis and the call for record annual average prices "possibly not until 2023-2025" is simply restated ([the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qjkFnDZ2YME) whose own title promises gold *won't* hit $10,000), plus March 7, 2022, August 29, 2023, and August 13, 2024, where the promised outlook never even reached the transcript. The recycled furniture is recognizable on sight: a recap of the week's tape, a consolidation frame, and calendar mechanics — the July 27-28, 2021 FOMC and a ~19.6-million-ounce August COMEX roll offered as near-term supports, the roll to the December silver contract noted in August 2025, Jackson Hole (August 27-29) flagged as the next catalyst in August 2026. Even the packaging repeats: two identically titled "[Gold Trade Signal and Recommendation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzmKuzuz2DQ)" videos [four days apart](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jbWsOJYnrtg) in June 2020, "stand aside" advice on [July 27, 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efUpv9lqFe8) echoed by the September 2022 sell recommendations, and a stable vocabulary — silver "treading war uh sideways" in July 2021, "treading water" again in November 2025.

Genuinely new material enters in bursts, almost always tied to an event or to price action breaking the standing frame. The September 18, 2020 update's single new element was a four-scenario election special report in preparation ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ssB8NII3xjU)); October then produced four election videos in three weeks ([into the election](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=375ocZPpiqI), then ["Backfield In Motion"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2KmnouE61Yg)), before November pivoted to aftermath titles like ["Gold and the Dollar Can Rise Together"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EdlOqZEF_xA) and ["Where Is The Bottom For Gold?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-HGMNPrInN8). Hard data arrives with the yearbooks: the [December 18, 2020 review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hu3yedI5Dbg) supplied the year's ledger — gold up about 24%, silver about 46%, platinum turned positive at +7% through December 17 — plus a fresh timing call that "some of the optimism that we've seen in the last month or two uh May dissipate at least in the first quarter of the Year." After that, newness clusters at shocks: the April 12, 2022 intraday decomposition ($11 overnight on Donbass escalation, another $14 the moment the March CPI printed); the March 14, 2023 SVB spike and a call for a retreat followed by roughly six months of consolidation; the February 25, 2025 risk-off day (gold off about $55) attributed to worries about the state of the US and global economies and political systems; the May 2, 2025 tariff-relief pullback of roughly $300 to ~$3,200. The format even survives a language switch — the August 2023 and December 2024 sessions run the same recap-and-outlook structure in Chinese.

The one piece of genuinely new analytical machinery appears April 5, 2024. With gold setting three records in a week (+$289.30, 14.2% off the end-February low to the April 4 high) and silver briefly over $27, Christian marshals five historical spike episodes — 1968-71, 1973-74, 1979-80, 1985-87, 1987-88, complete with the Alan Greenspan handover — and adds a thesis that post-spike outcomes depend on where in the economic cycle the spike occurs. Six weeks later that framework forces its first revision, and the revision is itself the news: gold over $2,400 in mid-May had reached levels CPM expected only by September, and "it just tells us not that the peak is here but rather that the peak will be higher than we had thought" (May 17, 2024). More often, "new" simply means old calls getting vindicated by tape. September 23, 2022: "gold dropped as low as 1648 we have been saying for months that we thought that there could be a drop down to 1650," with silver breaking the $19 target from the September 13 sell signal — a call echoing the [short-term SELL of January 22, 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JFZi4kXULCI). June 27, 2025: the year-start forecast of a second-quarter peak and summer consolidation is declared on schedule — "it's now looking in the last couple weeks as if that initial expectation of a summer low uh may in fact be emerging... we're headed into the summer doldrums if you will." December 9, 2025: "while our pricing and timing targets have been reached this year."

The author's explicit change-markers are rarer than the reruns, and they cluster where the market embarrasses the frame. The earliest, August 13, 2015, is structural: "the correlation between gold and oil as and a dollar has shifted," with the gold/oil ratio spiked to ~25 before easing back toward 20. November 11, 2022 concedes the Fed proved "more forceful... against inflation by the FED than we" expected, even as the tone flips — "we are not out of the woods but it is starting to look as if the prices are recovering." November 4, 2025 registers the rally's end: "Prices have been trading treading water for some time now and they continue to tread water," after gold's $1,000 run from ~$3,400 to ~$4,400 retraced ~$400 and silver's ~$39-to-$54 surge broke below $46. The sharpest reframe comes December 9, 2025, when $3,000 — "It was a record price just n months ago," first breached only in February 2025 — is recast as an insurance floor CPM does not expect gold to revisit for a decade. And August 4, 2026 admits its own drivers failed: "Those three factors that we were looking at for supporting higher prices 2 weeks ago really haven't materialized," the August range cut to $3,800–$4,150/$4,200 from an earlier $3,800–$4,800 summer view as gold slid from ~$4,800 toward ~$4,000 and silver — near $90 that January — tested $60 after spiking below $56, with palladium "a little bit stronger than we had expected." Across eleven years the standing bull thesis never flips; the vocabulary never changes; the only thing that reliably moves is the number it gets attached to.

## Debunking Myths and Misinformation

**113 item(s) in the archive.**

This archive is a debunking franchise, and its most striking feature is how little the raw material changes over two years. The core myth set is fixed by late 2020 and early 2021: that gold and silver are manipulated, that physical shortage is imminent, that the dollar relationship dooms gold, and that collapse is around the corner. The "myth vs reality" framing is on the table by July 2020 ([Gold: Myth vs Reality](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DtuyP7NiPsg), July 17, 2020), and the conspiracy/manipulation thread runs from February 2021 ([Conspiracy Theories, Manipulation…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9rVxB_tvGuM), February 5, 2021) through ([Who's Been Fooling Whom In Gold and Silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nnX-4J3WOlk), August 17, 2021). The clearest evidence that this is recycled stock material, not fresh analysis: the May 5, 2021 upload is explicitly a re-run — [Conspiracy Theories Are A Distraction (2008)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lM-Wl8tCaJg) — meaning the argument predates this entire archive by over a decade.

The recycling loop is easy to map because each standing myth gets re-debunked on a several-month cycle. Shortage claims: addressed in June 2021 ([The US Mint "Global Silver Shortage" Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gVEVhwK4UOc)) and again almost a year later ([Ample Supplies If You Know How To Look](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jDwWMa22XcQ), May 24, 2022). Collapse-and-hyperbole claims: August 2021 ([The Imminent Economic Collapse…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sRqbuPl9c_k)), December 2021 ([Overcome Misinformation and Fear…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwkmQyCIdyQ)), May 2022 ([Fatal Market Hyperbole](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sOZzfktO8ko)). Foundational nostalgia gets the same treatment ([Nostalgia For A Gold Standard That Never Was](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTCGEdqDwWk), August 27, 2021), as do the dollar-inversion and rates myths ([Gold and the Dollar Can Rise Together](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EdlOqZEF_xA), November 13, 2020; [Why Interest Rates Won't Hurt Gold and Silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RiRYdTEmEpw), August 20, 2021).

Genuinely new information enters only when the outside world forces it in, and it arrives in tight clusters. The February–March 2021 silver squeeze produced five items in under three months, including the event-specific [Silver Futures Contract Default Not Likely](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C1EYmE719jY) (February 23, 2021), a credibility defense of CPM's own record ([CPM Group's Record On Silver…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bxcPKsYf25o), March 3, 2021), and a direct open letter to the silver community ([The Company You Keep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7K3yzsxzlaI), April 27, 2021). The war in Ukraine triggered the second genuine infusion: [The Wartime Market](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=77RCqH8YI64) (March 18, 2022) and [The Truth About Russian Gold and The Ruble](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7q88UbXPoC0) (April 4, 2022). Everything else — the inflation relationship, the constitutional arguments, the "real factors" explainer ([The Real Factors Driving Gold and Silver Prices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E5KqE4MVKbo), December 23, 2021) — is elaboration of the standing framework, not new material.

On the specific question of moments where the author says something HAS changed: the digest here is title-level only, with no transcripts, so I cannot quote spoken claims and won't invent them. The only verbatim language available is the titles themselves, and notably their rhetoric runs the other way — they insist nothing fundamental has changed, that the same debunked narratives keep returning. The closest title-level change-assertions are denials of others' change claims: [No Smash Down In Gold and Silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJ6GSblMPwU) (August 13, 2021) rejects the claim that a smash-down occurred, and the dollar piece asserts a relationship (both rising together) that the standard narrative denies. The cadence tells the real story: 2020 runs at roughly monthly pace, 2021 accelerates to near-biweekly during the squeeze and again in a five-video August burst, and 2022 settles into event-paced monthly output. The template re-fires every two to six weeks under market stress, but the underlying argument — the myths are old, the fundamentals are knowable, nothing has really changed — never updates.

## Investment Strategies and Hedging

**69 item(s) in the archive.**

The archive's shape tells you most of what you need to know. It opens with a one-off dollar-strength piece in June 2016 ([How Strong is the US Dollar 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QSBDIUoKWV0)), then goes silent for four years before relaunching in August 2020 with Jeffrey Christian and CPM Group under a title that is essentially the entire thesis: "All Roads Lead To Gold" ([CPM Group - All Roads Lead To Gold - Jeffrey Christian](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNkMV4Je9Fk)). From there, every macro subject — debt, inflation, recession, politics — is resolved into the same gold-and-silver conclusion. One honest caveat before the timeline: this digest consists of titles and dates only, with no transcript text, so the "change" moments below are quoted from exact title wording rather than spoken remarks.

The single most recycled asset in this corpus is the recession itself. It debuts as "The Imminent Economic Collapse" on August 31, 2021 ([The Imminent Economic Collapse and What It Means For Gold and Silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sRqbuPl9c_k)), re-enters through the inflation-and-rates lens in June 2022 ([How Inflation, Interest Rates and Recession May Affect Gold and Silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=10zL22_Y39s)), gets a duration estimate a month later ([How Long The Recession Will Last And How It Will Affect Gold and Silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zp8K-lh1Bu4)), is declared flatly in October 2022 ([A Recession Is Coming - This Is Why](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dvWvJK5b8Kw)), reappears as a question in March 2023 ([Gold and Silver Market Update: The Next Recession?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QyOCdpZkhtU)), becomes preparation material by October 2023 ([Preparing For The Next Recession With Gold and Silver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JuUnsDXzh9k)), and is still headlining the 2024 forecast in May 2024 ([2024 Gold And Silver Forecast: Inflation, Recession, and Profiting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3BGoTQ7i_vg)). ## Geopolitics and Political Risk

**49 item(s) in the archive.**

The geopolitics thread in this CPM Group corpus begins where you'd expect: the November 2020 US election. Jeffrey Christian laid out the gold-and-silver landscape heading into the vote on October 9, 2020 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=375ocZPpiqI)), followed it with an "Election and its Aftermath" piece on October 23 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yCcUJABE6F8)), and by December 4 was folding the result into history — "Politics and Gold, The Lessons of 1933" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PVznAwB10mQ)), reaching back to the Depression-era gold recall for a framework. The template is set immediately: an external political shock, then the question of what it means for gold and silver. It fired once more on January 8, 2021 — "Gold Fell As DC Burned," two days after the Capitol riot ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gN-aOb4Iqag)) — and then the topic goes silent for eight months.

Geopolitics returns on September 10, 2021, genericized to "Worldwide Risks" ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MMzgPml9Kho)), and here genuinely new arguments enter: a September 17 piece on what the Constitution "REALLY" says about gold and silver ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDU7yndPMC0)) — a legal-textual angle absent from the 2020 material — and an October 1 tie between the debt-ceiling standoff and silver trading patterns ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cWcWZxI5WEY)). Then the war. "The Finance of War: Russia, Gold, and Silver" lands February 18, 2022, six days before Russia invaded Ukraine ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdP2OsZ8eIo)), and triggers the densest stretch in the archive: eight more titles in seven weeks, March 7 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pHw0NWad6U0)), March 8 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20QsWnUrRD0)), March 18 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=77RCqH8YI64)), March 22 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nZrR3D_TfDc)), March 25 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ki_Z9JP0lZs)), April 4 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7q88UbXPoC0)), April 8 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QRS6YAiqto)) and April 12 ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jWAqgYvBuV0)). The cadence, in short, is event-reactive rather than continuous: weekly bursts around the 2021 debt ceiling, the spring-2022 invasion and the 2024 election run-up, separated by two eight-month silences (January–September 2021; April–December 2022). What recycles is the prism — shock, then gold and silver — plus specific threads on long leashes: Russian gold from March 2022 to July 2023, Washington dysfunction from October 2021 to October 2023, politics-and-gold from December 2020 to July 2024. What's genuinely new each cycle is a discrete argument: 1933 history, constitutional text, digital currencies, China/Japan, Saudi oil. And what is conspicuously absent is retraction — at title level there is no "I was wrong" anywhere, only the March 2022 admission that prices weren't cooperating and the May 2024 dismissal of de-dollarization. ## Fed Policy & Interest Rates

From there the cadence turns industrial: six titles in the ten weeks between October 5 and December 14, 2021 ([one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0obgfA3eB0Q), [two](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5o9BHtiXJ_M), [three](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AbmjEzDxiGY), [four](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ScFb681FrjA), [five](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LqwpX2Qgck4), [six](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L_igFqCmfZI)), then two four days apart in January 2022 as markets digested Fed minutes ([Jan 7](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4zoD4yd1V_4), [Jan 11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3iijoftbjEk)), then three in three weeks in June 2022 ([Jun 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tbpqSM5puYU), [Jun 10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L-R8CIE_qME), [Jun 24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=10zL22_Y39s)). These are event-triggered re-runs — each Fed meeting or inflation print resets the numbers, never the frame. Thirteen of the listed titles fall in 2022 alone.

Three arguments carry the reuse. The low-base claim: on March 7, 2022, with inflation at 7% and talk of 14%, "now it's from zero percent to two percent" ([The Rise Of Gold: Betting Against the Fed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pHw0NWad6U0)); by July 29, 2022 it is "from 0 to 2.5 in the fed funds rate," yet still below pre-2006 levels. The Fed-versus-market thread is the most durable: named in a [June 3, 2022 title](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tbpqSM5puYU), formalized November 11, 2022 with the CME FedWatch path (hikes to roughly 4.75% by March, a 2023 plateau, cuts from November 2023), and sharpened on March 14, 2023, when CPM stuck with 25bp hikes in March and May while post-SVB markets flipped to easing. It returns May 16, 2023 — "The Disconnect between how the Federal Reserve says they will raise interest rates, and the market perception…" — and runs through 2024: January 5, only 2–3 cuts (~75bp) against market hopes of six; August 13, with core PPI at 3.3% ex food and energy, "there's been so much enthusiasm and expectation that the Fed will definitely cut interest rates in September…". Meanwhile the inflation-gold explainer recycles on [Oct 15, 2021](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5o9BHtiXJ_M), [Aug 19, 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kYkxdMHkAG0), and [Feb 14, 2023's "It's Not So Simple"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aiGDIKClJT8); [How Interest Rates May Affect Gold](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=im271XZWR18) in May 2022 is the same video family as January and August 2021.

The declared turning points are datable because the author flags them. On December 7, 2021 — between the [Dec 10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LqwpX2Qgck4) and [Dec 14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L_igFqCmfZI) videos on gold's odd softness amid rising inflation and oil — the Fed "announced that given stronger and more persistent inflation than perhaps some of them thought unexpected" an earlier bond-purchase exit, yet "since the feds started talking about tapering uh 10-year bond yields have actually come down." On March 7, 2022: "we need to withdraw monetary accommodation we need to buy fewer bonds in our portfolio." On July 29, 2022, two changes at once: "inflation rates are still very high but the rate of increase is declining," and "we've probably seen the bulk of the increases in interest rates now already." September 23, 2022 widens the lens — "there are recessionary conditions present in Europe and in the United States and Japan and the UK" — and November 11, 2022 completes the arc: "there are signs of peaking inflation there are signs that we are potentially moving toward peaking interest rates." As for the shape of the reuse: it tracks the drama. Sparse before 2021 — a lone dollar title from June 2016, the fourth in some earlier series ([How Strong is the US Dollar 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QSBDIUoKWV0)), a pair in 2020 ([All Roads Lead To Gold](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNkMV4Je9Fk), [Gold and the Dollar Can Rise Together](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EdlOqZEF_xA)) — then weekly-to-biweekly through the tightening shock, quarterly in 2023 ([Feb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aiGDIKClJT8), [Apr](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oH_UVuotrnE), [Aug](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6LEB_R7gA0), [Sep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9u03GgrWg1A)), semiannual through 2024, then a fifteen-month silence before November 2025. The through-line is striking: for nearly three years every entry assumed markets were too dovish relative to the Fed — until the dovish turn finally came from the Fed itself, leaving the franchise's core argument, like the gold price it tracks, "waiting for a direction."

The honest read: this is an evergreen argument machine, fired on a market-event trigger, with new ideas entering at a trickle while the demand thesis does the heavy lifting in every regime.
