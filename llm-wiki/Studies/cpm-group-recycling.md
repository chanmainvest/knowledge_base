# Study: How CPM Group recycles its own material

A deep read of **46 transcripts** (of 576 items, 2015-04-01 → 2026-08-28) mapping the recurring topics, what gets reused, when new material enters, and when the author says something has changed. Titles are classified across the full archive; the recycling evidence comes from the deep-read sample.

## The recycling machine

CPM Group's channel is best understood as one core call surrounded by a servicing apparatus: 476 of its 576 catalogued items (spanning 2015-04-01 to 2026-08-28) are gold and silver price outlooks, and nearly every other topic — hedging, misinformation, manipulation myths, inventories — exists to support that thesis. The cadence runs on a three-layer clock. A roughly weekly commentary issues running price targets (the 2020-05-19 trade signal raised the short-term goal to 1765 by May 29 after gold blew past the prior week's 1760 target and touched 1776); a monthly layer adds forecast videos and the Precious Metals Advisory, whose April 2021 issue led with "Hyperinflation is Unlikely" (2021-04-09); and an annual layer anchors to CPM's research products, from the Gold Yearbook launch panel with Rohit Savant and Mu Li streamed via Bloomberg on 2015-04-01, to the "Hard Lessons Learned" year-end review of 2021-12-21 and the yearly "Facts and Fantasies" debunk (2024-02-28). Around that clock, event-driven specials fire when news demands: the US Mint's silver-shortage clarification (2021-06-02), Russia's invasion and the LME's nickel halt (2022-03-11), the debt-ceiling countdown (2023-05-19), and Iran's missile attack on Israel (2024-10-04). The engine even grows new sub-franchises on air — the 2020-08-21 video teased the following week's silver commentaries.

What gets reused is the chassis. The hedging worked example returns with only the entry price moved — gold bought at 1850 with the $80-premium-doubling arithmetic on 2020-11-20. The historical charts, real versus nominal gold since 1700 and the 1968 close of the first gold window, recur on 2020-08-21. The structural theme that Western banks have been pulling out of bullion trading is restated on 2021-02-12 with nothing new attached. And some videos are pure reruns: the entries for 2023-01-10 and 2024-05-31 are both flagged as adding nothing, the honest measure of how much of the feed is maintenance broadcasting around a thesis fixed enough to be a literal title ("Always Own Gold and Silver," 2023-06-30).

What gets refreshed is the data layer and the villain roster. The same slots carry each month's specifics: gold $1,600–$1,800 and silver $22–$27 for the coming two-to-three months on 2021-04-09; London silver stocks at a record 1.25 billion ounces that same day; COMEX registered gold at record levels "by a multiple" of the past on 2021-11-09; a refreshed supply-demand and inventory picture supporting the silver surplus thesis on 2025-05-27. Christian is unapologetic about the churn — on 2021-06-02 he rebutted an online critic who suggested something was wrong with CPM precisely because it issues new projections so often. The refresh is the product; the argument is the packaging.

Genuine changes of view are signaled in a consistent grammar: revised targets and explicit rule-outs, never thesis flips. On 2021-04-09 the expected rally was pushed to late 2021 into 2022, with a lower, less-volatile season flagged; by 2021-07-30 he was publishing downside targets (gold 1780, possibly 1680; silver 24, possibly 22); by 2021-09-24 the frame had become a five-quarter consolidation of 1740–1900; by 2022-02-01 the call was two-sided — 1850–1860 upside against a 1780 downside pivot — as close to hedging his own brand as the format allows. Structural rule-outs are rarer and more telling: on 2022-10-07 he argued a 1985-style coordinated dollar-lowering accord is impossible because governments now benefit from a strong dollar (the dollar up 31% since 2011); on 2023-05-19 he marked the default-risk premium coming out of gold as a debt-ceiling deal got priced. In battery metals he conceded a call had landed — the prior bearish platinum view realized by 2022-05-31 — and pivoted to a palladium recovery the same day. The record also holds one unprompted self-correction: on 2021-07-30 he publicly revisited a misstatement from a 2011 debate, and his recession call of 2024–2025 was still being maintained, with the economy admittedly outrunning consensus, on 2023-06-30.

The debunking franchise is itself a recycling engine, and it is where the channel's tensions live. The 2021-12-21 review tallied the failed dollar-collapse and great-reset predictions and noted investors tuning out fear-sellers; 2024-01-09 framed the fight with Hannah Arendt's "Lying in Politics"; 2024-02-28 refreshed the villain roster from WallStreetSilver and Mike Maloney's $5,000 silver to missile-and-solar hype; 2024-04-09 added promoters seizing on Powell's 60 Minutes remark. The recurring material earns its keep mainly in the non-recurring entries — the overlooked mechanism of bank inventory liquidation behind 2020's ETF creations (2021-02-12), the Mint's blanks-not-silver reframe (2021-06-02), the case that OPEC's leverage is far weaker than in 1973 (2024-10-04). But note what goes unresolved: the same author who debunks dollar-collapse marketing warned of a "very significant constitutional crisis" heading into the next inauguration (2021-12-21), and on 2024-10-04 rebuked viewers begging him to stay away from politics. The channel debunks fear as a sales tactic while trading in political alarm of its own — and the archive never really reckons with that.

## Gold & Silver Price Outlook

**476 item(s) in the archive.**

The archive runs on two clocks. Annual yearbook launches anchor it — April 2015, then March 2017, March 2019, and March 2020 — and from July 2020 the video feed itself becomes a metronome, publishing commentary nearly every week: July 6, August 4, August 14, August 31, September 4 and 18, October 2, 9, 16, 23, 30, November 6, 13, 30, December 4, 11, 18, straight through into 2021 [([Jul 6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zTueacq7_r8), [Oct 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPfeR2Kml4I), [Dec 18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hu3yedI5Dbg))]. Several entries — August 14, October 2, October 30, November 30 — are even logged twice, same URL, same date, which is almost too on-the-nose for a recycling audit [([Aug 14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ukhj3jSRAQE), [Oct 30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2KmnouE61Yg))]. The deep reads confirm the recurrence is structural: every sampled item is flagged recurring, and two — May 19, 2020 and one of the three September 29, 2023 entries — record new="nothing" outright; an August 18, 2023 read is likewise "this week's snapshot only."

What repeats is a skeleton: today's gold and silver levels, the range "we've been talking about for a while" (May 31, 2022), fresh client trade recommendations ("cpm group today has issued new trade recommendations for gold and for silver," November 9, 2021; again "yesterday monday the 18th" in April 2022), and imperative titles like "No One Should Be Short On Gold" [([Aug 31, 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fRzghWogdJg))]. Market-state declarations are stock phrases too — "the gold price dropped 27 today" (September 24, 2021), "gold is now broken above 1800 it's actually broken above 1820 today uh it's gotten up to about 1832" (November 9, 2021). The most literal act of recycling comes May 31, 2024, when the video re-shows the 10-year silver projection chart from the 2021 Silver Yearbook, with Mike Hollings objecting they were "giving away uh what we charge a lot for" — and in the same breath the old forecast is walked back: "actually higher projections than we have now we've lowered the projections," a long way from 2021's call for annual average silver to "exceed $40."

Genuinely new material enters in two registers. Mostly it is level-setting — the week's digits ladder upward across the archive: $1,600–$1,800 gold and $22–$27 silver ranges (April 9, 2021), downside targets of 1,780 and possibly 1,680 (July 30, 2021), five-quarter consolidation bands of 1,740–1,900/1,740–2,000 (September 24, 2021), the record $1,798.97 annual average (December 21, 2021), then $2,538 intraday (August 2024), $2,700 (October 2024), roughly $3,000 (March 2025), $3,500 after "a $500 surge in about 10-12 days" (April 22, 2025), nearly $3,900 (September 30, 2025), over $4,200 (November 14, 2025), $4,500 (January 6, 2026), above $4,600 with silver near $71.38 (August 28, 2026). The other register is exogenous shocks: Ukraine's "major battle is starting today as we speak" (April 19, 2022), the post-CPI spike to $1,975 (November 14, 2023), the July 7, 2025 tariff-deadline whipsaw, and January 2026's shutdown-delayed data that "may be economically hostile" and "could add to the fuel." The earliest new-material flag is franchise-building rather than markets — August 21, 2020, when silver-focused commentaries spin off from the gold feed. Occasionally the loop pauses to keep score: the July 15, 2021 trade call largely materializing at $1,837, and platinum printing 904 against the projected 905 bottom (September 29, 2023).

The moments the author says something has changed are, tellingly, mostly confessions about timing. July 30, 2021: "we had thought that the prices would be a little bit stronger uh but they haven't been." May 31, 2022: "we thought that the prices would show a little bit more strength than they have." February 22, 2023: "prices have been weaker than some people expected they've been weaker than CPM group expected actually." November 14, 2023: "we have moderated some of our expectations for how high prices will go over the last several years." Meanwhile the core call never moves — April 9, 2021's "we do still expect higher gold and silver prices later at toward the last four months of this year and into 2022 and beyond" is the same deferral restated for years — until it finally cashes on May 31, 2024: "we thought we'd enter a period of sharply higher prices 2024 2025 and we are." The bookend arrives August 28, 2026, in the same grammar: "Instead, investor buying began earlier" than the expected timeline. Wrong on the clock, right on the compass, repeatedly.

Only once does the argument itself change rather than the numbers. On April 22, 2025, amid the $3,500 breakout, CPM flags that the safe-haven reflex has decoupled: "This time around, we're not seeing a surge into the dollar, and we're not seeing a surge into treasuries" — investors turning to gold alone, unlike the 1978–1980 and 2002–2011 spikes, as "the dollar has fallen and treasury bonds have fallen and gold prices have risen sharply." Everything else is the same machine with updated digits, right down to Jackson Hole serving twice as a calendar landmark — two-sided scenarios around it in August 2025, then a year later the note that "the gold price took off last year after the Kansas City Fed uh Jackson Hole conference." The cadence of reuse, in short, is metronomic — weekly-to-monthly updates inside annual yearbook re-anchors — the directional call barely moves for five years, and "change" is declared most often as an apology for being early.

## Investment Demand & Hedging

**161 item(s) in the archive.**

The spine of this topic is CPM Group's recurring trade-signal video, which in 2020 lands on a one-to-three-week rhythm — May 19, June 8, June 12, July 6, July 14, then roughly monthly after that ([May 19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ef8-E4UMeW4), [June 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KzmKuzuz2DQ), [June 12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jbWsOJYnrtg), [July 6](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zTueacq7_r8), [July 14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qjkFnDZ2YME)). What's genuinely new in each installment is almost always just updated levels: on May 19, 2020 Christian raised a short-term target of 1765 for May 29 with follow-ons at 1775 and a test of $1,800, then conceded gold "blew right past 1760 got up to 1776 and then came off it," announcing he was "looking at a new target on a short-term basis" — last week's forecast superseded, framework untouched. On that same cadence the recommendation wheel spins: "Why It Is Time To Stand Aside" on July 27 ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efUpv9lqFe8)), "All Roads Lead To Gold" three weeks later ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNkMV4Je9Fk)), "No One Should Be Short On Gold" ten days after that ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fRzghWogdJg)), a "short term SELL recommendation" in January 2021 ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JFZi4kXULCI)), then "STAND ASIDE recommendation for Gold; BUY for Silver" in March ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q8N1yQ5R8c0)) — a full long-to-flat-to-short-to-flat cycle in eight months.

What never changes is the core allocation thesis. On August 21, 2020 the deep-read flags two entries as "nothing" new, just the standing case: "we have written extensively and studied extensively the value and benefits of having gold as part of your wealth" ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vNkMV4Je9Fk)). Nearly three years later, on June 30, 2023, the same line reappears, again flagged "nothing": "CPM group has always advised that… you should always have some physical metal as that long-term insurance." The hedging series recycles verbatim across months — on February 1, 2022 Christian says "as i said last month when i started talking about hedging and hedging with uh for investors options are like dynamite," repeating January's warning word for word. And the purest repackaging: August 26, 2022's history of investor net-selling ("from 1990 until 2005 2006 you had about 16 years of consistent net sales") is restated on November 18, 2022 with three separate "nothing" flags, the old position now "packaged as a five-page market alert issued 17 Nov 2022 off the FTX collapse." New hook, same argument.

Genuinely new information arrives as event triggers, not thesis revisions. November 20, 2020 brings investment demand "more than double what it was last year," the contested election, the January Senate runoff, and a real shift in CPM's own activity: "we're actually talking to various companies about that right now" on producer hedging. February 2022 adds named option structures — an October 2022 1750 put at $58.30, a 1650 put funded by a 1900–2000 call spread for $3.60 net — alongside a stance flip: "today we issued new trade recommendations on gold and silver saying stay long because we do think that the prices have further to…," ending a stretch where "we've had a lot of stand asides." Then April 2022's tout pricing ($31–33 asked, $29 accessed), January 2023's disclosure that he "may be one of the largest silver stackers in the world," July 2024's spike ("it spiked higher on uh Thursday to about $2,425") and the intraday flip — "so now we have issued a new Ultra shortterm gold trade recommendation uh saying go long" — November 2024's handoff ("those risks and uncertainties disappeared with the election… a whole new set of risks and uncertainties have appeared"), April 2025's mea culpa ("I had said $1,200 to 3500. I had meant to say $2,600 to 3500"), January 2026's broadening of momentum flows into copper and aluminum ("This is not a silver event. This is a global event"), and February 2026's January ETF holdings data with Lunar New Year demand holding up.

The tell is what "changed" means in Christian's mouth: a trade, a price, or a news hook — never the thesis. The only regime-level change he declares is regulatory and about crypto, not metals: "central banks have taken a hands-off approach toward cryptocurrencies for most of the period since 2009. That's starting to change…", alongside governments gaining "the ability to come in and reverse transfers." The nearest thing to a metals-demand revision is February 2022's re-anchoring — "investors have backed away from their fear of recession," inflation down seven months, demand falling from 40m oz in 2020 to 26–27m oz in 2021. On cadence: the documented reuse is roughly monthly, with the deep-reads themselves flagging "nothing" new at least eight times — and since the deep-reads cover only about two dozen of the 161 archive items, with long unexamined stretches (February to November 2021; August 2023 to July 2024), the true recycling rate is almost certainly higher than the record shows.

## Debunking Precious Metals Misinformation

## CPM Group Research & Events

The spine of this archive is a machine built to repeat itself. At the April 1, 2015 Gold Yearbook launch, Jeffrey Christian noted "we've hosted this for several years" and that CPM has "change[d] the format over the years to modify and meet the needs of our clients" ([2015 launch panel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c48O8Oq1920)). April 9, 2024 is the densest data drop: Powell's February 4 "60 Minutes" warning ("they are unsustainable") set against Greenspan's identical 1987 warning — "the debt and deficits were unsustainable in 1987 at a fraction of what they are now: $1.3 trillion deficit, $34 trillion debt" — Congress having "basically haven't done anything"; the 2010–12 "Five Easy Pieces" report upgraded to "Seven Easy Pieces" in late 2023; and record offshore Treasury holdings of $8 trillion, up 10.5%, with "investors shifting their assets… into treasuries" — the standing rebuttal to de-dollarization. August 2024 refines the framing: gold has "lost that unit of account value" and no longer settles trade, but "is still a store of value, it's a reserve asset."

From 2025 the cadence thins to quarterly updates bolted onto the same spine. January 21, 2025: the dollar rising "contrary to de-dollarization narratives," with central banks "using their dollars to help stimulate their domestic economies" from the 58–60% of FX reserves they hold. April 22, 2025: "it was off 6% from the end of 2024, three and a half months ago," yet "the March average was the highest dollar exchange rate since 2002… so yes, the dollar has fallen, but it is still very high" — plus a rare self-audit, "it's not the correlation from 1868 to 2024. It's a correlation from 1968 to 2024." July 8, 2025 recycles the BRICS summit with only a new agenda and odds assessment; August 19, 2025 delivers the corpus's one wholly new topic — a viewer question answered with the conclusion that no definitive debt threshold exists — along with updated figures: debt growth "accelerated again after the financial crisis in 2007 to 2011 and… accelerated even further since 2020, 2021," with debt-to-GDP "at the highest level it's been at really since 1947." The August 28, 2026 session is flagged as containing nothing new at all: "the Congress has almost abrogated all of its responsibilities here" amid "out-of-control deficit spending and behavior on the part of the administration and the Congress" — lines that could have aired in 2024. That is the shape of the reuse: the thesis never turns; what changes, by his own account, is the data — dollar levels, debt totals — and the crisis du jour hung on it.
