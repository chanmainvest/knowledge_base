# Executive summary

As of the most recent reported quarter‑end (**FY/Q4 close December 31, 2025**, reported in February 2026), **both Newmont and Barrick report large positive book net assets (shareholders’ equity)**, but they differ materially in balance‑sheet composition, reserve base scale, and the magnitude of long‑dated obligations (deferred taxes, closure/reclamation, and benefit plans).

### Book NAV (equity attributable to company shareholders) at 2025‑12‑31

* **Newmont:** **$33.867B** attributable to Newmont stockholders.
* **Barrick:** **$26.557B** attributable to Barrick shareholders.

### NAV per diluted share (using diluted weighted‑average shares)

* **Newmont:** diluted shares **1,108M** → **Book NAV/sh ≈ $30.56**.
* **Barrick:** diluted shares **1,707M** (basic = diluted) → **Book NAV/sh ≈ $15.56**.

### NAV per ounce of attributable proven & probable (P&P) gold reserves

* **Newmont:** attributable gold reserves **118.2 Moz** → **Book NAV ≈ $287/oz**.
* **Barrick:** attributable P&P gold reserves **85 Moz** → **Book NAV ≈ $312/oz**.

### Capital structure and “net cash” positioning (balance‑sheet defined)

* **Newmont:** cash **$7.647B** and debt **$5.115B** (plus lease/financing obligations) implies a **net cash** posture consistent with management’s “net debt (cash)” disclosure.
* **Barrick:** cash **$6.706B** vs total debt **$4.724B** = **~$2.0B net cash**; Barrick also explicitly describes “debt net of cash and equivalents” as **$(2.0)B** at 2025‑12‑31.

**Key analytical takeaway:** book NAV is accounting‑based (historical cost, accumulated depreciation, impairment tests) and **is not the same as the mining‑industry DCF NAV** commonly used in sell‑side valuation. To bridge that gap, this report provides:

* a transparent **book NAV build**,
* a **tangible/adjusted NAV** overlay with explicit assumptions, and
* sensitivity ranges that are most “load‑bearing” for miners (deferred taxes, closure provisions discounting, and development/exploration asset realizability).

## Data sources and key definitions

### Quarter‑end used

* **December 31, 2025** (most recently reported quarter‑end available as of 2026‑04‑10 in North America), based on each company’s latest annual/Q4 reporting package.

### Primary / official sources

* **Newmont:** Q4/FY2025 earnings release PDF containing the consolidated balance sheets and year‑end disclosures (investor relations hosted).
* **Newmont 10‑K filing content (text mirror):** CapEdge renders the filing text (used to extract note‑level detail such as inventory composition, PP&E/mineral interests staging, pension/other benefits tables, reclamation roll‑forward, and diluted shares).
* **Barrick:** 2025 Annual Report PDF (financial statements + MD&A + reserves/resources discussion), including consolidated balance sheets, inventory note, PP&E note, deferred tax note, provisions note, and EPS share counts.

### NAV definitions used in this report

* **Book NAV (GAAP/IFRS net assets):** equity attributable to common shareholders (total equity **excluding** noncontrolling interests).
* **Tangible NAV:** Book NAV less goodwill and explicitly reported intangible assets (where separately disclosed).
* **Adjusted NAV (analytical overlay):** Tangible NAV plus/minus explicit, stated assumptions for:

  + discounting long‑dated deferred tax liabilities,
  + haircutting exploration/development‑stage capitalized mineral interests, and
  + remeasuring closure provisions based on disclosed discount‑rate sensitivity (Barrick).

## Methodology

### Core book NAV identity (accounting)

Book NAV (shareholder net assets) = **Total assets − Total liabilities**, attributable to the parent’s shareholders (excluding noncontrolling interests). Both companies present this directly as “equity attributable to shareholders/stockholders.”

### Per‑share NAV

* NAV per diluted share = **Book NAV ÷ diluted weighted‑average shares outstanding** (from EPS notes/tables).

### Per‑ounce NAV (P&P reserves basis)

* NAV per attributable P&P reserve ounce = **Book NAV ÷ attributable P&P gold reserves (Moz)**.

### Treatment of required balance‑sheet attributes

* **Cash & equivalents:** taken directly from the balance sheet.
* **Total debt:** short + long‑term debt; leases/other financing shown separately.
* **Minority / noncontrolling interests:** shown separately and excluded from NAV attributable to shareholders.
* **Deferred taxes:** deferred tax assets (DTA) and liabilities (DTL) shown explicitly; net DTL highlighted.
* **Working capital:**

  + total net working capital = current assets − current liabilities, and
  + operating working capital proxy = (current assets excluding cash) − (current liabilities excluding short‑term debt).
* **Inventories (ore, concentrate):** taken from inventory notes.
* **Capitalized exploration & evaluation assets:**

  + Newmont: “exploration‑stage/development‑stage mineral interests.”
  + Barrick: “mining property costs not subject to depreciation” (proxy).
* **PP&E:** taken directly from PP&E statements/notes.
* **Goodwill / intangibles:** goodwill separately stated for both; Barrick also reports intangible assets. Newmont does not present a standalone intangible asset line item.
* **Pension obligations:** extracted from benefit plan disclosures.
* **Environmental liabilities / reclamation provisions:**

  + Newmont: “reclamation and remediation liabilities.”
  + Barrick: “provision for environmental rehabilitation (PER)” with discount‑rate sensitivity.

* **Book NAV (USDm):**

  + Barrick **85**

  + Newmont **$287/oz**
  + Barrick **$312/oz**
* **Cash / debt (USDm):**

  + Newmont **7,647 / 5,115**
  + Barrick **6,706 / 4,703**
* **Net deferred tax liability (USDm):**

  + Newmont **~4,000**
  + Barrick **3,941**

  + Newmont **6,297**

* **Noncontrolling interests (USDm):**

  + Newmont **175**

### Interpretation

* **Reserve scale:** Newmont’s larger attributable reserve base drives lower book NAV per ounce despite higher absolute equity.
* **JV structure:** Barrick’s large noncontrolling interests materially reduce NAV attributable to Barrick shareholders.
* **Closure provisions:** Newmont carries significantly larger reclamation/remediation balances, while Barrick provides explicit discount‑rate sensitivity.
* **Deferred taxes & assumptions:** Both companies highlight sensitivity of valuations and reserve economics to metal prices and discount rates.

* **Barrick E&E assets:** Capitalized exploration/development balances are embedded in PP&E; proxies are explicitly labeled as assumptions.
* **Market capitalization:** Shown as approximate, based on price × disclosed share counts.
