## Executive Summary

**Recommendation: Hold, with a positive bias and a preference to buy only on weakness.** The core bullish case on SSR Mining is now much easier to see than it was a year ago: the company has reoriented toward the Americas, cleaned up its balance sheet, added a very cash-generative U.S. asset in CC&V, is monetizing its highest-risk Turkish exposures, and is returning capital through a large buyback rather than stretching into new capex-heavy growth. In the first quarter of 2026, SSR reported revenue of $581.8 million, net income attributable to shareholders from continuing operations of $252.5 million, operating cash flow from continuing operations of $299.6 million, free cash flow from continuing operations of $210.8 million, cash of $634.1 million, and no significant long-term debt outstanding after the convertible note conversion and redemption. The pending sale of the 80% Çöpler stake for $1.5 billion in cash, expected before the end of the third quarter of 2026, is the biggest single de-risking event in the story.

The reason this is **not** a straightforward Buy at current levels is that the stock has already re-rated very sharply, the go-forward growth pipeline is materially slimmer after the sale of the Hod Maden equity stake in exchange for a 4.0% uncapped NSR royalty, and the remaining portfolio still has visible mine-life and execution issues. CC&V and Puna look like the current earnings engines, but Marigold is mature, Seabee had a very weak first quarter, and Puna still needs life-of-mine extension work. In other words, SSRM is becoming safer, but also somewhat less obviously “growthy.” At roughly **10.2x trailing P/E, 6.8x EV/EBITDA, and 8.5x P/operating cash flow** on delayed TSX market data, SSRM still screens cheaper than premium North American peers like Alamos and somewhat cheaper than Pan American, but not so cheap that the remaining risks are irrelevant.

**Confidence level: Moderate.** My base case is that SSRM is a better company than it was pre-Çöpler-sale, but the stock is now closer to fair value than distressed value. The most attractive setup is a pullback-driven entry, or buying only after clear confirmation that the Çöpler transaction closes on time and that management allocates the proceeds intelligently.

## Business Profile and Peer Frame

SSR Mining is in the middle of a strategic reset. As of the first quarter of 2026, management had signed definitive documentation to sell Çöpler in Türkiye for $1.5 billion in cash and had classified Çöpler as a discontinued operation; the company also later agreed to sell its 20% Hod Maden equity interest and operatorship position in exchange for a 4.0% uncapped royalty on 100% of the project. That leaves the investment case centered on four producing assets: **Marigold** in Nevada, **CC&V** in Colorado, **Seabee** in Saskatchewan, and **Puna** in Argentina. That is a simpler, more investable portfolio than the pre-2026 mix, but it also means SSR is giving up part of the upside associated with its former Turkish platform. This is an inference from management’s public actions, but it is a well-supported one.

For peer comparison, I used **Alamos Gold, Pan American Silver, Eldorado Gold, and B2Gold**. The selection is deliberate. Alamos is the closest “premium North American gold” benchmark. Pan American is the most relevant silver-heavy Americas comparator, especially because SSR owns Puna. Eldorado is useful because it is a mid-tier producer with both operating mines and a visible growth pipeline, and because it has historically had some similar jurisdictional complexity. B2Gold is a larger production peer but still a common mid-tier gold comparator, useful mainly as a lower-multiple benchmark that reflects a different risk profile.

## Operations, Reserves, and Growth

SSR’s current investment case rests on four producing mines and a much smaller development/royalty overlay. The company’s year-end 2025 reserve update reported **10.6 million ounces of gold** and **24.2 million ounces of silver** in mineral reserves on an attributable basis, or **11.0 million GEO**, up 38% year over year. However, that reserve headline included assets that are now being sold or restructured, and the company explicitly warned that the historical Çöpler reserve figures should be treated as such because it could not determine whether a restart would ever occur. That means the published consolidated reserve base is **not the clean post-transaction reserve base** that investors should ultimately underwrite.

* Reserves / life: **505 koz** gold reserves; ongoing work at Santoy and Porky for extension
* Assessment: Near-term operational weak spot; still potentially attractive if development work restores grade/volume later in the year

**Puna (Argentina)**

* What matters now: Q1 2026 production of **1.739 Moz silver**; 2026 guidance **6.25–7.00 Moz silver**; Q1 AISC **$23.14/oz**
* Reserves / life: **18.1 Moz** silver reserves; company says it expects an update on life extension opportunities over the next 12 months
* Assessment: High-margin silver exposure, but life-of-mine extension is a critical strategic need

**Çöpler / Hod Maden (Türkiye)**

* What matters now: Çöpler sale signed for **$1.5B cash**, expected by end-Q3 2026; Hod Maden stake sold for **4.0% uncapped NSR**
* Reserves / life: Legacy reserve base becoming less relevant to future SSR economics
* Assessment: Big de-risking moves, but they also reduce direct organic growth torque

The highest-quality operating point in the portfolio right now is **CC&V**. In Q1 2026, management said the mine drove roughly **$187 million of mine-site revenue** and more than **$120 million of mine-site free cash flow**, bringing total mine-site revenue and free cash flow attributable to SSR since acquisition to approximately **$637 million** and **$325 million**, respectively. That is exactly the kind of accretive brownfield acquisition performance that can justify a higher valuation multiple.

**Puna** is the other asset doing a lot of work for the equity story. SSR’s 2025 annual release said Puna realized nearly **$460 million in revenue** and generated mine-site free cash flow of **more than $250 million** in 2025, and the Q1 2026 release described it as one of the highest-margin primary silver mines globally. The problem is not current economics; the problem is **duration**. If management cannot turn Chinchillas laybacks, Melina, and Cortaderas into a credible extension, the market will keep discounting the silver contribution.

**Seabee** is the main operational blemish in the near term. It already suffered a temporary suspension in June 2025 before restarting later that month, and Q1 2026 output was far below the prior-year comparison while AISC ballooned because of winter road timing and development work. Management still says the mine is on track for full-year guidance and is strongest in the fourth quarter, but at this point Seabee needs to **prove** that, not just guide it.

## Risks, ESG, and Management

The single largest non-commodity risk remains the **Çöpler tail**. SSR’s 2025 annual release said the company spent **$21.7 million** on reclamation and remediation in 2025, bringing total spending related to the incident to **$149.3 million**. Before the sale deal, the company was still saying it could not estimate or predict when, or under what conditions, operations at Çöpler would resume. The pending sale is therefore both a de-risking event and an admission that the prior Turkish growth thesis has been fundamentally broken. If the transaction closes as expected, this risk will shrink materially; if it is delayed, repriced, or challenged, it will remain a key drag on valuation.

There are also **portfolio-concentration and reserve-life risks**. Marigold is a reliable but mature open-pit operation. Seabee is high grade but operationally volatile and logistically remote. Puna is strategically valuable because silver exposure is scarce, but its long-term value depends on successful reserve conversion and nearby development. And after the Hod Maden sale, SSR has chosen de-risking over direct development torque. I view that as sensible capital allocation, but it does mean investors are increasingly underwriting a **cash-return story**, not a large internal growth story.

Management quality is better judged as **disciplined but still under safety scrutiny**. Rod Antal has more than 30 years of mining experience and has led Alacer/SSR since the 2020 merger, becoming Executive Chairman in June 2023. Under this team, SSR executed the CC&V acquisition, cleaned up the convertibles, moved decisively to monetize Turkish risk, and launched a **$300 million** normal-course issuer bid. Those are shareholder-friendly actions. At the same time, no management assessment can ignore that the 2024 Çöpler disaster happened on this team’s watch, so I would not score governance as “premium” in the same way I would for a company like Alamos.

## Valuation Scenarios and Practical Price Levels

My preferred way to think about SSRM is to use **two lenses at once**: an illustrative DCF built on normalized post-Çöpler continuing free cash flow, and a relative-multiples check against peers. Both point to upside from distressed valuations, but neither screams “must buy immediately.”

### Illustrative DCF

I would not trust a simplistic DCF that capitalizes current quarterly free cash flow without adjustment. Q1 2026 was unusually strong, the portfolio mix is changing, and published trailing screens may still mix continuing and discontinued economics. So my DCF is deliberately conservative and **explicitly illustrative**.

**Base-case assumptions**

* Starting normalized continuing FCF in 2026: **US$450M**
* FCF path: rises modestly to **US$525M** in 2028, then eases back toward **US$475M** by 2030
* Discount rate: **9%**
* Terminal growth: **0%**
* Risk-adjusted net Çöpler sale proceeds added to equity value: **US$1.28B**
* Separate value assigned to 4% Hod Maden NSR: none explicitly; treated as upside option

That framework yields a **base-case equity value of roughly US$30–31/share**. The main reason the number is not much higher is that I am not giving full credit to peak Q1 economics, and I am not separately capitalizing Hod Maden royalty optionality. The main reason the number is not much lower is the expected cash inflow from the Çöpler sale and the now-cleaner balance sheet. The assumptions are anchored in SSR’s official Q1 cash generation and the announced asset transactions.

### Relative-Multiple View

Relative valuation tells a similar story. SSRM’s current **6.8x EV/EBITDA** is below Alamos (**10.5x**), below Pan American (**8.9x**), and slightly below Eldorado (**7.2x**), while remaining far above B2Gold’s very low **2.8x**. On that basis:

**Discount case**

* If the market keeps SSRM near a “somewhat de-risked but reserve-life-constrained” profile, the stock belongs around the **high-20s US$**

**Base case**

* If Çöpler closes and capital allocation stays disciplined, SSRM can justify the **low-30s US$**

**Bull case**

* If the company closes the sale, extends Puna life, stabilizes Seabee, and uses cash very well, the stock can justify the **mid-30s US$**

Those scenario ranges line up reasonably well with the illustrative DCF, which is exactly what you want from a decision framework.

## Open Questions and Limitations

A few limitations matter to the interpretation of this report. First, some peer operational metrics were easier to confirm than others; in particular, this report is strongest on **SSRM** and stronger on **peer valuation** than on peer mine-by-mine reserve detail. Second, delayed market-data aggregators can present TTM figures that do not line up perfectly with management’s continuing-operations presentation after major asset reclassifications, so SSRM trailing multiples should be treated as **screens**, not a substitute for a full mine-by-mine NAV. Third, I have not hard-coded a TSX-equivalent price target in Canadian dollars because SSRM reports in U.S. dollars and the exact USD/CAD conversion should be refreshed at the time of trading.

**Bottom line:** SSRM is now a significantly cleaner and safer story than it was before the Çöpler and Hod Maden transactions, and that deserves credit. But the stock has already clawed back a lot of that credit, and the remaining portfolio still has enough reserve-life and execution questions that I would **not chase it aggressively here**. My stance is **Hold**, with a willingness to upgrade to **Buy** on either a better entry price or a successful close-and-redeploy outcome on the Turkish asset monetizations.
