## Executive Summary

ASA Gold and Precious Metals Limited (“ASA Gold”; NYSE: ASA) is a Bermuda-domiciled closed-end fund focused on precious-metals and mining equities. A multi-year proxy fight and related litigation has largely been driven by a structural valuation problem typical of closed-end funds: **ASA has traded at a persistent discount to NAV**, most recently around **-12.6%** (Bloomberg data as of 03/30/2026), meaning **closing the discount to 0% would mechanically imply ~+14.4% upside** to the market price if NAV stayed constant.

The contest has featured two core factions:

* **Saba Capital (activist)**
* **Legacy/Incumbent governance defenders** (aligned with the adviser and, later, a shareholder campaign led by ASA’s COO)

Saba’s explicit thesis in its contested proxy materials is that ASA’s board needs “fresh ideas” to address the **persistent discount** and governance/rights plan actions. The legacy/defender side argues Saba is pursuing a **closed-end fund “arbitrage” playbook**, potentially including adviser replacement and/or mandate changes, and that “takeover defense” actions (poison pill/rights plans, committees) were meant to protect long-term shareholders.

**Where the situation stands (as of 04/03/2026):**

* **Saba appears to have consolidated meaningful influence**, including:

  + **~24.64% beneficial ownership** disclosed in ASA’s 2025 proxy statement
  + A board chaired by **Saba partner Paul Kazarian**
* ASA’s board has initiated what looks like an endgame process:

  + A **Special Committee “company review” supported by Cantor Fitzgerald**
  + A **90-day extension of the investment advisory agreement** with Merk Investments LLC (commencing 04/01/2026)

**Return-maximizing voting view (evidence-based, conditional):**  
For shareholders prioritizing **maximizing near- to medium-term value**, the highest-probability value catalyst is **credible discount-to-NAV reduction and/or NAV liquidity** (tender offer(s), structural change/combination, or liquidation/managed wind-down) executed with disciplined tax/cost controls. Because Saba’s stated campaign focus is to force action on the discount and governance barriers, and because courts have criticized/invalidated parts of ASA’s repeated rights-plan regime (inference of entrenchment cited in filings; rescission of a 2024 plan), **a vote that empowers the board faction most likely to execute a time-bound discount-realization plan** is the return-maximizing default.

However, this recommendation becomes **less certain** if Saba’s path to value requires:

* (a) replacing a performing adviser with an inferior one,
* (b) a mandate shift that destroys optionality in a gold-equity bull market, or
* (c) prolonged litigation/service-provider instability that overwhelms any discount-capture benefit.

Those risks are expressly raised by the opposing side.

## Company and Value Drivers Relevant to the Proxy Fight

ASA is a **closed-end precious-metals and mining equity fund**; as a closed-end structure, its shares can trade **above or below NAV** for extended periods. ASA’s long-run history shows substantial NAV and share-price volatility and long stretches of discount trading (premium/discount chart through 12/31/2025).

The key return levers for shareholders are therefore dual:

### Portfolio / NAV Performance Lever

* The adviser’s security selection and portfolio construction drives NAV.
* Shareholders approved an **investment advisory agreement with Merk Investments LLC on 04/12/2019**.
* Bloomberg-linked fund data shows strong trailing total return recently (e.g., **1-year return ~92.81%** as of 03/30/2026), consistent with a powerful precious-metals equities regime.

### Structure / Discount Lever

* Bloomberg data shows **Premium/Discount ~ -12.60%** and average 52-week premium/discount around **-10.82%**.
* Closing a -12.6% discount implies about **+14.4% price uplift** (1/(1–0.126)–1).
* This arithmetic is the economic foundation of the activism.
