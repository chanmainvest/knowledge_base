# Trump Accounts and related ideas

- Publication: dampedspring (dampedspring)
- URL: https://dampedspring101.substack.com/p/trump-accounts-and-related-ideas
- Published: 2026-02-08
- Audience: everyone

Some musings.

I am a huge fan of improving financial literacy in our society. I am a huge fan of charitable people who give money to those with less resources. I am, perhaps more than most, supportive of our elected represetatives forcing the transfer of wealth from those who have, to those who don’t. But perhaps most importantly I am a super optimistic person regarding the future of humanity and our ability to improve our standard of living through hard work and innovation. The last point makes me a long term bull on growth and equity market returns. Given my bias it would seem obvisous that I would be a big fan of the Trump Accounts. I’m not.

A Trump account, as defined to date, is simply an investment account funded by the government and recently some charitable individuals and companies. The account becomes the property of a newly born U.S. citizen and the appreciation and income from the account can provide future compounded wealth for the new born when they reach adulthood. The account can also be used as an opportunity to improve financial literacy as the child grows up and observes the forces that impact the appreciation and includes the “savings” as part of their personal financial plan. As mentioned, improved financial literacy is something I love. If you are reading this there is a fair chance you already know my passion for teaching financial literacy and paying forward my understandings. Trump accounts are a positive outcome for financial literacy for the receipients.

Now before I tell you why I’m not a fan of these accounts let’s start with I really don’t care much about the topic because it is tiny in size. Roughly 3.7MN new citizens are born in the US each year. The goverment plan is to give each baby $1000 of government contributed funds. Thats $3.7BN in total which is 5/100 of a percent of the total expenditures made by the federal government each year and just a touch over 1/100 of a percent of the US GDP. Its simply irrelevant. Even if the private sector wealthy donors and corporations 10X the account its still tiny. So before you get super excited that I don’t much like the Trump Accounts concept relax. I don’t really care and your shouldn’t either. Like many tiny programs this effort is going to result in absolutely no difference to anyone and is simply a political gimmick to bolster the party in charge.

Ill address the specific Trump Account Plan and the add on charitable versions later but first I want to step back and deal with a variety of important topics that directly relate to the Trump Accounts and require some perspective before diving down the specific rabbit hole of the relatively tiny and unimportant Trump Accounts.

The big topics that one needs to consider are:

* Financial Literacy
* Charity
* Government wealth transfers
* What drives future returns and What is a good portfolio
* Aggregate macro investment consequences
* Tradeoffs when weighing policy shifts and charitable donations
* The Grand Daddy of the them all. Social Security Trust Fund investment policy changes
* Incentives

**Financial literacy**

If I could wave a wand and magically and free of cost increase financial literacy across the US population I would absolutely do it. I can’t imagine how this could be controversial. I spend hundreds of hours a year purely and totally altruistically attempting to impove financial literacy in this country and around the world. I am a son, nephew, husband, and father of teachers and have spent my entire career paying forward my knowledge to those interested in learning. I deeply value education. Nonetheless the reality is that the forces of click bait, maintstream media telling the hot story of the day, budgetary issues in our schools, legalazation of gambling, and charlatans of all stripes including our government officials are driving financial literacy in the exact opposite direction. I don’t think I am an old man screaming at the sky (perhaps I am) but I think the forces in place are making us dumber about our financial lives at a worrissome dumber pace at tragic current level. I have no confidence that giving a newborn a one time starter position in SPY will any difference at all against the dumbing trend. That said while it could be a negative the odds are its the slightest of postives but perhaps wildly ineffecient. For instance and this is not a suggestion. Instead of spending $1000 for SPY spend $200 on building and implementing a goverment required unit on financail literacy that every 6th grader had to complete and if completed would provide $800 of SPY in an account.

**Charity**

Purely altruistic charity is fantastic. Willingness to share wealth to help provide for ones fellow man is probably a value that humans have prized for almost all of our history. Charitable donations are highly personal and where the money goes reflects what each of us value. Charity can go to those in need, to our religious insitutions for their great work, to research, to the arts, to education, to our local community, to our neighbors. If given without strings or without financial or government provided incentives charity is pure. Some wealthy people and even some corporations have announced their intention to donate money or assets to fund Trump Accounts. I completly respect the decision of these folks to give in the way they believe aligns with their values best. If the Trump Accounts unlock more charitable contributions than would have existed prior to the Trump Accounts then its an absolute positive. If on the other hand the Trump Accounts don’t change the aggregate charitable contributions then its worth noting what charitable beneficiaries lost out and what the incentive was that created the shift. In other words the charitable announcements are wonderful and thats the big thing but whether the announcements were tainted with incentive is worth considering as well.

**Government wealth transfers**

I have a simple framework for the broad role of government in society. This framework doesnt judge what should be or whether the goverment is any good at its functions. I have views on what the goverment “should do” but have little interest in sharing my views in general and particularly in this article. Furthermore this framework is not supposed to be a full description of the governments role in society, its meant to be close enough to generalized the princple functions of governement. Essentially the Federal Government has two functions.

1. Do things that must be done for society that the private sector can’t or wont do.
2. Transfer wealth

I will not be commenting in the first function at all in this article. However, the second function is critical. The Goverment collects taxes and borrows money partly to fulfill function #1 and the rest of the money gathered is handed back to the private sector. The money handed back to the private sector goes to a different cohort would substantial overlap but nowhere near complete overlaps to those that paid the tases and lent the money by buying US Treasury Debt. The lack of complete overlap means that a wealth transfer has occured. As I’ve mentioned in many Damped Spring Reports eventually the wealth transfer does result in spending which then results in income which then results in savings and wealth transfer shrinks but nonetheless the goverment continues to operate this way year after year and a wealth transfer is the result. The characteristics of the wealth transfer and who are the beneficiaires of short term spending and taxation decisions is obviously up to our elected leaders. However, looking narrowly at the decisions made on taxes and spending does a poor job of truly representing the wealth trasnfers that the goverment actions generate. This is a complex topic. Beyond the progressive tax regime, estate taxes, wealth taxes and verious spending programs you support or dislike bigger forces are at play.

The Goverment transfers wealth by borrowing today and spending. If it were the case that the government intended to tax more and spend less in the future to pay back its borrowings the wealth transfer would be one way. If the government decided to debase its currency to pay back its debt then a whole different impact would be fealt by different cohorts. The point is that inflation is a policy choice and inflation is wealth transfer.

So once again I consider the role of government to manage wealth transfers, both immediate and longer term, to be one of its two functions. The Trump Accounts are a small part of that function but are fully embedded in the complex system we live in. What we know is the government version (not the charity version) of the trump accounts buy stock from one cohort paying for it with bonds, and gifts the stocks to another cohort, while a third cohort (the whole of society) owe more future taxes or will experience more future inflation to pay back the borrowing.

**Aggregate macro investment consequences**

The Trump Accounts have virtually no investment consequences at current scale. Even if the account grows to 10X its proposal based on corporate and individual contributions the total size per year is 40BN. But lets dig in anyway as its important to understand the flows in case the program is massively expanded or if the Grand Daddy of them all the Social Security Trust Fund becomes part of the plan.

*US Government portion*

If the Government wants to hand an equity account to newborns. It must buy equities and issue debt to do pay for it. The private sector will do the exact opposite trade. As the Government is the price taker in the transaction they will pay transaction costs to do execute. The transaction will bid up stock prices to attract sellers and bid up bond yields (hit prices) to attract buyers of bonds. That cost will accrue to the Childrens’s account and to the American People who owe the debt.

*Private sector charity portion*

Ignoring taxes which we will cover later a charitable gift of SPY held by the donor to the Trump Account could be the entire flow. Low cost if that simple. That said it will be important to understand the actual characteristics of the gift. For instance in Michael Dell’s incredibly generous gift he may donate his Dell Shares. In that case the government will have to sell the Dell Shares and buy SPX 500. That trade will have a transaction cost. Its important to understand who will pay that cost. But in the traditional charitable gift fund the donor gets full value for the low basis shares donated and the transactions costs of converting the shares to cash and then using the cash to properly invest back into the equity market are entirely borne by the beneficiary of the gift.

**Tradeoffs when weighing policy shifts and charitable donations**

The decision to create Trump Accounts by the government and the decision by charitable individuals and companies to add on to that effort can not be looked at in a vacuum. Every decision is a tradeoff.

*Government*

The goverment will be executing a wealth transfer funded by debt. Sound familiar? There are hundreds of alternative ways to transfer wealth. Deciding to do this one seems fine but also not likely to be better or worse than any other debt funded spending initiative. As a fiscal conservative in gereral and a massive budget hawk since 2022 I see no reason at all to pursue debt funded spending initatives and instead would strongly prefer the opposite. Reduce the deficit not increase it. The country is growing at above trend rates, inflation is not at target, employment is stable, why in heck should we be throwing on more debt fueled spending. The answer is obvious. The party in charge for the past 40 years is always willing to give out candy to maintain its power.

***Charity***

Its possible that by creating Trump accounts which make it very convenient for the private sector to directly give to a particular cohort required the government that the private sector will increase its charitible giving. The government has the means to distribute the money and the track the accounts. That’s a new thing. If so its a nice thing which results in more charity. However, its also likely that the money that is going to Trump Accounts may mean less for other worth causes. I’m not sure how to weigh this tradeoff but acknowledge it probable will result in more charity in general.

**The Grand Daddy of the them all. Social Security Trust Fund policy changes.**

For decades the fate of Social Security has been debated and one idea has been to have the Fund by stocks. When discussing the Trump Accounts many of the ideas apply to this massive problem.

My thinking on this is to consider the aggregate current holdings of the private sector, the future social security payments “owed” to the private sector and the assets that the social security trust fund owns to offset its future liabilities. What is clear is that the promises made for future social security payments are unfunded. That means the assets held in the the fund can not possibly cover the liabilities. But let’s actually look at the liabilities. Even if we posit that the liabilities will be completely honored in full they can be honored if the fund owned no assets at all. In other words the Social Security Trust Fund is not in a real sense unfunded. It just that the asset it owns is a promise with no line item on the Federal budget. Its existence is not really in doubt by anyone on the planet. Its size will depend on the willingness for those who are in charge of transefering wealth to either pay less than receipients are owed or generate inflation which will hit receipeints and the rest of society. Its a policy choice. In the end of the day it doesnt matter at all what the trust fund owns as an asset. The only asset that matters is the promise to pay.

Anyway let’s ignore that whole part and just assume the current assets inclusive of the unrecorded promise equal the liabilities adjusted for the potential for not honoring what is owed. The question then is whether the government should change its asset holdings. Should it sell its promisies (debt securities) to the private sector and buy equities. Well if it did the private sector savings and retirement accounts away from Social Security would own less equity and more debt. Today the Private sector benefits more than the SSTF from equity outperformance vs debt . After the SSTF does its rebalance the private sector savers will benefit much less from equity outperformance than it used to and the SSTF will benefit much more.

IN AGGREGATE the Private sector savings including NON SSTF and SSTF savings will have identical exposures before and after the rebalance. But importantly the cohorts who own SSTF and Private Sector savings are different. That’s a wealth transfer decision. Further more its a risky one. Perhaps the goal is to transfer wealth from private sector savers to Social Security Beneificiaries and yet over the next 20 years equities underperform bonds. The swap backfires.

Unlike the Trump accounts. The SSTF is enormous. Even a small add of equities funded by selling treasuries would have massive implications on short term assets prices bidding up stocks and yields if the goal was to convert form 100% bonds to 60/40 even over a decade that flow would distort prices for years. Frankly the whole idea accomplishes nothing. Either the benefits are reduced or the aggregate economy will experience dollar debasement and inflation. Switching who owns stocks won’t change that.

**Incentives**

In order to see what sort of behavior is likely to occur one has to look at incentives. The incentives of creating the Trump Accounts by the administration are pretty clear. They can speak to ownership of America. They can play on the heart strings by linking it to newly born American citizens. They can leverage that financial literacy is a value. All along they can under the table stimulate the economy by using debt financed equity purchases and bid up the stock market to keep the wealth effect rolling.

The incentives beyond the well intentioned charitable notion of the private sector are also clear. Please the administration has value always and particularly when the administration is quite transactional. Lighten up on stocks with no transactions costs and with most of the donation financed by favorable capital gains taxes. The incentive is large for those leaning toward charity.

Investors are heavily motivated to get new buyers of their bags and the sell side is happy that another 3.8MN brokerage accounts are created each year. Of course they will be supportive.

**So why do I not like the Trump Accounts**

The real reason I am not a fan of the Trump account is that how it works results in a immediate benefit for existing equity shareholders which must be paid by the Trump Account recipient, and it increases the nations debt in a time when DEBT IS THE PROBLEM. Do I really care much? Nah. It’s tiny and doesnt matter. Am I happy it may have a modest impact on financial literacy. TBH nah it wont move the ball at all and other ways would work better. Am I happy a tiny amount of chariticable giving may be unlocked sure.
